阅读理解

04-2020新高考Ⅰ，D

文化遗产、艺术与跨文化

According to a recent study in the Journal of Consumer Research, both the size and consumption habits of our eating companions can influence our food intake. And contrary to existing research that says you should avoid eating with heavier people who order large portions (份), it's the beanpoles with big appetites you really need to avoid.

To test the effect of social influence on eating habits, the researchers conducted two experiments. In the first, 95 undergraduate women were individually invited into a lab to ostensibly (表面上) participate in a study about movie viewership. Before the film began, each woman was asked to help herself to a snack. An actor hired by the researchers grabbed her food first. In her natural state, the actor weighed 105 pounds. But in half the cases she wore a specially designed fat suit which increased her weight to 180 pounds.

Both the fat and thin versions of the actor took a large amount of food. The participants followed suit, taking more food than they normally would have. However, they took significantly more when the actor was thin.

For the second test, in one case the thin actor took two pieces of candy from the snack bowls. In the other case, she took 30 pieces. The results were similar to the first test：the participants followed suit but took significantly more candy when the thin actor took 30 pieces.

The tests show that the social environment is extremely influential when we're making decisions. If this fellow participant is going to eat more, so will I. Call it the “I'll have what she's having” effect. However, we'll adjust the influence. If an overweight person is having a large portion, I'll hold back a bit because I see the results of his eating habits. But if a thin person eats a lot, I'll follow suit. If he can eat much and keep slim, why can't I?

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What is the recent study mainly about?

  • A.Food safety.
  • B.Movie viewership.
  • C.Consumer demand.
  • D.Eating behavior.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

1. D细节理解题。根据文章第一句“根据《消费者研究》杂志上的一项最新研究，和我们一块用餐的同伴的体型和吃饭习惯都会影响我们的食物的摄入量”可知这项研究是关于我们的饮食行为的。故选D项。

2What does the underlined word “beanpoles” in paragraph 1 refer to?

  • A.Big eaters.
  • B.Overweight persons.
  • C.Picky eaters.
  • D.Tall thin persons.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2. D猜测词义题。画线单词所在的句子表示：现有的研究成果认为你应该避免和那些较重的要大份饭菜的人一块用餐，与此相反的是你需要真正避开那些瘦高个且饭量大的人。画线单词与前面的heavier people相反，故选D项。

3Why did the researchers hire the actor?

  • A.To see how she would affect the participants.
  • B.To test if the participants could recognize her.
  • C.To find out what she would do in the two tests.
  • D.To study why she could keep her weight down.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

3. A推理判断题。作者在第二、三、四段描述了研究人员雇用这位演员和那些参与者做的两个测试，发现这位演员的饮食摄入量都会影响参与者的饮食量。由此推断出雇用她的目的就是测试她是如何影响参与者的。故选A项。

4On what basis do we “adjust the influence” according to the last paragraph?

  • A.How hungry we are.
  • B.How slim we want to be.
  • C.How we perceive others.
  • D.How we feel about the food.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

4. C推理判断题。根据最后一段第二、三句(如果这个同伴吃得多，我也要吃得多。我们称之为“我要有她有的东西”效应)推断出我们要想调整这种影响，就是要调整我们看待别人的方式。故选C项。 长难句 And contrary to existing research that says you should avoid eating with heavier people who order large portions, it’s the beanpoles with big appetites you really need to avoid. 译文 现有的研究表明，你应该避免和那些要大量饭菜的较重的人一起吃饭，和现有的研究相反，你真正需要避免的是那些大胃口的瘦高个子。 分析 这是一个主从复合句。contrary to...作状语，其中that引导定语从句，修饰先行词research；who引导定语从句，修饰先行词people。

By Rebecca Picciotto and Nicholas G. Miller

    Moving Back Home Used to Be a Sign of Failure. Now It Shows Financial Savvy.

    搬回父母家曾被视为失败的标志。如今它却体现了财务智慧。

    savvy
    n. 实际知识、判断力；adj. 聪慧的、懂门道的
  2. 02

    Samantha Stobo was fresh off a breakup and unable to afford her Manhattan two-bedroom alone. So the 29-year-old decided to move in with her mom in Miami until she could get back on her feet. She told herself it would only take a few months. Now 33 years old, Stobo has no plans to move out. "I thought it was going to be temporary," Stobo said. "But it's been three years now, and I love it."

    萨曼莎·斯托博（Samantha Stobo）刚经历了一次分手，无力独自承担她在曼哈顿那套两居室的房租。于是，这位29岁的姑娘决定搬回迈阿密和母亲同住，等她能重新站稳脚跟再搬走。她告诉自己，这顶多只需几个月。斯托博今年33岁了，还没有搬出去的打算。“我原本以为这只是暂时的，”斯托博说，“但转眼已经三年了，我很喜欢这样。”

    temporary
    adj. 短暂的，临时的
  3. 03

    Living at home as a 20-something was once viewed as a failure to launch and even a source of embarrassment in a culture that places a premium on independence. That is no longer the case. Living at home is now often viewed as a sign of financial prudence, and for some, a long-term prospect.

    在崇尚独立自主的文化中，一个二十多岁的人还住在父母家，曾被视为启程失败，甚至是一种耻辱。但如今情况不同了。住在父母家现在常常被视为财务审慎的体现，对有些人来说，更是一种长期打算。

    prudence
    n. 审慎，精明，稳健
  4. 04

    Chronically high living costs are helping reshape the milestones of early adulthood in America. The national median home price hovers above $400,000. Rents are at record highs in cities across the U.S.

    长期居高不下的生活成本正在重塑美国年轻人的人生节点。全美房屋价格中位数徘徊在40万美元以上。全美各地城市的租金也都创下历史新高。

    徘徊在……之上
    chronically
    adv. 长期地，慢性地
    reshape
    v. 重塑
  5. 05

    Jessica Suzio, a 52-year old widow in Michigan, is happy to have her sons at home. "When they were younger, I was daydreaming about the days that I would be an empty-nester," she said. "But I've grown to really appreciate them sticking around."

    密歇根州52岁的寡妇杰西卡·苏齐奥（Jessica Suzio）很高兴儿子们能住在家里。“他们小时候，我曾幻想过自己成为‘空巢老人’的日子，”她说，“但现在我越来越感激他们能一直陪在我身边。”

    empty-nester
    n. 空巢老人；子女成年离家的父母
