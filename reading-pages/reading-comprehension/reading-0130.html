成长、韧性与心理调适

In May 1987 the Golden Gate Bridge had a 50th birthday party. The bridge was closed to motor traffic so people could enjoy a walk across it. Organizers expected perhaps 50,000 people to show up. Instead, as many as 800,000 crowded the roads to the bridge. By the time 250,000 were on the bridge, engineers noticed something terrible: the roadway was flattening under what turned out to be the heaviest load it had ever been asked to carry. Worse, it was beginning to sway(晃动). The authorities closed access to the bridge and tens of thousands of people made their way back to land. A disaster was avoided.

The story is one of scores in To Forgive Design: Understanding Failure, a book that is at once a love letter to engineering and a paean(赞歌) to its breakdowns. Its author, Dr. Henry Petroski, has long been writing about disasters. In this book, he includes the loss of the space shuttles(航天飞机) Challenger and Columbia, and the sinking of the Titanic.

Though he acknowledges that engineering works can fail because the person who thought them up or engineered them simply got things wrong, in this book Dr. Petroski widens his view to consider the larger context in which such failures occur. Sometimes devices fail because a good design is constructed with low quality materials incompetently applied. Or perhaps a design works so well it is adopted elsewhere again and again, with seemingly harmless improvements, until, suddenly, it does not work at all anymore.

Readers will encounter not only stories they have heard before, but some new stories and a moving discussion of the responsibility of the engineer to the public and the ways young engineers can be helped to grasp them.

“Success is success but that is all that it is,” Dr. Petroski writes. It is failure that brings improvement.

1What happened to the Golden Gate Bridge on its 50th birthday?

  • A.It carried more weight than it could.
  • B.It swayed violently in a strong wind.
  • C.Its roadway was damaged by vehicles.
  • D.Its access was blocked by many people.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1.A　细节理解题。根据文章第一段“In May 1987...to sway”可以看出,多达80万人挤满了通往大桥的道路。当25万人在桥上时,工程师们注意到了一个可怕的现象:路面变平了。更糟的是,它开始晃动。因此可以看出,金门大桥50岁生日那天,它承受的重量超过了它的承受能力。

2Which of the following is Dr. Petroski's idea according to paragraph 3?

  • A.No design is well received everywhere.
  • B.Construction is more important than design.
  • C.Not all disasters are caused by engineering design.
  • D.Improvements on engineering works are necessary.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

2.C　推理判断题。根据文章第三段可以推知,博士的想法是并非所有的灾难都是由工程设计引起的。

3What does the last paragraph suggest?

  • A.Failure can lead to progress.
  • B.Success results in overconfidence.
  • C.Failure should be avoided.
  • D.Success comes from joint efforts.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

3.A　细节理解题。根据文章最后一段的 “It is failure that brings improvement.”可知,失败能带来改进。A项与此相呼应,即失败能带来进步。

4What is the text?

  • A.A news report.
  • B.A short story.
  • C.A book review.
  • D.A research article.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

4.C　推理判断题。文章第一段的故事来自《请原谅设计》一书,并且后面三段都在写这本书里的内容,再结合最后一段这本书的升华内容“It is failure that brings improvement.”可知,文章内容都是关于这本书的,所以这篇文章是书评。

合作40年的肯德基改投可口可乐，百事到底输在哪？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    PepsiCo used to embody everything investors prized in a stock: dependable brands, reliable earnings growth and steady returns. That reputation is fading. The stock is down about a third from its 2023 peak and trades at its steepest discount to Coca-Cola in more than 25 years. The problem may be bigger than a few weak brands. Pepsi needs to stop tinkering and consider more fundamental changes, including a spinoff of its bottling operations.

    百事公司曾集投资者对一只股票珍视的一切于一身：可靠的品牌、稳健的盈利增长和持续的回报。这一声誉正在褪色。其股价较 2023 年的高点已下跌约三分之一，相对可口可乐的折价幅度创下 25 年多来的最高纪录。问题可能比几个疲软品牌更严重。百事需要停止修修补补，并考虑更根本性的变革，其中包括剥离其瓶装业务。

    embody
    v. 成为……的化身，集中体现
    spinoff
    n. 分拆，切分
    tinker
    v. 小修小补
    prize
    v. 把……当成压箱底的宝贝来珍视，看重
  2. 02

    Pepsi has tried to revive its North American snacks business with price cuts, new products and more marketing. Volumes edged up last quarter, but the snack unit's operating profit fell 13%, and Pepsi now plans to raise some prices again.

    百事曾试图通过降价、推出新产品和加大营销投入来重振其北美零食业务。上季度销量略有回升，但零食部门的营业利润下滑了 13%，而百事如今又计划上调部分产品价格。

    revive
    v. 使复苏，重振旗鼓
  3. 03

    Drinks are worse. "We're not competing well in soft drinks," Laguarta told analysts this month. Volumes fell 2% in the latest quarter, and The Wall Street Journal reported KFC has struck a deal to replace Pepsi with Coca-Cola at thousands of its U.S. restaurants.

    饮料业务的情况更糟。“我们在软饮料领域竞争不力，” 拉蒙・拉瓜尔塔（Ramon Laguarta 百事首席执行官）本月对分析师表示。最新季度销量下降了 2%，据《华尔街日报》报道，肯德基已达成协议，将在其美国数千家门店用可口可乐取代百事。

  4. 04

    A big part of the problem may simply be sprawl. Coca-Cola has outperformed in part because it handed its bottling back to independent operators and focused headquarters on building brands. Pepsi's management, meanwhile, has to run Frito-Lay, a sprawling U.S. drinks business with its own trucks and warehouses, and a fast-growing international arm.

    问题的很大一部分可能就出在摊子铺得太大。可口可乐之所以表现更优，部分原因在于它把瓶装业务交还给了独立运营商，让总部专注于打造品牌。而百事的管理层既要分心经营菲多利零食帝国，又要管一个自己养卡车，自己建仓库的庞大臃肿的美国本土饮料业务，同时还得兼顾一个快速增长的国际业务部门。

    sprawl
    n. 无计划发展
  5. 05

    None of this would be easy. Refranchising would take years and weigh on earnings. And unlike Coke, which sold its distribution network to large, well-funded bottlers, Pepsi has few independent operators big enough to take them on. Coke itself absorbed years of margin pain before shedding its North American bottling. The payoff, though, was higher margins and a sharper focus.

    这些变革没有一件是容易的。重新特许经营需要多年时间，并会拖累盈利。与可口可乐把分销网络出售给资金雄厚的大型瓶装商不同，百事几乎没有规模足够大的独立运营商能够接手这些业务。可口可乐自身在剥离北美瓶装业务之前，也承受了多年的利润率阵痛。不过，回报是更高的利润率和更专注的业务重心。

    refranchising
    n. 重新特许经营化
    shed
    v. 剥离
  6. 06

    Pepsi's leaders say they are open to every option. Investors are waiting for them to pick one.

    百事的管理层表示，他们对所有选项持开放态度。投资者正等着他们做出选择。
