阅读理解

02-2023新课标Ⅰ，B

AI、脑科学与新技术

When John Todd was a child, he loved to explore the woods around his house, observing how nature solved problems. A dirty stream, for example, often became clear after flowing through plants and along rocks where tiny creatures lived. When he got older, John started to wonder if this process could be used to clean up the messes people were making.

After studying agriculture, medicine, and fisheries in college, John went back to

observing nature and asking questions. Why can certain plants trap harmful bacteria(细菌)？ Which kinds of fish can eat cancer-causing chemicals? With the right combination of animals and plants, he figured, maybe he could clean up waste the way nature did. He decided to build what he would later call an eco-machine.

The task John set for himself was to remove harmful substances from some sludge(污泥). First, he constructed a series of clear fiberglass tanks connected to each other. Then he went around to local ponds and streams and brought back some plants and animals. He placed them in the tanks and waited. Little by little, these different kinds of life got used to one another and formed their own ecosystem. After a few weeks, John added the sludge.

He was amazed at the results. The plants and animals in the ecomachine took the sludge as food and began to eat it! Within weeks, it had all been digested, and all that was left was pure water.

Over the years, John has taken on many big jobs. He developed a greenhouse-like facility that treated sewage(污水) from 1,600 homes in South Burlington. He also designed an eco-machine to clean canal water in Fuzhou, a city in southeast China.

“Ecological design” is the name John gives to what he does. “Life on Earth is kind of a box of spare parts for the inventor,” he says. “You put organisms in new relationships and observe what’s happening. Then you let these new systems develop their own ways to self-repair.”

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What can we learn about John from the first two paragraphs?

  • A.He was fond of traveling.
  • B.He enjoyed being alone.
  • C.He had an inquiring mind.
  • D.He longed to be a doctor.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

1. C推理判断题。根据第一段中的explore、started to wonder和第二段中的studying、went back to observing、asking questions可以推断出，约翰·托德自孩童时代就喜欢探究和质疑。故选C。inquiring意为“爱探索的，好奇的”。

2Why did John put the sludge into the tanks?

  • A.To feed the animals.
  • B.To build an ecosystem.
  • C.To protect the plants.
  • D.To test the eco-machine.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2. D推理判断题。本题问的是John把污泥放进水箱的目的。根据第二段最后一句可知，John决定建造一个生态箱（eco-machine）。第三段主要讲述了John的实验过程（把污泥放进水箱里并观察）。第四段描述实验结果——这个生态箱里的动植物把污泥当成食物吃了。由此可推断出，John把污泥放进水箱的目的是测试他研制的生态箱。故选D。

3What is the author’s purpose in mentioning Fuzhou?

  • A.To review John’s research plans.
  • B.To show an application of John’s idea.
  • C.To compare John’s different jobs.
  • D.To erase doubts about John’s invention.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3. B推理判断题，考查写作目的。根据题干关键词Fuzhou可定位到第五段。根据“John has taken on many big jobs”可知，John已经承担了很多大型工作。紧接着通过他的发明应用于南伯灵顿和福州的例子来说明他的想法在实践中的应用（application）。故选B。

4What is the basis for John’s work?

  • A.Nature can repair itself.
  • B.Organisms need water to survive.
  • C.Life on Earth is diverse.
  • D.Most tiny creatures live in groups.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

4. A细节理解题。根据最后一段中的“Then you let these new systems develop their own ways to self-repair”可知John的研究依据是自然可以进行自我修复。故选A。 长难句 With the right combination of animals and plants, he figured, maybe he could clean up waste the way nature did. 译文 他认为，如果让动植物和谐共生，或许他就能以大自然的方式清理废物。 分析 the way nature did是方式状语从句，意为“以……方式”，相当于in the way that nature did。

从会议到约会，录音应用正在偷走我们的“私密谈话”

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    This Conversation Is Being Recorded. They All Are.

    这段对话正在被录音。所有对话都一样。

  2. 02

    To record or not to record? That is no longer a question for people in tech. The answer is a resounding yes.

    录音，还是不录音？对科技圈的人来说，这已不再是个问题。答案是响亮的“当然”。

    resounding
    响亮、明确的。a resounding yes 夸张地说，科技圈早已把录音当成理所当然的做法。
  3. 03

    A Zoom call isn’t complete without an artificial-intelligence note taker. Phones are out at meetings, capturing every word. During impromptu conversations with coworkers, someone might turn on the Granola transcription app, which can turn the interactions into one-page summaries or a list of action items. Even at bars and on dates, people are using AI-infused listening apps to analyze conversations later on.

    如今，没有AI记事工具参与的Zoom在线会议似乎就不能算完整：会上，人们纷纷拿出手机，录下每一句话。即便是一时兴起与同事聊上几句，也会有人打开诸如 Granola 的转录应用 ，将谈话整理成一页纸的摘要或列出一份待办事项清单。甚至在酒吧、约会时，人们也开始使用搭载AI的监听应用，以便事后分析这段谈话。

    impromptu
    没有事先计划的。impromptu conversations 指临时聊起来的对话。
    transcription
    把录音转成文字。transcription app 指提供这种功能的软件。
  4. 04

    Gone are the days of any interaction being private by default. Asking for permission is out, even though in some situations, that could mean violating the law. It isn’t creepy if it’s in the name of productivity, say the people doing the recording. “It happens on a daily basis,” says Eric Bahn, a venture capitalist in San Francisco who started noticing it about a year ago.

    一切互动被默认属于私域的时代已经一去不复返。凡事须征得对方许可的操作已然过时——尽管在某些情况下，这可能意味着违法。录音的人会认为，只要是为了提高效率，这么做就没什么不妥。“这种情况每天都在发生。”旧金山风险投资人埃里克·巴恩（Eric Bahn）说。他大约在一年前开始注意到这一现象。

    productivity
    完成工作的效率或产出。文中有人拿提高效率当录音的理由。
    violate
    违反。violating the law 即触犯法律；原文用 could 限定，指某些情况下可能违法。
  5. 05

    Bahn used to ask if his conversations were being recorded. Nowadays, he just assumes they are, and chooses his words more carefully. “I just see this as normal protocol,” he says.

    过去，巴恩还常常会问对方是否在录音，如今干脆默认了这一事实，并因此更谨慎地措辞。“我已经把它看成一种常规流程。”他说。

    protocol
    约定的做法；通常的流程。这里指把谈话可能被录下当作日常。
  6. 06

    AI note takers started appearing in meetings several years ago but there’s usually an indication of their presence. Apps that record or transcribe without any type of alert have taken off more recently.

    AI记事工具几年前就开始出现在会议中，不过通常会明确提示人们它们的存在。而那些无须提示即开始录音或转录的应用是最近才流行起来的。

  7. 07

    Jeremy Levine, a venture capitalist who lives in New York, finds the practice of surreptitious recording hugely offensive.“That should be socially unacceptable behavior,” Levine says. “I believe it will stifle conversation.”

    纽约风险投资人杰里米·莱文（Jeremy Levine）认为，这种偷偷录音的做法让人极其反感。“这本就不该是能被社会接受的行为，”莱文说，“我认为它会扼杀对话。”

    stifle
    抑制；使话说不出来。stifle conversation 指人一怕被录音，就不敢畅所欲言。
    surreptitious
    偷偷进行的。这里特指没有告知对方的录音。
