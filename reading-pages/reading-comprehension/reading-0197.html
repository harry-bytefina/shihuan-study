阅读理解

02-2017课标全国Ⅰ，B

学习、阅读与教育方式

I work with Volunteers for Wildlife, a rescue and education organization at Bailey Arboretum in Locust Valley. Trying to help injured, displaced or sick creatures can be heartbreaking;survival is never certain. However, when it works, it is simply beautiful.

I got a rescue call from a woman in Muttontown. She had found a young owl(猫头鹰)on the ground. When Iarrived, I saw a 2- to 3-week-old owl. It had already been placed in a carrier for safety.

I examined the chick(雏鸟)and it seemed fine. If I could locate the nest, I might have been able to put it back, but no luck. My next work was to construct a nest and anchor it in a tree.

The homeowner was very helpful. A wire basket was found. I put some pine branches into the basket to make this nest safe and comfortable. I placed the chick in the nest, and it quickly calmed down.

Now all that was needed were the parents, but they were absent. I gave the homeowner a recording of the hunger screams of owl chicks. These advertise the presence of chicks to adults;they might also encourage our chick to start calling as well. I gave the owner as much information as possible and headed home to see what news the night might bring.

A nervous night to be sure, but sometimes the spirits of nature smile on us all!The homeowner called to say that the parents had responded to the recordings. I drove over and saw the chick in the nest looking healthy and active. And it was accompanied in the nest by the greatest sight of all—LUNCH!The parents had done their duty and would probably continue to do so.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What is unavoidable in the author’s rescue work according to paragraph 1?

  • A.Efforts made in vain.
  • B.Getting injured in his work.
  • C.Feeling uncertain about his future.
  • D.Creatures forced out of their homes.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1.A　推理判断题。根据第一段中的 “can be heartbreaking;survival is never certain”可知,拯救野生生物的工作有时令人心碎,因为动物不一定能活下来,也就是说可能会徒劳无功,由此可知答案为A项。B:在工作中受伤;C:对未来不确定;D:生物会被迫离开栖息地。以上三项在文中均未被提及,故全部排除。

2Why was the author called to Muttontown?

  • A.To rescue a woman.
  • B.To take care of a woman.
  • C.To look at a baby owl.
  • D.To cure a young owl.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

2.C　细节理解题。根据第二段中的 “She had found a young owl on the ground.”和第三段中的“I examined the chick and it seemed fine.”可知,作者接到一位居住在Muttontown的妇女的电话,于是赶过去看看这只雏鸟的情况。选项C中的“look at(检查)”与文中的“examined”相对应,故答案为C项。

3What made the chick calm down?

  • A.A new nest.
  • B.Some food.
  • C.A recording.
  • D.Its parents.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

3.A　细节理解题。题干中的“calm down”是关键词。文中第四段指出,作者把雏鸟放进特意为它做的鸟巢后,它很快安静了下来,由此可知新的鸟巢使雏鸟平静了下来,故答案为A项。其他选项的信息是故事发展到后期出现的,故排除。

4How would the author feel about the outcome of the event?

  • A.It’s unexpected.
  • B.It’s beautiful.
  • C.It’s humorous.
  • D.It’s discouraging.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

4.B　推理判断题。短文的最后三句描写作者看到巢中的小猫头鹰十分健康、活跃,最令人喜悦的是它的父母给它觅来了午餐。由此可以推断小猫头鹰今后也将得到其父母的悉心照料。作者对事件结局的感受通过“the greatest sight”表现出来,与文章第一段中的“beautiful”相呼应。因此答案应为B项。

用网暴的方式对抗网暴？TikTok掀起“善意围攻”

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    'It feels like a calling': The internet vigilantes who weaponise kindness

    “这就像一种召唤”：把善意变作武器的网络义警

    weaponise
    v. 把……变成武器
    vigilante
    n. 义警，义务警员
  2. 02

    Every day, Laura Tigeleiro of Staten Island, New York, would open TikTok to a sea of hatred. "Everyone was making fun of my eye," she says.

    每天，纽约史泰登岛的劳拉·蒂格雷罗（Laura Tigeleiro）一打开TikTok，看到的都是满屏的恶意。“所有人都在取笑我的眼睛，”她说。

  3. 03

    Tigeleiro has multiple sclerosis, an autoimmune disease that's made one of her piercing blue eyes droop sideways. "I just wanted to share healthy recipes, and maybe make some friends," she says. "But they said terrible things. It really hurt." Then, last March, her phone exploded, and everything changed.

    蒂格雷罗患有多发性硬化症，这是一种自身免疫性疾病，导致其一只明亮的蓝眼睛向侧边下垂。“我只是想分享一些健康食谱，也许再交几个朋友，”她说。“可他们说了很难听的话，真的很伤人。”去年三月，她的手机突然被消息刷爆——一切，从此改变。

    droop
    v. 下垂，耷拉
  4. 04

    One of her videos had gone viral, with over 15,000 friendly comments. "I didn't know what was going on. I was shocked," says Tigeleiro. Days later, she figured it out: she was under attack by the Kindness Mob.

    她的一条视频走红了，底下有15000多条友善的评论。“我不知道发生了什么，完全懵了，”蒂格雷罗说。几天后，她才明白过来：自己遭到了“善意暴民”的“围攻”。

  5. 05

    The Kindness Mob is a TikTok channel run by Tyler Brickley. "It's a form of trolling," he says. Every day, Brickley points his half-a-million followers at someone who's being bullied or overlooked, and has them flood the target with kind comments. "We're hijacking the machinery of online harassment," like coordination and anonymity, and using them for benevolence. "And I gotta say, it works."

    “善意暴民”是泰勒·布里克利（Tyler Brickley）运营的一个TikTok 账号。“这也是一种‘钓鱼’，”他说。每天，布里克利把他的50万粉丝引向一个正在被霸凌或被忽视的人，让大家用善意的评论淹没对方。“我们其实是劫持了网络暴力的那套机制，”比如协调行动、匿名，并用来行善。他忍不住补了一句：“我得说，这招真管用。”

    benevolence
    n. 仁慈，善行
    anonymity
    n. 匿名
    hijack
    v. 劫持
  6. 06

    There's something more interesting here than people being nice. When the mob descends, the bullying often stops, sometimes forever. It's an example of what scholars call "social contagion theory", where positive or negative behaviour spreads from person to person like a virus.

    而这里发生的事情，可比“大家都很友善”有趣得多：这群人一“杀到”，霸凌往往就此停止——有时，是永久停止。这正是学者提出的“社会传染理论”的一个例证：无论是好的行为，还是坏的行为，都会像病毒一样，在人群间传播。

    descend
    v. 突袭
    contagion
    n. （疾病在人与人之间的）接触传染
  7. 07

    After Brickley turned his army on Tigeleiro, she gained so many new followers that TikTok started paying her. She says she made enough money to buy her family a much-needed new car.

    布里克利把他的“大军”引向蒂格雷罗之后，她的粉丝数飞涨，TikTok也开始向她支付报酬。她说，自己挣的钱，已足够给家里买一辆急需的新车了。

  8. 08

    Brickley, meanwhile, goes out of his way to keep the work hidden from the Kindness Mob's targets. "We're not the point of the story. The point is to remove the distraction, to get all these hateful comments out of the way so the person's content can shine through."

    与此同时，布里克利则想方设法不让被“围攻”的对象察觉到他的存在。“我们不是故事的重点，重点是消除干扰，把恶意评论冲走，好让这个人自己的内容再次闪耀。”

  9. 09

    "It feels like a calling," Brickley says. "The Kindness Mob is just one step. But kindness, for everyone, always, feels like a good place to start.

    “这就像一种召唤，”布里克利说。“‘善意暴民’只是一小步。但对每一个人来说，在任何时候，善意都是一个很好的起点。”
