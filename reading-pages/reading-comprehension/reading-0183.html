睡眠、运动与身心健康

Monkeys seem to have a way with numbers.

A team of researchers trained three Rhesus monkeys to associate 26 clearly different symbols consisting of numbers and selective letters with 0~25 drops of water or juice as a reward. The researchers then tested how the monkeys combined—or added—the symbols to get the reward.

Here's how Harvard Medical School scientist Margaret Livingstone, who led the team, described the experiment: In their cages the monkeys were provided with touch screens. On one part of the screen, a symbol would appear, and on the other side two symbols inside a circle were shown. For example, the number 7 would flash on one side of the screen and the other end would have 9 and 8. If the monkeys touched the left side of the screen they would be rewarded with seven drops of water or juice; if they went for the circle, they would be rewarded with the sum of the numbers—17 in this example.

After running hundreds of tests, the researchers noted that the monkeys would go for the higher values more than half the time, indicating that they were performing a calculation, not just memorizing the value of each combination.

When the team examined the results of the experiment more closely, they noticed that the monkeys tended to underestimate(低估) a sum compared with a single symbol when the two were close in value—sometimes choosing, for example, a 13 over the sum of 8 and 6. The underestimation was systematic: When adding two numbers, the monkeys always paid attention to the larger of the two, and then added only a fraction(小部分) of the smaller number to it.

“This indicates that there is a certain way quantity is represented in their brains,” Dr. Livingstone says.“But in this experiment what they're doing is paying more attention to the big number than the little one.”

越玩泥巴越健康？芬兰科学家发现“最接地气”的免疫奥秘

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Why getting your hands dirty is good for you

    为什么亲近泥土对你有好处

  2. 02

    The idea of more bacteria on our skin might not sound appealing. But when there's a rich mix of microbes living alongside each other, they keep each other in check and defend us against more harmful, pathogenic bacteria. A growing body of research shows that contact with natural materials, like soil and plants, is an easy way to boost our skin's microbial diversity, and that this not only benefits our skin, but also our immune system.

    让皮肤上沾染更多细菌听起来可能并不令人愉悦。但当各种微生物以丰富的组合形式共存时，它们会相互制衡，并帮助我们抵御更具危害性的致病菌。越来越多的研究表明，接触土壤和植物等天然物质是提升皮肤微生物多样性的简便方法，这不仅有益于我们的皮肤，也有益于我们的免疫系统。

    microbes
    微生物；文章讨论自然材料如何增加人皮肤上的微生物种类。
    pathogenic
    能致病的；pathogenic bacteria 特指会引发疾病的细菌，范围比 microbes 窄。
    diversity
    多样性；microbial diversity 关注有多少种不同微生物，不等于微生物总数。
  3. 03

    Mucky hands are the norm for children at Aurinkolinna daycare centre in central Finland. They spend at least a couple of hours every day playing outside in a wooded playground, racing between bushes and trees, splashing in puddles and grabbing fistfuls of dirt and woodchips. By the end of each day they are covered head to toe in mud. Scientists are now studying how this could be good for their health.

    在芬兰中部的奥林科林纳日托中心，满手泥巴是孩子们的家常便饭。他们每天至少有几个小时在树木繁茂的户外游乐场玩耍，在灌木丛和树木间穿梭奔跑，在水坑里踩水，抓起一把把泥土和木屑。每天放学时，他们从头到脚都沾满了泥巴。科学家们目前正在研究这对他们的健康有何益处。

    norm
    常态；are the norm 表示幼儿园里这种情形司空见惯。
    mucky
    沾满泥污的；mucky hands 写孩子们在户外玩耍后双手脏兮兮的样子。
    daycare
    白天照护；daycare centre 在本文指学龄前孩子白天接受照护的机构。
    fistfuls
    满满的一把把；fistfuls of dirt and woodchips 写孩子用手抓起大量泥土和木片。
  4. 04

    Aurinkolinna is one of 43 daycare centres in Finland taking part in the Vahvistu research project, which builds on a recent two-year study led by environmental scientist Marja Roslund.

    奥林科林纳是芬兰参与“Vahvistu”研究项目的43家日托中心之一，该项目基于环境科学家玛尔雅·罗斯伦德（Marja Roslund）最近主导的一项为期两年的研究。

  5. 05

    This diversity is important for the health of our skin. Increasing the diversity of bacteria on the skin can prevent disease-causing bacteria from becoming dominant, keeping the microbial community in balance, Roslund says.

    这种多样性对我们的皮肤健康至关重要。罗斯伦德表示，增加皮肤上细菌的多样性可以防止致病菌占据主导地位，从而保持微生物群落的平衡。

    dominant
    占主导的；prevent bacteria from becoming dominant 指不让某类菌在群落中过度占优。
  6. 06

    Roslund's research found fewer potential pathogens on children's skin when they had played on the forest floor, likely because the number and diversity of other microbes crowds out these "bad" bacteria. "Because there is so much of everything else, there is not so much space for the pathogens," she says.

    罗斯伦德的研究发现，当孩子们在森林地面上玩耍后，皮肤上的潜在病原体数量减少了，这很可能是因为数量众多、多样性丰富的其他微生物排挤了这些“有害”细菌。“因为其他微生物数量庞大，病原体就没有多少生存空间了，”她说。
