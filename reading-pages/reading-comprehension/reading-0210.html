03-2016课标全国Ⅰ，C

睡眠、运动与身心健康

I am Peter Hodes,a volunteer stem cell courier. Since March 2012, I've done 89 trips—of those, 51 have been abroad. I have 42 hours to carry stem cells(干细胞) in my little box because I've got two ice packs and that's how long they last. In all, from the time the stem cells are harvested from a donor(捐献者)to the time they can be implanted in the patient, we've got 72 hours at most. So I am always conscious of time.

I had one trip last year where I was caught by a hurricane in America. I picked up the stem cells in Providence, Rhode Island, and was meant to fly to Washington then back to London. But when I arrived at the check-in desk at Providence, the lady on the desk said: “Well, I'm really sorry, I've got some bad news for you—there are no flights from Washington.” So I took my box and put it on the desk and I said:“In this box are some stem cells that are urgently needed for a patient—please, please, you've got to get me back to the United Kingdom.” She just dropped everything. She arranged for a flight on a small plane to be held for me, re-routed(改道)me through Newark and got me back to the UK even earlier than originally scheduled.

For this courier job,you're consciously aware that in that box you've got something that is potentially going to save somebody's life.

1Which of the following can replace the underlined word“courier”in Paragraph 1?

  • A.provider
  • B.delivery man
  • C.collector
  • D.medical doctor
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1.B　猜测词义题。根据文章第一段可知从在捐献者身上取到干细胞到移植到病人身上最多有72小时的时间,由此可判断作者是干细胞的运送者,故选B项。A、C、D三项和作者的递送工作无任何关系,因此可排除。

2Why does Peter have to complete his trip within 42 hours?

  • A.He cannot stay away from his job too long.
  • B.The donor can only wait for that long.
  • C.The operation needs that much time.
  • D.The ice won't last any longer.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2.D　细节理解题。根据文章第一段第三句可知作者有两个冰袋,而它们能持续的时间为42小时,故选D项。A项意为“他不能离开工作太长时间”。B项意为“捐献者只能等那么长时间”。C项意为“手术需要很长时间”。

3Which flight did the woman put Peter on first?

  • A.To London.
  • B.To Newark.
  • C.To Providence.
  • D.To Washington.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

追光千年，人类与极光的不解之缘

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    If You Were Inspired by the Northern Lights, You're Not Alone

    若你为北极光所震撼，那你不是一个人

  2. 02

    The aurora borealis has intrigued humans for millenniums. Scholars have identified portrayals in prehistoric cave paintings, in the Bible and other ancient historical texts, and in a number of myths and legends.

    北极光已令人类为之着迷数千年。学者们发现在史前洞穴壁画、《圣经》及其他古代史籍中，均有对北极光的描绘，诸多神话传说里也能寻见它的踪迹。

    intrigue
    v. When something "intrigues someone", it makes them curious.
    portrayal
    n. A "portrayal" is the way something or someone is shown in a book, film, picture, or a story.
  3. 03

    The Inuit believed that the lights were torches ignited by the spirits of the dead to guide new spirits to a higher realm.

    因纽特人相信，极光是逝者灵魂点燃的火把，用以指引新的灵魂前往更高的境地。

    realm
    n. A "realm" is a world or area connected to a certain topic or idea.
  4. 04

    Some cultures, including an Indigenous Siberian tribe, viewed the phenomenon as a sign of imminent childbirth. In ancient China, a written record from 2,600 B.C. said that an emperor's mother had become pregnant after seeing "a big lightening circulating around" a star in the northern sky, "with the light shining all over the field."

    包括西伯利亚一个原住民部落在内的部分文化族群，将这一自然现象视作新生命即将降生的征兆。在古代中国，一份公元前2600年的文字记载写道，一位帝王的母亲见北方天际有 “一道强光绕星旋转”，“光芒遍照原野”，此后便身怀六甲。

    imminent
    n. If something is "imminent," it is going to happen very soon.
    indigenous
    adj. "Indigenous" describes people, animals, or plants that have lived in a certain place for a long time and started there.
  5. 05

    The aurora borealis got its scientific name from Galileo in the early 17th century, at a time when Renaissance scholars were studying the phenomenon intensely. He named it after Aurora, the Roman goddess of dawn.

    17世纪初，伽利略为北极光赋予了这个科学名称，彼时文艺复兴时期的学者正对这一现象展开深入研究。他以罗马的黎明女神奥罗拉（Aurora）为其命名。

  6. 06

    The astronomer Edmond Halley, the namesake for Halley's comet, linked the northern lights to the Earth's magnetic field. Other scientists would later confirm Halley's hypothesis. The northern lights happen when charged particles from the sun, carried by the solar wind, collide with gases in Earth's upper atmosphere, guided toward the planet's poles by its magnetic field.

    以哈雷彗星闻名的天文学家爱德蒙・哈雷（Edmond Halley），率先将北极光与地球磁场关联起来，这一假说后来也得到了其他科学家的证实。北极光的形成，源于太阳释放的带电粒子随太阳风运动，在地球磁场的引导下涌向两极，与地球高层大气中的气体发生碰撞，便产生了这一奇观。

    namesake
    n. A "namesake" is a person or thing that has the same name as someone else, often because it was named after them.
    collide with
    To "collide with" means to hit something strongly, to crash into something, or for two things to come together and cause a problem.
  7. 07

    The lights have inspired plenty of artists, too.

    北极光也为无数艺术家带来了创作灵感。

  8. 08

    "Aurora Borealis," an 1865 painting by the American landscape painter Frederic Edwin Church, shows a ship trapped in Arctic ice under turquoise and red streaks in the night sky.

    美国风景画家弗雷德里克·埃德温·丘奇在 1865 年创作的《北极光》中，画了一艘被困在北极冰层里的船。夜空中划过青绿色和红色的光带。

    turquoise
    n. "Turquoise" is that blue-green color, like clear seawater.
  9. 09

    In recent decades, the northern lights have animated songs by the rock bands Death Cab for Cutie and Renaissance, among others.

    近几十年来，北极光也为 Death Cab for Cutie、Renaissance 等摇滚乐队的歌曲带来了灵感。
