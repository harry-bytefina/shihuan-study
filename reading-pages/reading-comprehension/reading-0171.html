12-2020全国Ⅲ，D

文化遗产、艺术与跨文化

We are the products of evolution, and not just evolution that occurred billions of years ago. As scientists look deeper into our genes(基因), they are finding examples of human evolution in just the past few thousand years. People in Ethiopian highlands have adapted to living at high altitudes. Cattle⁃raising people in East Africa and northern Europe have gained a mutation(突变)that helps them digest milk as adults.

On Thursday in an article published in Cell, a team of researchers reported a new kind of adaptation—not to air or to food, but to the ocean. A group of sea⁃dwelling people in Southeast Asia have evolved into better divers. The Bajau, as these people are known, number in the hundreds of thousands in Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines. They have traditionally lived on houseboats;in recent times, they've also built houses on stilts(支柱)in coastal waters.“They are simply a stranger to the land,”said Rodney C. Jubilado, a University of Hawaii researcher who studies the Bajau.

Dr. Jubilado first met the Bajau while growing up on Samal Island in the Philippines. They made a living as divers, spearfishing or harvesting shellfish.“We were so amazed that they could stay underwater much longer than us local islanders,”Dr. Jubilado said.“I could see them actually walking under the sea.”

In 2015, Melissa Ilardo, then a graduate student in genetics at the University of Copenhagen, heard about the Bajau. She wondered if centuries of diving could have led to the evolution of physical characteristics that made the task easier for them.“It seemed like the perfect chance for natural selection to act on a population,”said Dr. Ilardo. She also said there were likely a number of other genes that help the Bajau dive.

1What does the author want to tell us by the examples in paragraph 1?

  • A.Environmental adaptation of cattle raisers.
  • B.New knowledge of human evolution.
  • C.Recent findings of human origin.

答案：D

2.D　细节理解题。根据第二段中的They have traditionally lived on houseboats...in coastal waters.可知,巴瑶人把房子建在沿海水域,故选择D(邻近海岸)。

  • A.They could walk on stilts all day.

答案：C

4What can be a suitable title for the text?

  • A.Bodies Remodeled for a Life at Sea
  • B.Highlanders' Survival Skills
  • C.Basic Methods of Genetic Research
  • D.The World's Best Divers

4.A　主旨要义题。本文主要讲述了巴瑶人因为靠海为生而导致基因变化,从而可以长时间潜水,故A选项(为适应海上生活而改造的身体)正确。

英语正在“通货膨胀”：Very贬值后，下一个是谁？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    How We Speak Has Gotten Extravagant. Literally.

    我们说话的方式，变得越来越浮夸了。简直了。

    extravagant
    adj. 奢侈的，挥霍的
    literally
    adv. 字面上地、确确实实地
  2. 02

    What was once a single exclamation point now has to be (at least) two. Instead of just saying, "It was insane," someone will say, "It was actually insane," or, to the dismay of many, "It was literally insane."

    曾经一个感叹号就足以表达情绪，如今至少要用上两个。人们不再只说“这太离谱了”，有的人会说“这真的太离谱了”，或者，更让人看不下去的是，还有人会说，“这简直太离谱啦”。

  3. 03

    Why can't we just tamp it down and relax? The reason is that human conversation is not only about making statements, but about making statements worth your interlocutor's attention.

    我们为什么不能收敛一点、放松一点？原因在于，人类的交谈不只是为了陈述，更是为了做出值得你对话者注意的陈述。

  4. 04

    This means that language is always drawing new things into the churn to keep us sounding urgent, committed. The linguist Martin Haspelmath calls this "extravagance," where we operate according to the maxim "Talk in such a way that you will be noticed."

    这意味着，语言总在把新东西卷进这台搅拌机，好让我们听起来热切、恳切。语言学家马丁· 哈斯佩尔马特把这种现象称为“浮夸”——我们的说话方式遵循着这样一条准则：“以会被注意到的方式说话。”

    extravagance
    n. 浮夸
  5. 05

    This extravagance is behind much of what can sound like jittery filler. "Yeah, no" is a great example. The "no" does not contradict the "yeah" but complements it. "Yeah, no" is a stronger version of "yeah."

    许多听起来像是紧张兮兮的口头禅，背后都是这种浮夸。“Yeah, no”（是，不）就是个绝佳的例子。这个“不”并不否定那个“是”，而是在为它加码。“Yeah, no”是“yeah”的加强版。

    complement
    v. 相辅相成，衬托
    jittery
    adj. 紧张兮兮的
  6. 06

    In this vein, English is always and forever seeking new ways of saying "truly" in an emphatic, collar-clutching manner. In the 1300s "very" was the one to beat, but over time it lost its semantic punch.

    循着这条脉络，英语永远都在寻找新的方式，以一种激烈的、揪住人衣领般的姿态来说“真的”。14世纪，“very”是无人能敌的那一个，但随着时间流逝，它的语义力道也消磨殆尽。

  7. 07

    Or, yes, "literally insane," or "I was literally dying of thirst so we stopped at a 7-Eleven." Because in this usage of "literally," we do not actually mean that we were about to perish from clinical dehydration. But words often have contradictory meanings with no one batting an eye. A chair is stuck fast to the floor, but a rabbit runs fast. The figurative use of "literally" is never confusing. It's just one more way of having a "truly" that pops.

    或者，这么说没错（literally insane）“简直离谱”，又或者（I was literally dying of thirst）“我简直要渴死了，所以我们在一家711停了一下”，这么说也没错。因为在“literally”的这种用法里，我们并不真的在说：我们快要因为临床意义上的脱水而丧命了。而词语还常常自带相互矛盾的含义，却没有谁为此眨一下眼。椅子纹丝不动（fast）地钉在地上，兔子跑得飞快（fast）。“Literally”的比喻用法从来不会造成误解。它不过是又一个让“（truly）真的”这个词砰然作响的方式罢了。

    perish
    v. 死亡，消亡
  8. 08

    Not long ago I heard someone praising my tween daughter's new hair color as "tuff." OK, that one is weird to me — but in the end, it's a more extravagant way of saying "cool." "Cool," after all, is a tad elderly now to get the job done.

    前不久，我听到有人夸我十来岁女儿的新发色“tuff”。好吧，这个用法对我来说是有点怪——但说到底，它不过是“cool”的一种更浮夸的说法。毕竟，“cool”如今上了年纪，已经有点干不动这份活了。

    elderly
    adj. 上了年纪的
    tween
    n. 十来岁的孩子
