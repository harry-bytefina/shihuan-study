动物行为与动物认知

The question, “What do elephants and fish have in common?” may sound strange but researchers have found that they both have a feature not observed in any other creature.

A team at Georgia Institute of Technology has discovered that elephants use a special form of air suction(吸力) in their trunks to pick up food and handle delicate objects. Animals using suction to handle objects are typically found in water. Many fish perform suction feeding that requires specialised body parts to quickly generate air pressure that can draw nearby food into their mouths.

The researchers have found that an elephant could do the same thanks to its huge nostrils(鼻孔) and lung capacity. The radius (半径)of an elephant's nostrils can increase by up to 30%,and the volume of air that it can push through its trunk increases by 64%. This allows it to take in air at 30 times the speed of a human sneeze，enabling it to drink 3L of water a second.

In an experiment at Atlanta Zoo, the researchers looked at how an elephant would pick up a small crispy （酥脆的) pancake，which was only 0.5mm thick. The force required to break the pancake was less than 1% of the trunk's weight (about 100kg). The elephant was found to either use suction at a fixed distance from the pancake to “raise” the object in the air and hold it, or gently press the tip of its trunk against the pancake and apply suction to pick it up. At no time during the experiment did the elephant break the pancake.

The researchers believe that the findings could help technology companies develop better machines that use air to do their jobs，as suction has long been used in robotics to grab objects. The fascinating part of the study for robotics is that elephants can use air suction both on ground and in water. The researchers are so impressed that they suggest the elephant's suction technique could help improve robotics technology.

Nature never ceases to surprise. Indeed, it is thought that over four-fifths of living species on earth remain undiscovered. That is a reminder of how little we know of our planet,and that we are not the only creatures with hidden complexities.

吸猫吸狗不是人类专有，猴子长臂猿也爱养宠物？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Humans Love Having Pets. Seems Like Some Other Primates Do, Too.

    人类爱养宠物，其他灵长类动物似乎也一样

  2. 02

    A study published recently suggests that we are not the only ones to take care of other species—and that the roots of wanting a pet companion may stretch deep into our primate past.

    最近发表的一项研究表明，我们并非唯一会照顾其他物种的生物——而想要一个宠物伴侣的愿望，其根源可能深植于我们灵长类祖先的遥远历史中。

  3. 03

    Pets have long posed an evolutionary enigma for researchers. We have to feed them, house them, cut their toenails, clean their teeth and scratch behind their ears. A relationship like that could be considered a poor return on investment. Yet many cultures across the world have pets and cherish them dearly.

    对于研究人员来说，宠物一直以来都是一个进化上的谜题。我们必须给它们喂食、提供住所、剪指甲、清洁牙齿，还要挠挠它的耳后。这样的关系从投资回报率来看似乎并不划算。然而，世界上许多文化都有饲养宠物的传统，并且深深珍视它们。

    cherish
    珍爱、珍视；很多文化里人们把宠物看得很重要。
    enigma
    难以解释的谜；evolutionary enigma 指养宠物耗费精力却仍普遍存在这一难题。
  4. 04

    "There are theories that pet-keeping began either because animals were useful for tasks like hunting and protection or because humans have a natural tendency to care for and bond with other living beings," said Zoë Goldsborough, a behavioral ecologist.

    “有一种理论认为，饲养宠物的开始要么是因为动物在狩猎和护卫等任务中有用，要么是因为人类天生就有照顾其他生物并与之建立亲密关系的倾向，”行为生态学家佐伊·戈尔兹伯勒（Zoë Goldsborough）说道。

    句子结构

    "There are theories that pet-keeping began / either because animals were useful for tasks like hunting and protection / or because humans have a natural tendency to care for and bond with other living beings," / said Zoë Goldsborough, a behavioral ecologist.

    theories 后的 that 从句陈述假说内容，that 在里面不作主语或宾语。either … or … 并列的是两个 because 从句，分别从动物的实用价值和人类照顾其他生物的倾向解释 pet-keeping began。引语结尾才出现说话人。

  5. 05

    The idea to scrutinize our cousins for pet-owning behaviors began when Cyril Grueter, a biological anthropologist and primatologist at the University of Oxford and the new study's lead author, was visiting a Shanghai Zoo. He saw that a female gibbon managed to capture a small mouse, gently holding and caressing it in her hand. "She kept taking very good care of that little mouse," Dr. Grueter said, "and the mouse seemed to be comfortable in her palm."

    牛津大学的生物人类学家和灵长类动物学家、也是这项新研究的首席作者西里尔·格鲁特（Cyril Grueter），在访问上海动物园时，萌生了研究我们灵长类近亲是否也饲养宠物的想法。他看到一只雌性长臂猿成功捕获了一只小老鼠，并将其轻轻捧在掌心，温柔地抚摸着。“她一直非常细心地照顾那只小老鼠，”格鲁特博士说，“而那只老鼠似乎在她手掌里也很自在。”

    scrutinize
    细致审查或观察；研究者要逐一检查其他灵长类动物有没有类似养宠物的行为。
    primatologist
    灵长类动物学家；研究猴、猿等灵长类动物的学者。
    caressing
    轻柔地抚摸；指长臂猿把小鼠握在掌心并抚摸它。
  6. 06

    Interspecies interactions like these might hold key clues about why we fill our households with furry creatures, Dr. Grueter reasoned.

    格鲁特博士推断，像这样的物种间的互动，可能会为我们为何会在家中饲养毛茸茸的动物提供重要线索。

    interspecies
    跨物种的；interspecies interactions 指不同物种之间的互动。
  7. 07

    To move beyond isolated examples, he and his colleagues scoured media reports and scientific literature for similar accounts of primates interacting with other species. Their assessment drew 427 entries across 88 primate species. The data revealed that every primate, regardless of species, held some behavior that could be linked to pet-keeping.

    为了超越这些孤立的例子，他和同事们仔细查阅了媒体报道和科学文献，寻找灵长类动物与其他物种互动的类似案例。他们的评估共收集到涉及88种灵长类动物的427条记录。数据表明，每种灵长类动物，无论属于哪个物种，都表现出某种与饲养宠物有关联的行为。

    scoured
    搜遍、仔细找过；scour A for B 指翻查媒体和学术资料寻找类似记录。
    accounts
    对事件的记录或描述；similar accounts 指媒体和论文里记载的相似案例。
