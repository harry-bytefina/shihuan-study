文化遗产、艺术与跨文化

Storytelling is an ancient art form that has been used to hand down legends, tales and factual stories. Perhaps the first thing that springs to mind when you think of storytelling is the fantasy land that took shape in your mind while Mom or Dad told you stories about princes, castles and monsters, with a unique voice for each character.

Storytelling does not just take place at bedtime or round a campfire, however. It can take some other forms, either via the books we read or the films we watch. But they usually have some elements in common: rather than a list of dry facts, stories have plots and they introduce characters. We respond to stories, particularly when there is emotional detail.

And unlike what we suppose, storytelling is not just a form of entertainment, but a form of learning as well. Long before reading and writing became widely spread and available, oral storytelling had already been a form that the wisdom and knowledge of the people were passed down from elders to children. A good story engages our curiosity, emotions and imagination.

Storytelling helps with learning also because stories are easy to remember. Research has found that learning obtained from a well-told story is remembered more accurately, and for far longer, than from facts and figures.

And above all, stories have a transformative power to allow us to see the world in a different way than we do if we just encounter it on our own. Stories are an entry point to understanding a different experience of the world.

This aspect of storytelling—presenting a different perspective of the world—is important when it comes to connecting with each other. It gives us an opportunity to learn from another person's experience and it can shape, strengthen or challenge our opinions and values. So, when someone tells us their own personal story, we catch a glimpse (瞥见) of a view of the world that may be slightly or significantly different from our own. When we see the world as they see it, or walk in their shoes, the experience can inspire empathy (共情;同理心) with them.

为什么我们总为奥斯卡落选者“意难平”？

词汇梳理背景补充
  1. 01

    It's an honor just to be nominated. Alas, one is not always granted the option for such graciousness. When actors are left out of the running for an Academy Award, they have to make do with being the subject of public uproar, of cries for justice. The Oscar snub has become its own conversational domain, a prominent outgrowth of the Oscars themselves.

    被提名就是一种荣幸。可惜，人们并非总能有机会展现这般风度。当演员连角逐奥斯卡奖的资格都没有得到时，他们只能将就着接受自己成为公众舆论哗然的焦点，至少还有人为自己打抱不平。“奥斯卡遗珠”已演变成一个独立的讨论场，成为奥斯卡奖本身一个重要的衍生话题。

    graciousness
    表现得有风度、得体。这里指演员能以大方的态度接受评奖结果。
    snub
    冷落；有意不给某人应有的重视。Oscar snub 常指影迷眼中的“遗珠”或被亏待的落选。
    uproar
    群起抗议；舆论哗然。这里说影迷公开为落选者鸣不平。
  2. 02

    During snubbing season, there are endless posts, pieces, reels and threads, all arguing about which art and artists should have been recognized by the academy. One could go so far as to suggest that our fixation on Oscar snubs is healthy. In a culture that can't concentrate, that routinely forgets the names of films and actors, we have trained ourselves to notice what isn't there.

    在这个“遗珠季”，无数的帖子、文章、短视频和一连串讨论，都在争论哪些艺术作品和艺术家本该获得学院的认可。我们甚至可以这样说：我们对奥斯卡遗珠的执念是健康的。在一个注意力涣散、人们常常记不住电影和演员名字的文化中，我们却能训练自己去察觉那些缺席的存在。

    fixation
    过度的关注或执念。这里说大家反复讨论谁本该入围。

    句子结构

    During snubbing season, / there are endless posts, pieces, reels and threads, / all arguing about which art and artists should have been recognized by the academy.

    there are 后列举四类内容，all 回指这四类说法，arguing 说明它们争论什么。which art and artists 是 about 后的疑问从句里的一整个主语，which 意为「哪些」。should have been recognized 说的是本应得到的认可。

  3. 03

    For many fans, becoming agitated over the Oscars is a simple way of telegraphing sophistication and memory. It's an extension of fandom to be nonplused that one's favorite actor or film was left off a list. If people take these things personally, it's because a film has done its job.

    对许多影迷来说，因为奥斯卡奖情绪激动，可以简单直接地展现自己品味高、记性好。对于自己最喜欢的演员或电影落选感到错愕茫然，也是粉丝行为的一种延伸。如果人们把这些事都往心里去，那恰恰说明一部电影成功地触动了人心。

    nonplused
    困惑得一时不知怎么反应。这里说影迷对自己喜欢的作品落选感到错愕。
    telegraph
    有意或无意地显露。这里是借评论奖项来让别人看出自己的品味。
  4. 04

    The moviegoing public will often use the word "overlooked" when discussing snubs. But it's not accurate. No one escaped anyone's attention. Choices were made. Some of them hurt.

    观影大众在讨论这些遗珠时，常常会用“被忽视”这个词。但这种说法并不准确。没有人真的被忽视，不过是评委做出了选择，而其中一些选择令人心痛。

  5. 05

    We all know what this is like. When you receive approval, your peers are a bunch of geniuses. When you don't, they are out to lunch. What's always been surreal is seeing this emotional parade televised. And yet, we at home continue to crave more of what snubbing season has offered, more moments of humanity, not just humility.

    我们都对此深有体会：当你获得认可时，你的同行们就是一群慧眼识珠的天才；当你落选时，他们就是有眼无珠的糊涂虫。向来令人感到荒诞的，是看着种种情绪如同一场游行，被搬上电视屏幕。然而，我们这些坐在电视机前的观众，却依然渴望着“遗珠季”能带来更多东西，更多展现真实人性的时刻，而不仅仅是谦卑地做做样子。

    surreal
    不真实得像在做梦的。这里形容这种情绪场面被电视播出来时的荒诞感。
    humility
    谦逊。这里指面对奖项结果时表现出来的谦卑姿态。

背景补充

奥斯卡的「遗珠」不是正式奖项

Oscar snub 是媒体和观众的说法，指原本被看好的人或作品没进提名名单。提名与最后评奖是两轮投票；多数奖项的提名由对应的专业分支或符合条件的会员投出，最佳影片的提名则面向所有分支的合资格会员。Snub 带着「本该入围」的主观看法，不能当作官方认定。
