02-2022新高考Ⅰ，B

食物、农业与地方身份

Like most of us, I try to be mindful of food that goes to waste. The arugula (芝麻菜) was to make a nice green salad, rounding out a roast chicken dinner. But I ended up working late. Then friends called with a dinner invitation. I stuck the chicken in the freezer. But as days passed, the arugula went bad. Even worse, I had unthinkingly bought way too much;I could have made six salads with what I threw out.

In a world where nearly 800 million people a year go hungry, “food waste goes against the moral grain,” as Elizabeth Royte writes in this month’s cover story. It’s jaw-dropping how much perfectly good food is thrown away—from “ugly” (but quite eatable) vegetables rejected by grocers to large amounts of uneaten dishes thrown into restaurant garbage cans.

Producing food that no one eats wastes the water, fuel, and other resources used to grow it. That makes food waste an environmental problem. In fact, Royte writes, “if food waste were a country, it would be the third largest producer of greenhouse gases in the world.”

If that’s hard to understand, let’s keep it as simple as the arugula at the back of my refrigerator. Mike Curtin sees my arugula story all the time—but for him, it’s more like 12 boxes of donated strawberries nearing their last days. Curtin is CEO of DC Central Kitchen in Washington, D. C., which recovers food and turns it into healthy meals. Last year it recovered more than 807,500 pounds of food by taking donations and collecting blemished (有瑕疵的) produce that otherwise would have rotted (腐烂) in fields. And the strawberries? Volunteers will wash, cut, and freeze or dry them for use in meals down the road.

Such methods seem obvious, yet so often we just don’t think. “Everyone can play a part in reducing waste, whether by not purchasing more food than necessary in your weekly shopping or by asking restaurants to not include the side dish you won’t eat,” Curtin says.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What does the author want to show by telling the arugula story?

  • A.We pay little attention to food waste.
  • B.We waste food unintentionally at times.
  • C.We waste more vegetables than meat.
  • D.We have good reasons for wasting food.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1. B推理判断题。根据第一段中的“Like most of us, I try to be mindful of food that goes to waste.”及“Then friends called with a dinner invitation. I stuck the chicken in the freezer. But as days passed, the arugula went bad.”可知，作者是一位比较关注食物浪费的人，但因朋友邀请共进晚餐，导致几天后芝麻菜变质了。由此可推知作者讲述芝麻菜的故事是想表明“我们有时会无意地浪费食物”。

2What is a consequence of food waste according to the text?

  • A.Moral decline.
  • B.Environmental harm.
  • C.Energy shortage.
  • D.Worldwide starvation.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

2. B细节理解题。根据第三段中的“That makes food waste an environmental problem.”可知，食物浪费会引起环境问题，食物浪费的结果就是伤害环境。

3What does Curtin’s company do?

  • A.It produces kitchen equipment.
  • B.It turns rotten arugula into clean fuel.
  • C.It helps local farmers grow fruits.
  • D.It makes meals out of unwanted food.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

3. D细节理解题。根据第四段中的“Curtin is CEO of DC Central Kitchen in Washington, D.C.,which recovers food and turns it into healthy meals...by taking donations and collecting blemished produce that otherwise would have rotted in fields.”可知，Curtin的公司用人们不想要的食物来做饭，将食物回收并转化为健康的食物。

4What does Curtin suggest people do?

  • A.Buy only what is needed.
  • B.Reduce food consumption.
  • C.Go shopping once a week.
  • D.Eat in restaurants less often.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

4. A推理判断题。根据最后一段中的“Everyone can play a part in reducing waste, whether by not purchasing more food than necessary in your weekly shopping”可知，Curtin建议人们在每周的购物中不购买非必需的食物，即只买需要的东西。

打卡网红美食：错过，比饥饿更难受

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Why travellers keep queueing for viral food

    游客为何总为网红美食排长队

  2. 02

    Thomas A P van Leeuwen has a riveting view from his Amsterdam flat. Day after day, tourists form long queues on the bridge, holding up €5.50 cones of fries against the gabled backdrop for TikTok or Instagram posts.

    从自己阿姆斯特丹的公寓望去，托马斯·A P·凡·莱文（Thomas A P van Leeuwen）总能看到一幅引人入胜的景象。游客们日复一日地在桥上排起长队，举着 5.5 欧元的纸筒薯条，以山形墙建筑为背景，拍摄发TikTok或Ins的素材。

    gabled
    adj. 有山墙的，山墙式的
    riveting
    adj. 极具吸引力的，令人目不转睛的
    cone
    n. 圆锥体
  3. 03

    Across the world, travellers are now willing to wait an hour or more for trendy takes on everyday staples. A queue doesn't just signal popularity, it triggers powerful psychological cues. The fear of missing out (FOMO) is the strongest explanation for why people wait for food they've only heard about, says Rachel S Herz, author of Why You Eat What You Eat.

    如今，世界各地的游客都愿意为那些花样翻新的日常小吃，排队等候一个小时甚至更久。排队现象不仅是人气象征，更会触发强烈的心理暗示。《饮食心理学》Why You Eat What You Eat. 一书作者瑞秋・赫茨表示（Rachel S Herz），错失恐惧症（FOMO）最能解释为什么人们甘愿为“只听过，没尝过”的食物排大队。

  4. 04

    Whether we realise it or not, waiting in line for trending food is all part of a broader performance. People aren't just eating the viral food—they're filming themselves doing it, with their subsequent posts indicating to others that they're onto something special.

    无论我们是否意识到，为网红美食排队本身，就是一场更宏大 “表演”的一部分。人们不仅在品尝这些网红食物，——还在拍自己吃的过程，随后发帖向别人表明：自己发现了很特别的东西。

  5. 05

    And because so much of this performance now happens online, the places people visit aren't discovered through wandering; they're delivered by algorithms. That changes not just how travellers behave, but where they go in the first place.

    由于这场“表演”如今大多发生在线上，人们的旅行目的地不再靠闲逛发现，而是经由算法推送。这不仅改变着游客的行为模式，更从根源上重塑了他们的旅行动线。

    wander
    v. 漫无目的地闲逛，探索
    algorithm
    n. 算法
  6. 06

    But it comes at a cost. Algorithms prioritise what is already popular, not what is diverse or under-the-radar, which means the same places are pushed to millions of people at the same time. The meteoric rise is as much a strain on existing infrastructure as an economic opportunity.

    但这种现象也伴随着代价。算法优先推送已具热度而非多元或小众的内容，这意味着同一批地点会同时进入数百万人视野。流量的爆发式增长在带来经济机遇的同时，也对既有的基础设施造成压力。

    strain
    n. 压力
    under-the-radar
    不引人注目的，低调的，小众的
  7. 07

    Still, crowds rarely deter people. For many, the wait has become as meaningful as the meal.

    即便如此，拥挤的人潮很少会劝退游客。对许多人而言，等待早已和餐食本身一样，意义非凡。

    deter
    v. 阻挠，制止
