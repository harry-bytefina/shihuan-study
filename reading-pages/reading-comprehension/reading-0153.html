02-2021全国乙，B

志愿服务、社区与包容

When almost everyone has a mobile phone,why are more than half of Australian homes still paying for a landline (座机)?

These days you'd be hard pressed to find anyone in Australia over the age of 15 who doesn't own a mobile phone. In fact plenty of younger kids have one in their pocket. Practically everyone can make and receive calls anywhere,anytime.

Still, 55 percent of Australians have a landline phone at home and only just over a quarter (29%) rely only on their smartphones, according to a survey(调查). Of those Australians who still have a landline,a third concede that it's not really necessary and they're keeping it as a security blanket—19 percent say they never use it while a further 13 percent keep it in case of emergencies.I think my home falls into that category.

More than half of Australian homes are still choosing to stick with their home phone. Age is naturally a factor(因素)—only 58 percent of Generation Ys still use landlines now and then,compared to 84 percent of Baby Boomers who've perhaps had the same home number for 50 years. Age isn't the only factor; I'd say it's also to do with the makeup of your household.

Generation Xers with young families, like my wife and I, can still find it convenient to have a home phone rather than providing a mobile phone for every family member. That said, to be honest the only people who ever ring our home phone are our Baby Boomers parents, to the point where we play a game and guess who is calling before we pick up the phone(using Caller ID would take the fun out of it).

How attached are you to your landline?How long until they go the way of gas street lamps and morning milk deliveries?

1What does paragraph 2 mainly tell us about mobile phones?

  • A.Their target users.
  • B.Their wide popularity.
  • C.Their major functions.
  • D.Their complex design.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1.B　主旨要义题。根据第二段第一句(如今,在澳大利亚你很难发现15岁以上的人没有手机。)和第二段中的关键词“plenty of younger kids”“everyone can make and receive calls”可知,手机在澳大利亚非常流行,故答案为B项。

2What does the underlined word “concede” in paragraph 3 mean?

  • A.Admit.
  • B.Argue.
  • C.Remember.
  • D.Remark.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

2.A　猜测词义题。虽然在澳大利亚,超过一半的家庭还留着座机,三分之一拥有座机的澳大利亚人不会把它当作一种必需品,而是把它当作一种安全保障,19%的人永远不会使用它,13%的人保留座机是为了预防紧急情况。由此可判断画线单词的意思为“承认”,故选A项(Admit)。

3What can we say about Baby Boomers?

  • A.They like smartphone games.
  • B.They enjoy guessing callers' identity.
  • C.They keep using landline phones.
  • D.They are attached to their family.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

3.C　推理判断题。根据题干中的“Baby Boomers”将答案区域迅速锁定在第四段,根据关键信息“84 percent of Baby Boomers who've perhaps had the same home number for 50 years.”可知,婴儿潮一代保留着使用座机的习惯,故答案为C项。

4What can be inferred about the landline from the last paragraph?

  • A.It remains a family necessity.
  • B.It will fall out of use some day.
  • C.It may increase daily expenses.
  • D.It is as important as the gas light.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

4.B　推理判断题。“How long until they go the way of gas street lamps and morning milk deliveries?”中作者提到的煤气街灯和早晨送奶服务都是被淘汰了的旧事物,因此可推断作者认为将来座机会被淘汰,故答案为B项。

阅读难度参考：CSE 6

外媒视角下的中国相亲角：A4 纸写满房车养老金，藏着两代人博弈

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Every Friday and Saturday morning, hundreds pour into a hilltop park in this southwestern Chinese city looking for love. It's a real-life version of a dating app—except the people "swiping" are mostly retirees eager to find spouses for their adult children.

    在中国西南部这座城市的山顶公园里，每个周五和周六的早晨，都会有数百人涌入，只为寻找爱情。这就像现实版的交友软件——只不过在这里“滑动浏览”的，大多是急于为成年子女物色配偶的退休老人。

    spouse
    n. 配偶
    retiree
    n. 退休人员
    swipe
    v. 翻阅浏览
  2. 02

    They call it a xiangqin jiao, which translates to "matchmaking corner." In English, it's often dubbed a "marriage market."

    他们把这里称作“相亲角”，英文里常称之为“婚姻市场”。

  3. 03

    It involves paper résumés that function as dating profiles. There's the standard information: gender, age, height, job and what they want in a partner. But some include details that would be taboo in the U.S., like weight, earnings and whether the parents have pensions. Many don't include a photo or name.

    相亲角里，人们用纸质简历充当交友资料。简历上有基本信息：性别、年龄、身高、职业以及择偶要求。不过，有些在美国会被视为禁忌的细节问题，比如体重、收入，还有父母是否有养老金等，这里也会写上。很多简历上没有照片，也没有姓名。

    taboo
    adj. 禁忌的，忌讳的
    profile
    n. 简介，个人资料
  4. 04

    One volunteer matchmaker, an older woman known as Sister Gao, comes each week with dozens of profiles for attendees to browse. She spends much of her time reassuring parents who don't understand why their children haven't gotten hitched. "In our generation, people put up with a lot. Today, young people think, 'why should I settle?'" Gao said.

    一位名叫高大姐的志愿者红娘每周都会来这里，带着几十份资料供参与者翻阅。她大部分时间都在安抚那些不明白孩子为何还没结婚的父母。“我们那一代人，什么都能忍。现在的年轻人想的是，‘我凭什么要将就？’”高大姐说。

    reassure
    v. 使某人再次相信，使人安心、放下焦虑
  5. 05

    Kids these days, you might have heard, aren't getting married. The picture in China is particularly bleak. In the country of more than a billion, just 6.1 million couples registered their marriages in 2024, according to government data, down about 21% from the previous year and a record low.

    你可能听说过，现在的年轻人都不愿意结婚了。在中国，这种情况尤为严峻。政府数据显示，在这个拥有十几亿人口的国家，2024年仅有610万对新人登记结婚，较前一年下降约21%，创下历史新低。

    bleak
    adj. 前景黯淡、令人担忧的；荒凉的，阴冷的
  6. 06

    For wannabe-grandparents, marriage markets offer somewhere to channel their angst. Marriage-market success stories are largely unheard of, yet the events remain highly attended. One theory: the meetups are also an excuse for retirees to hangout and gossip.

    对于那些盼着抱孙子的老人来说，婚姻市场成了他们发泄焦虑的地方。虽然婚姻市场里鲜有成功牵手的案例，但这类活动依然人气颇高。有一种说法是，这些聚会也是退休老人们聚在一起闲聊八卦的由头。

    angst
    n. 深层的焦虑、不安
