阅读理解

04-2014天津，D

睡眠、运动与身心健康

People aren't walking any more—if they can figure out a way to avoid it.

I felt superior about this matter until the other day I took my car to mail a small parcel.The journey is a matter of 281 steps.But I used the car.And I wasn't in any hurry,either.I had merely become one more victim of a national sickness:motorosis.

It is an illness to which I had thought myself immune(免疫的),for I was bred in the tradition of going to places on my own two legs.At that time,we regarded 25 miles as a good day's walk and the ability to cover such a distance in ten hours as a sign of strength and skill.It did not occur to us that walking was a hardship.And the effect was lasting.When I was 45 years old I raced—and beat—a teenage football player the 168 steps up the Statue of Liberty.

Such enterprises today are regarded by many middle-aged persons as bad for the heart.But a well-known British physician,Sir Adolphe Abrahams,pointed out recently that hearts and bodies need proper exercise.A person who avoids exercise is more likely to have illnesses than one who exercises regularly.And walking is an ideal form of exercise—the most familiar and natural of all.

It was Henry Thoreau who showed mankind the richness of going on foot.The man walking can learn the trees,flowers,insects,birds and animals,the significance of seasons,the very feel of himself as a living creature in a living world.He cannot learn in a car.

The car is a convenient means of transport,but we have made it our way of life.Many people don't dare to approach Nature any more;to them the world they were born to enjoy is all threat.To them security is a steel river thundering on a concrete road.And much of their thinking takes place while waiting for the traffic light to turn green.

I say that the green of forests is the mind's best light.And none but the man on foot can evaluate what is basic and everlasting.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What is the national sickness?

  • A.Walking too much.
  • B.Traveling too much.
  • C.Driving cars too much.
  • D.Climbing stairs too much.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

1.C　细节理解题。由第二段最后一句I had merely become one more victim of a national sickness:motorosis.可知,国民的通病就是开车太多。

2What was life like when the author was young?

  • A.People usually went around on foot.
  • B.People often walked 25 miles a day.
  • C.People used to climb the Statue of Liberty.
  • D.People considered a ten-hour walk as a hardship.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

2.A　细节理解题。由第三段中for I was bred in the tradition of going to places on my own two legs可知,作者小时候的传统就是无论去哪里都靠两条腿,故选择A项。

3The author mentions Henry Thoreau to prove that _____ .

  • A.middle-aged people like getting back to nature
  • B.walking in nature helps enrich one's mind
  • C.people need regular exercise to keep fit
  • D.going on foot prevents heart disease
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3.B　推理判断题。根据第五段的描述可知,步行可以使人更好地了解自然界和自我。由此可推断作者引用Henry Thoreau的例子就是为了证明步行能丰富人的思维。故选B。

4What is compared to“a steel river”in Paragraph 6?

  • A.A queue of cars.
  • B.A ray of traffic light.
  • C.A flash of lightning.
  • D.A stream of people.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

4.A　词义猜测题。由画线词后面的内容“thundering on a concrete road在混凝土路面上轰隆隆地移动”可猜测出,作者在这里把a steel river比作川流不息的车队。

5What is the author's intention of writing this passage?

  • A.To tell people to reflect more on life.
  • B.To recommend people to give up driving.
  • C.To advise people to do outdoor activities.
  • D.To encourage people to return to walking.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

5.D　主旨大意题。文章最后一段告诉我们,绿色可以净化人的心灵,步行者可以判断什么是基本和永恒的东西。由此可判断作者的写作目的是鼓励我们多步行。故选D。

如何活得久，活得好？女性在“长寿医学领域”开启高光时代

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Step Aside Longevity Bros. It's Time for the Longevity Ladies.

    “长寿兄弟”请让一让，该轮到“长寿女士”登场了。

  2. 02

    Women have long dominated the wellness industry, with a focus on keeping the skin dewy and the limbs slender and Pilates-toned. Women have also been major contributors to the science of aging. The biologist Cynthia Kenyon helped launch the field with her 1993 discovery that a single gene mutation doubled the lifespan of worms. Kenyon is now vice president of aging research at Calico Life Sciences, a company focused on the biology of aging.

    长久以来，女性在健康行业中占据着主导地位，关注的重点是保持肌肤水润，让四肢纤细并充满普拉提式的力量感。在延缓衰老的科学研究领域，女性也作出了重要贡献。生物学家辛西娅·肯扬（Cynthia Kenyon）在1993年发现，仅仅通过一个基因突变，就能让蠕虫的寿命翻倍，这一发现促成了衰老研究领域的开创。如今，肯扬在卡里科生命科学公司（Calico Life Sciences）担任衰老研究副总裁，该公司专注于对衰老机制的研究。

    dominate
    v. 在某领域占据主导地位
    dewy
    adj. 水润的
  3. 03

    Still, when it comes to longevity, particularly the high-profile, publicity-generating pursuit of lifespans extending to 100 and beyond, men have been the faces of the movement.

    然而，说到长寿，情况就不同了。尤其是那些为了追求活到100岁甚至更久而高调进行的尝试，引来了无数媒体报道，男性一直是这股风潮的代表人物。

    publicity-generating
    adj. 制造很多话题的，极具媒体曝光度的
  4. 04

    That is starting to change with the rise of an increasingly prominent group of female scientists, entrepreneurs and influencers. In a field dominated by "longevity bros," they could be called the "longevity ladies."

    随着一批女性科学家、企业家和有影响力的人士在该领域日益崭露头角，局面开始发生变化。她们或许可以被称为“长寿女士”，正在改写“长寿兄弟”即男性主导的行业格局。

  5. 05

    What these longevity ladies all address in varied ways is a mystery that continues to perplex the field. Women live longer than men, five years on average in the U.S. Yet women also spend more years of their lives than men do in poorer health.

    这群“长寿女士”正在以各种方式探究一个困扰长寿领域至今的谜题：女性寿命比男性长，在美国平均多出5年，但女性在健康状况较差的状态下度过的岁月也比男性要多。

    perplex
    v. 困扰，使困惑不解
  6. 06

    The rise of the longevity ladies can be attributed to the convergence of two major changes. One is a broader cultural embrace of the role lifestyle choices, such as social connections and a good diet, can play in both enriching a life and lengthening it. These are areas where female voices have traditionally held sway.

    长寿女士们的崛起可归因于两大变化的共同作用。其一是社会文化层面，越来越多人意识到生活方式的选择对健康长寿的重要性，比如（良好的）人际关系、健康饮食等因素既能提升生活质量，也能延长寿命。而在这些领域，女性的意见一直都很有影响力。

    convergence
    n. 汇聚，交汇
    enrich
    v. 让……更充实、更有意义
    attribute
    把……归因于……
    lengthen
    v. 延长
  7. 07

    The other big driver in the rise of the longevity ladies is the emergence of scientific findings showing the role that sex differences play in the aging process—and a growing belief that research on women might improve how aging is understood and treated in men too.

    另一个重要推动力是，科学研究逐渐揭示出性别差异在衰老过程中所起的作用，人们也越来越相信，针对女性的研究或许也能帮助理解男性的衰老机制，并改善应对方式。
