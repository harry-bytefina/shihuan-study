阅读理解

04-2017天津夏，D

食物、农业与地方身份

I read somewhere that we spend a full third of our lives waiting. But where are we doing all of this waiting, and what does it mean to an impatient society like ours?To understand the issue, let's take a look at three types of “waits”.

The very purest form of waiting is the Watched-Pot Wait. It is without doubt the most annoying of all. Take filling up the kitchen sink(洗碗池)as an example. There is absolutely nothing you can do while this is going on but keep both eyes fixed on the sink until it's full. During these waits, the brain slips away from the body and wanders about until the water runs over the edge of the counter and onto your socks. This kind of wait makes the waiter helpless and mindless.

A cousin to the Watched-Pot Wait is the Forced Wait. This one requires a bit of discipline. Properly preparing packaged noodle soup requires a Forced Wait. Directions are very specific.“Bring three cups of water to boil, add mix, simmer three minutes, remove from heat, let stand five minutes.”I have my doubts that anyone has actually followed the procedures strictly. After all, Forced Waiting requires patience.

Perhaps the most powerful type of waiting is the Lucky-Break Wait. This type of wait is unusual in that it is for the most part voluntary. Unlike the Forced Wait, which is also voluntary, waiting for your lucky break does not necessarily mean that it will happen.

Turning one's life into a waiting game requires faith and hope, and is strictly for the optimists among us. On the surface it seems as ridiculous as following the directions on soup mixes, but the Lucky-Break Wait well serves those who are willing to do it. As long as one doesn't come to rely on it, wishing for a few good things to happen never hurts anybody.

We certainly do spend a good deal of our time waiting. The next time you're standing at the sink waiting for it to fill while cooking noodle soup that you'll have to eat until a large bag of cash falls out of the sky, don't be desperate. You're probably just as busy as the next guy.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1While doing a Watched-Pot Wait, we tend to _____ .

  • A.keep ourselves busy
  • B.get absent-minded
  • C.grow anxious
  • D.stay focused
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1.B　细节理解题。第二段对Watched-Pot Wait进行了具体的描述。由第二段倒数第二句中的“...the brain slips away from the body and wanders about...”可知,人们在这样的等待中容易走神,故选B。

2What is the difference between the Forced Wait and the Watched-Pot Wait?

  • A.The Forced Wait requires some self-control.
  • B.The Forced Wait makes people passive.
  • C.The Watched-Pot Wait needs directions.
  • D.The Watched-Pot Wait engages body and brain.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

2.A　细节理解题。根据第三段第二句“This one requires a bit of discipline.”可知,A项中的“requires some self-control”与之相符,故选A。

3What can we learn about the Lucky-Break Wait?

  • A.It is less voluntary than the Forced Wait.
  • B.It doesn't always bring the desired result.
  • C.It is more fruitful than the Forced Wait.
  • D.It doesn't give people faith and hope.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3.B　细节理解题。 第四段对Lucky-Break Wait作了详细的描述,本段最后一句中的“waiting for your lucky break does not necessarily mean that it will happen”表明,这种等待不一定会带来预期的结果,故选B。

4What does the author advise us to do the next time we are waiting?

  • A.Take it seriously.
  • B.Don't rely on others.
  • C.Do something else.
  • D.Don't lose heart.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

4.D　细节理解题。 根据题干中的the next time可知,本题细节出现在最后一段。根据最后一段第二句“The next time...don't be desperate.”可知,作者建议我们在等待时不要绝望,故选D。

5The author supports his view by _____ .

  • A.exploring various causes of “waits”
  • B.describing detailed processes of “waits”
  • C.analyzing different categories of “waits”
  • D.revealing frustrating consequences of “waits”
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

5.C　推理判断题。本文分析了日常生活中的三种等待。故选C项。

全球最大民宿平台一纸新规，引发房东房客"家务"拉锯战

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    No more washing up: How Airbnb's new house rules have divided hosts and guests

    再也不用洗碗了：爱彼迎的新房屋守则让房东和房客产生分歧

  2. 02

    When Lisa Clewes returned to her Welsh holiday let after a group of guests checked out, she was horrified by the mess.

    当丽莎·克鲁斯（Lisa Clewes）在一群客人退房后回到她在威尔士的出租度假屋时，眼前的一片狼藉景象让她大为震惊。

    let
    出租房。英式用法，holiday let 指按短期出租的度假住宿。
  3. 03

    The house was left with broken glasses, drinks cans on the floor, pans full of food on the table and fish guts left in the garden. "It was really unbelievable. Had I had another booking the same day, I would have had to cancel or delay check-in," says Clewes.

    碎了的玻璃杯还留在房间里，地板上散落着饮料罐，桌上摆着装满食物的锅，花园里残留着鱼的内脏。“真是难以置信。如果我当天还有别的预订，我恐怕就要取消或推迟入住，”克鲁斯说。

    guts
    内脏。fish guts 是处理鱼时掏出来的内脏。
  4. 04

    She charges a cleaning fee and asks guests to empty the fridge, put rubbish in the bins and load the dishwasher.

    她会收取清洁费，并要求客人清空冰箱、把垃圾扔进垃圾桶，以及把餐具放进洗碗机。

  5. 05

    But Airbnb's new rules mean hosts can no longer ask guests to do jobs normally carried out by cleaners, including washing and putting away dishes, stripping beds and taking rubbish off site. The updated guidelines state that hosts can now set seven standard house rules and add other "reasonable" requests.

    但爱彼迎的新规定意味着，房东不再能要求房客完成通常由保洁人员负责的工作，包括清洗并收好餐具、拆下床单以及把垃圾带走。更新后的守则规定，房东现在可以设定七条基本的房屋使用规则，并添加其他“合理”的要求。

    guideline
    指导规则。这里指平台关于房东能给房客布置哪些要求的新版规定。
    strip
    把表面的东西除去。strip beds 指把床单、被套等从床上拆下。
  6. 06

    Clewes says it typically takes cleaners four hours to turn around her three-bedroom property. She fears guests leaving more mess could mean hiring extra cleaners and increasing costs – expenses that may ultimately be passed on to her guests.

    克鲁斯表示，保洁人员通常需要四个小时才能打扫完她的这套三居室。她担心，如果客人把房间搞得更脏更乱，可能意味着需要雇佣更多保洁人员，从而增加成本——这些费用最终可能会转嫁到客人身上。

  7. 07

    The changes have come as a relief to guests like Jessica Newhook, who says she has been faced with lengthy checkout lists, often on top of hefty cleaning fees. Newhook says the host told them to wash and fold towels, clean the bedding and kitchen, and sweep and mop the floors.

    这些变化让杰西卡·纽胡克（Jessica Newhook）这样的房客松了一口气，她说自己以前总要完成冗长的退房清单上列出的事，而且往往是在支付了高额的清洁费之后。纽胡克表示，房东要求他们清洗并叠好毛巾、清理床上用品和厨房，还要扫地拖地。

    hefty
    数额不小的。hefty cleaning fees 指一笔颇高的清洁费。
    mop
    用拖把拖地。与 sweep（扫地）并列，属于房客被要求做的清洁。
  8. 08

    While Newhook likes the home comforts of staying in an Airbnb, she says the combination of relentless rules and rising cleaning fees has taken away much of its appeal. "It is getting to the point where a hotel is cheaper and I know what to expect."

    虽然纽胡克喜欢入住爱彼迎时那种回家般的舒适感，但她表示，层出不穷的规则加上不断上涨的清洁费，已经让它失去了很大一部分吸引力。“现在已经到了这种程度：住酒店反而更便宜，而且它会更符合我的预期。”

    relentless
    没完没了、让人喘不过气的。这里写房客面对一长串退房要求的感受。
    appeal
    吸引力。高额费用和家务要求让这种住宿没那么吸引人了。
