How to Use a Modern Public Library

Has it been a while since your last visit to a public library?If so, you may be surprised to learn that libraries have changed for the better. It's been years since they were dusty little rooms with books. They have transformed themselves into places where you can develop your love of knowledge, meet interesting people, or find out how to start a business.

Check out a book. While libraries still loan out(出借)books, you'll find it easier to get a copy of whatever you're looking for, thanks to a cooperative network of area libraries. Via such networks, libraries share their books with each other through the use of delivery vehicles. Once the book you've requested is delivered to the nearest branch, they will inform you by e-mail, so you can pick it up.

Check out other items. The library is now a multimedia zone, loaded with information in many formats(载体形式). You can borrow movies on DVDs, music on CDs, and popular magazines. Some libraries even loan out toys and games. If a popular magazine you want isn't offered and the library keeps a list of such requests, they may bring it in when enough interest is shown.

Join targeted reading groups.Libraries will often hold reading-group sessions targeted to various age groups. Perhaps you'd like to learn a language or improve your English. The library may sponsor a language group you could join. If you have difficulties reading, ask about special reading opportunities. Your library might be able to accommodate you. And you might find it relaxing to bring your small kid to a half-hour Story Time while you sit quietly in a corner with a good book.

Start a business using the help of your local library. If you want to have a business of your own, your local library can become a launch space for it. In library books and computers, you can find information on starting a business. Many libraries will help you with locally supplied information about business management shared through chambers of commerce(商会) and government agencies, and they will offer printing, faxing and database services you need.

1Public libraries connected by a cooperative network benefit readers by _____ .

  • A.sharing their books on the Internet
  • B.giving access to online reading at a library branch
  • C.sending a needed book to a library branch nearby
  • D.making the checkout procedures diverse
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

1.C　细节理解题。根据文章第二段第三句和第四句(通过这样的网络,图书馆可以借助运输工具互相分享书籍。一旦你申请的书籍被运送到最近的分部,它们将会以邮件的形式通知你,这样你就可以领取书籍。)可知C项正确。

2.C　细节理解题。根据第三段第三句和第四句(你可以借电影光盘、音乐唱片和受欢迎的杂志。有些图书馆甚至出借玩具和游戏。)可知C项正确。

阅读难度参考：CSE 7

哈佛新课爆火：为什么严苛到极致，还人人抢着上？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    The Brutal Harvard Class That Suddenly Everyone Wants to Take

    那门严苛的哈佛课，突然人人都想上

    brutal
    adj. 严苛的，要求高得让人吃不消的
  2. 02

    On a Tuesday morning in October, Samantha Matherne paced as she marked up on a white board explaining the philosophical underpinnings of Jane Austen's "Emma."

    十月的一个周二上午，萨曼莎·马瑟恩（Samantha Matherne）一边踱步，一边在白板上写写画画，讲解简·奥斯汀《爱玛》背后的哲学根基。

    underpinning
    n. 基础，支撑
  3. 03

    The philosophy professor posed big questions to the 90 students gathered in Boylston Hall: "Why did Austen create such an unlikable heroine?" she asked. "Why read novels at all?"

    这位哲学教授向聚集在博伊尔斯顿厅的90名学生抛出了几个大问题：“奥斯汀为什么要塑造一个这么不讨喜的女主人公？”她问道，“我们到底为什么还要读小说？”

    pose
    v. 提出（问题，风险）
  4. 04

    Hum 10, as it's commonly known, has become the surprise hit of Harvard's academic lineup and something of a campus institution. Applications (essays required) shot up 40% this fall with 180 incoming freshmen vying for 90 spots.

    这门课通称 Hum 10（Humanities 10），它意外走红，成了哈佛课程表上最热门的一门课，也成了校园里的一种文化奇观。今年秋天，申请人数（需提交申请短文）激增了40%，180名大一新生竞争90个名额。

  5. 05

    The class is definitely not for padding the GPA. Students read a hefty book a week and write regular essays. There is no pass/fail option. Digital devices are verboten, and attendance is taken in every lecture (pink slips for those who arrive late).

    这门课绝不是用来刷高绩点的。学生每周要读完一本厚厚的书，还要定期写论文。课程没有“通过/不通过”的评分选项。电子设备一律禁用，每堂讲座都点名，迟到者会领到一张粉色单子。

    verboten
    adj. 被禁止的
  6. 06

    "I did have the sense that a lot of students feel that the culture at large is not hospitable to deep reading, deep thinking or deep engagement with writing," said David Elmer, a classics professor.

    “我确实感觉到，许多学生觉得，整个时代的大环境并不欢迎深度阅读、深度思考，也不欢迎深度地投入写作，”古典学教授戴维·埃尔默（David Elmer）说。

  7. 07

    It's all distinctly old school. The curriculum begins with "The Odyssey," travels forward in time to James Joyce's "Ulysses" and then backward to end with "The Iliad."

    这一切都太过老派。课程大纲从《奥德赛》（The Odyssey）开始，一路讲到詹姆斯·乔伊斯（James Joyce）的《尤利西斯》（Ulysses），再折返回去，以《伊利亚特》（The Iliad）收尾。

  8. 08

    In their applications and in interviews, students expressed the urge to be forced to tackle classics they otherwise wouldn't read, the need to combat frayed attention spans and the desire for an antidote to The Machine. Several mentioned with disdain that people who aren't in the class wondered why they didn't "just Chat the books," i.e., use AI.

    在申请短文和面试中，学生们表达了这样几种愿望：希望有人逼着自己去读那些本来不会读的经典；希望修复自己日渐涣散的注意力；也希望找到一剂对抗“机器”的解药。有几位学生还不无鄙夷地提到，没选这门课的人总问他们，为什么不“直接把书扔给 Chat”——也就是用 AI 来读。

    tackle
    v. 着手处理（困难的事）
    combat
    v. 努力对抗；n. 战斗
    frayed
    adj. 涣散的，边缘散开的
  9. 09

    "A lot of what we get out of this class is the human experience, and you can't get that out of AI," said Peggy Urdan.

    “我们从这门课里得到的东西，很多都是人的亲身经验，这是 AI 给不了的，”佩吉·厄丹（Peggy Urdan）说。

  10. 10

    At the core of the humanities is, of course, humanity.

    人文学科的核心，终究还是“人”。

    humanity
    n. 人类；人之所以为人的那些处境和特性
  11. 11

    "It's the only class I took at Harvard where people would call each other out for not having done the reading," said Ellie Powell, a Hum Alum who graduated in 2025. "We all felt like we were building something, and that was a remarkable feeling."

    “这在哈佛上过的所有课里，只有这一门，如果没读完指定书目，会被同学当面指出来，”2025年毕业的 Hum 老学员埃莉·鲍威尔（Ellie Powell）说。“我们都觉得，我们在共同建造一样东西。那种感觉，非常难得。”
