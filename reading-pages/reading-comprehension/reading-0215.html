太空、地球系统与科学探索

A new collection of photos brings an unsuccessful Antarctic voyage back to life.

Frank Hurley's pictures would be outstanding—undoubtedly first-rate photo-journalism—if they had been made last week. In fact, they were shot from 1914 through 1916, most of them after a disastrous shipwreck(海难),by a cameraman who had no reasonable expectation of survival. Many of the images were stored in an ice chest, under freezing water, in the damaged wooden ship.

The ship was the Endurance, a small, tight, Norwegian-built three-master that was intended to take Sir Ernest Shackleton and a small crew of seamen and scientists, 27 men in all, to the southernmost shore of Antarctica's Weddell Sea. From that point Shackleton wanted to force a passage by dog sled(雪橇)across the continent. The journey was intended to achieve more than what Captain Robert Falcon Scott had done. Captain Scott had reached the South Pole early in 1912 but had died with his four companions on the march back.

As writer Caroline Alexander makes clear in her forceful and well-researched story The Endurance, adventuring was even then a thoroughly commercial effort. Scott's last journey, completed as he lay in a tent dying of cold and hunger, caught the world's imagination, and a film made in his honor drew crowds. Shackleton, a onetime British merchant-navy officer who had got to within 100 miles of the South Pole in 1908, started a business before his 1914 voyage to make money from movie and still photography. Frank Hurley, a confident and gifted Australian photographer who knew the Antarctic, was hired to make the images, most of which have never before been published.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What do we know about the photos taken by Hurley?

  • A.They were made last week.
  • B.They showed undersea sceneries.
  • C.They were found by a cameraman.
  • D.They recorded a disastrous adventure.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

1.D　推理判断题。根据文章第二段第二句可知,Frank Hurley的照片拍摄于1914年到1916年,大多数是在经历海难后,在没有生存的希望的情况下拍摄的,由此可判断它们记录了一次海难。

2Who reached the South Pole first according to the text?

  • A.Frank Hurley.
  • B.Ernest Shackleton.
  • C.Robert Falcon Scott.
  • D.Caroline Alexander.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

2.C　推理判断题。根据文章第三段中的The journey was intended to achieve more than what Captain Robert Falcon Scott had done.以及下句可知,1912年Robert Falcon Scott到达了南极,而Endurance航行的目的是超越他,但是遭遇了海难,由此可判断Robert Falcon Scott到达南极的时间更早。

3What does Alexander think was the purpose of the 1914 voyage?

  • A.Artistic creation.
  • B.Scientific research.
  • C.Money making.
  • D.Treasure hunting.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

3.C　细节理解题。根据文章第四段第一句中的a thoroughly commercial effort可知,Alexander认为那时的冒险完全是一种商业行为,也就是为了赚钱,因此可判断选C项。

逐鹿“高边疆”：美国到底把什么送上了太空？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    What weapon could the US have put into space?

    美国到底可能往太空里放了什么武器？

  2. 02

    Washington has given away little in its announcement that it has deployed a space weapon in Earth's orbit. Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink said the "on-orbit" weapon was necessary to safeguard US forces against hostile enemy action, but gave no details about the weapon's capabilities or when it was put into orbit.

    华盛顿方面宣布已在地球轨道上部署了一件太空武器，但这份公告披露的信息寥寥无几。美国空军部长特洛伊・迈因克（Troy Meink）称，这款 “在轨” 武器是保护美军免受敌方敌对行动攻击所必需的，但没有透露该武器的性能，以及它何时被送入轨道。

    safeguard
    v. 保护
    hostile
    adj. 有敌意的
  3. 03

    Space has been a militarised domain since the start of the space age — in the late 1960s, the Soviet Union deployed weapons up there. The Istrebitel Sputnikov (IS; Satellite Destroyer) programme was made up of satellites that were designed to release projectiles that detonated, releasing shrapnel into the path of another (usually American) satellite.

    自太空时代开启以来，太空就已经成为军事化领域。20 世纪 60 年代末，苏联就曾在太空部署武器。“卫星歼击者” 计划由多颗卫星组成，这些卫星可以释放弹药并引爆，将爆炸弹片散布到另一颗卫星（通常是美国卫星）的运行轨道上。

    militarised
    adj. 军事化的
    detonate
    v. 引爆（比explode更强调定向引爆）
    shrapnel
    n. 弹片
  4. 04

    Nowadays, advanced militaries deploy satellites in space for a range of purposes, including reconnaissance, secure communications, detecting missile launches and providing precise location information for targeted strikes. As warfare becomes more technologically advanced, analysts expect the use of military hardware in space to increase.

    如今，军事强国在太空部署卫星，用途广泛，包括侦察、保密通信、探测导弹发射，以及为定点打击提供精准定位信息。随着战争的技术水平不断提升，分析人士预计，太空军用装备的使用会越来越多。

  5. 05

    Dr Bleddyn Bowen, an associate fellow in military sciences at the Royal United Services Institute, said, "I would expect there to be some kind of electronic warfare or radio-jamming platform, possibly, this would be a nondestructive kind of anti-satellite weapon." Such weaponry already exists on Earth and can disrupt satellite communications, he said. "If you take out the radio links then your satellite is very useless."

    英国皇家联合军种国防研究所军事科学副研究员布莱迪恩・鲍文博士（Dr Bleddyn Bowen）表示：“我猜测这可能是某种电子战或者无线电干扰平台，可能是一种非破坏性的反卫星武器。” 他说，这类武器在地面已经存在，可以干扰卫星通信。“一旦切断无线电链路，那颗卫星就没什么用了。”

    disrupt
    v. 扰乱，打断
    nondestructive
    adj. 非破坏性的
  6. 06

    Bowen also would not rule out a riskier and more disruptive type of weaponry, which could create debris and destruction.

    鲍文也不排除另一种风险更高、破坏力更强的武器，这类武器会产生太空碎片并造成损毁。

    debris
    n. 碎片，残骸 （来自法语débris）
  7. 07

    Given that weapons for use in space have been around for so long, why did the US announcement come when it did? Experts agree that it's not a major development as such, more a political signal.

    既然太空武器早已存在，美国为何偏偏在此时发布这一消息？专家们一致认为，这件事本身算不上重大技术进展，更多是一种政治信号。
