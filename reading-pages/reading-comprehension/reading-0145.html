02-2022全国乙，B

学习、阅读与教育方式

In 1916, two girls of wealthy families, best friends from Auburn, N.Y.—Dorothy Woodruff and Rosamond Underwood—traveled to a settlement in the Rocky Mountains to teach in a one⁃room schoolhouse. The girls had gone to Smith College. They wore expensive clothes. So for them to move to Elkhead, Colo. to instruct the children whose shoes were held together with string was a surprise. Their stay in Elkhead is the subject of Nothing Daunted:The Unexpected Education of Two Society Girls in the West by Dorothy Wickenden, who is a magazine editor and Dorothy Woodruff's granddaughter.

Why did they go then? Well, they wanted to do something useful. Soon, however, they realized what they had undertaken.

They moved in with a local family, the Harrisons, and, like them, had little privacy, rare baths, and a blanket of snow on their quilt when they woke up in the morning. Some mornings, Rosamond and Dorothy would arrive at the schoolhouse to find the children weeping from the cold. In spring, the snow was replaced by mud over ice.

In Wickenden's book, she expanded on the history of the West and also on feminism, which of course influenced the girls' decision to go to Elkhead. A hair⁃raising section concerns the building of the railroads, which entailed(牵涉) drilling through the Rockies, often in blinding snowstorms. The book ends with Rosamond and Dorothy's return to Auburn.

Wickenden is a very good storyteller. The sweep of the land and the stoicism(坚忍) of the people move her to some beautiful writing. Here is a picture of Dorothy Woodruff, on her horse, looking down from a hill top:“When the sun slipped behind the mountains, it shed a rosy glow all around them. Then a full moon rose. The snow was marked only by small animals: foxes, coyotes, mice, and varying hares, which turned white in the winter.”

全部题目与逐题讲解

1Why did Dorothy and Rosamond go to the Rocky Mountains?

  • A.To teach in a school.
  • B.To study American history.
  • C.To write a book.
  • D.To do sightseeing.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1.A　 细节理解题。根据第一段第一句中的traveled to a settlement in the Rocky Mountains to teach in a one⁃room schoolhouse可知,她们是去教书。

2What can we learn about the girls from paragraph 3?

  • A.They enjoyed much respect.
  • B.They had a room with a bathtub.
  • C.They lived with the local kids.
  • D.They suffered severe hardships.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2.D　推理判断题。根据第三段中的had little privacy, rare baths, and a blanket of snow on their quilt可知,她们在当地生活得很艰苦,故选D项。虽然第一句提到她们搬到了当地一户人家居住,但是并没有说是和当地的孩子住在一起,所以C项不正确。

3Which part of Wickenden's writing is hair⁃raising?

  • A.The extreme climate of Auburn.
  • B.The living conditions in Elkhead.
  • C.The railroad building in the Rockies.
  • D.The natural beauty of the West.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

3.C　细节理解题。根据第四段第二句中的A hair⁃raising section concerns the building of the railroads可知,此题选择C项。

4What is the text?

  • A.A news report.
  • B.A book review.
  • C.A children's story.
  • D.A diary entry.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

4.B　推理判断题。根据第一段最后一句中的Their stay in Elkhead is the subject of Nothing Daunted:The Unexpected Education of Two Society Girls in the West by Dorothy Wickenden可知,两个女孩在Elkhead的经历是这本书的主题,且文章的倒数第二段第一句再次出现In Wickenden's book,由此可以推断这篇文章是一则书评。

谁有资格上天才班？这场争论撕裂美国

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Why America’s Debate Over Which Children Are ‘Gifted’ Won’t Go Away

    为何美国“天才儿童”之争始终难以平息

  2. 02

    In New York City, families sparred over whether a few thousand 4-year-olds should be funneled into gifted education programs. And in Fairfax County, Va., school leaders wrestled with a thorny question: Should we still label children "gifted"?

    在纽约市，家长们为了几千名4岁儿童是否应该被送进天才教育项目而争得面红耳赤。而在弗吉尼亚州的费尔法克斯县，学区领导则纠结于一个棘手的问题：我们是否还应该给孩子贴上“天才”的标签？

    thorny
    adj. 棘手的，麻烦的；带刺的
    funnel
    v. 导向，输送
    spar
    v. 争论，言语交锋
  3. 03

    Welcome to America's gifted and talented debate. More than three million U.S. public school students are estimated to be enrolled in gifted programs. But those programs are a lightning rod, because the divide between who gets in and who is left out often falls along lines of race, income and disability status. The child from the affluent family will be twice as likely to receive gifted services, research shows.

    欢迎来到美国天才教育的辩论现场。据估计，全美有三百多万名公立学校学生进入了天才培养项目。但这些项目一直饱受争议，因为谁能进入、谁又落选的分界线，往往与种族、家庭收入以及残障状况紧密相关。研究显示，来自富裕家庭的孩子进入天才项目的概率是普通家庭孩子的两倍。

    affluent
    adj. 富裕的，富足的
  4. 04

    That divergence has generated federal civil rights complaints accusing big districts such as Los Angeles of failing to provide all children with an equal education.

    这种差异已经引发多起联邦层面的民权投诉，指控洛杉矶等大型学区未能给予所有孩子平等的教育机会。

    generate
    v. 造成，引起
    divergence
    n. 差异，分歧
  5. 05

    Some districts identify children who perform above local standards rather than national averages. That is the approach in San Luis, Ariz., a farming town on the Mexican border whose small system is among the state's poorest and once did little to help high-achieving children. But it began enrolling hundreds of fourth through eighth graders in an early college program to take advanced math and community college courses for credit.

    有些学区筛选天才儿童时参照的是本地标准而非全国平均水平。亚利桑那州圣路易斯镇就是这样一个例子，该镇位于美墨边境，当地的小型学区是全州最贫困的学区之一，过去几乎没法为优等生提供支持。但后来，该学区开始让数百名四到八年级学生参与大学预科项目，让他们修读高级数学以及社区大学课程，并获得学分。

    identify
    v. 确定，认定
  6. 06

    Homero Chavez, who runs the program, recalled a fifth grader who did not score well on a gifted test and "came back really sad." His friends teased him for having believed he was gifted. But Mr. Chavez asked the boy to join the early college program.

    负责该项目的奥梅罗·查韦斯（Homero Chavez）回忆起一名五年级小男孩，他在天才测试中成绩不佳，“回来的时候难过极了”，还被同学嘲笑“以为自己是天才”。但查韦斯先生邀请他加入大学预修项目。

    recalled
    v. 回忆
  7. 07

    He recently completed a biomedical engineering degree at an Ivy League university.

    最近，这名学生已在一所常春藤盟校完成了生物医学工程学位的课程。
