03-2025天津夏，C

动物行为与动物认知

All animals take in oxygen from the air they breathe in, and release CO2 from their blood when breathing out. Most mammals(哺乳动物) can't directly detect oxygen levels in the blood supplied to their tissues. Instead, they rely on the rising level of CO2 in their blood to signal that they might need to take a breath. But a recent study published in Science suggests seals(海豹) can sense the amount of oxygen in the blood, and change their diving behavior in response.

To find out if oxygen levels affected seal behavior, Professor McKnight at the University of St. Andrews and his colleagues created a special section in a pool where young seals were held. In one corner, there was a breathing chamber(呼吸室), where they were sheltered from the rain and the wind.

The breathing chamber was surrounded by panels that prevented surface swimming, yet swimming below the surface for about 200 feet would give the seals access to a feeder where they could eat as much fish as they liked. Once the seals got familiar with the setup, the researchers started to gradually change the composition of the air in the breathing chamber, increasing or reducing the levels of oxygen and CO2 to see an effect on their behavior. Sure enough: the higher the level of oxygen, the longer the seals stayed at the feeder.

The finding suggests that seals don't just physically respond to oxygen levels by changing their heart rate or breathing, but that they are sufficiently aware of them to change their behavior. This ability would put seals in a class beyond any land mammals that have been tested. Since oxygen levels on land remain stable, humans don't seem to have evolved(演化) to notice low blood oxygen levels, sometimes not even when they're about to pass out in free-diving.

Therefore, in free-diving without oxygen tanks, accidents are quite common. Our reliance on sensing CO2 levels in our blood instead of oxygen may be to blame. Actually, this is a perfectly reasonable strategy on land,where growing CO2 tends to signal breathing issues. But when holding our breath during diving, relying on CO2 levels is risky, especially on repeated dives. Because every time we surface and breathe in, our sensitivity to CO2 is decreased, even if its levels are already high, and this increases the chance that a person will, without awareness, pass out before they get to the surface.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What do most mammals rely on to determine when to take a breath?

  • A.The growing amount of CO2 in their blood.
  • B.The rising level of oxygen in their lungs.
  • C.The intensity of their physical activity.
  • D.The blood supply to body tissues.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1. A　细节理解题。根据第一段中“they rely on the rising level of CO2 in their blood to signal that they might need to take a breath”可知,哺乳动物依靠血液中二氧化碳含量的上升判断可能需要进行呼吸。故选A。

2When would the seals stay at the feeder for a longer period of time?

  • A.When they needed to take in more food at the feeder.
  • B.When the oxygen level in the chamber grew higher.
  • C.When they familiarized themselves with the setup.
  • D.When the CO2 level in the chamber was raised.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

2. B　推理判断题。根据第三段中“the higher the level of oxygen, the longer the seals stayed at the feeder”可推知,当呼吸室中氧气浓度升高时,海豹会在喂食器处停留更长的时间。故选B。

3What results in humans' inability to notice low oxygen levels in their blood?

  • A.The unstable CO2 levels in the air.
  • B.Their lack of attention to breathing.
  • C.The constant oxygen levels on land.
  • D.Their functionally changeable heart rate.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

3. C　推理判断题。根据第四段中“Since oxygen levels on land remain stable...”可推知,因为陆地上的氧气含量一直保持稳定,所以人们无法察觉血液中氧气含量降低这一情况。故选C。

4Why do accidents often occur when divers go free-diving?

  • A.Their breath cannot be held long enough.
  • B.They cannot adjust the consumption of oxygen.
  • C.They may fail to notice rising CO2 levels soon enough.
  • D.Their breathing organs stop working properly underwater.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

4. C　推理判断题。根据第五段内容,尤其是“Because every time we surface and breathe in, our sensitivity to CO2 is decreased”可知,人类主要依靠对血液中二氧化碳浓度的变化判断血氧水平,但潜水过程会使人类对二氧化碳浓度变化的感知力不断降低,因此人类在潜水过程中容易出现意外。故选C。

5Which statement is probably supported by McKnight's seal research?

  • A.Seals have evolved to survive in low oxygen environments.
  • B.Seals are quick to sense oxygen levels and act accordingly.
  • C.Seals can maintain their heart rate even with low blood oxygen levels.
  • D.Seals are more sensitive to changes in the environment than other mammals.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

5. B　主旨要义题。根据McKnight的实验发现可知,海豹并非只是通过改变心率或呼吸来对氧气含量做出生理反应,而是能够充分意识到氧气水平的变化,并据此调整自己的行为。故选B(海豹能够迅速感知氧气含量,并据此做出反应)。

养狗使人长寿，但前提是你得做到这一点

词汇梳理背景补充
  1. 01

    Research dating back decades has found that people who own pets, especially dogs, tend to be healthier than people who don't.

    数十年前的研究就已发现，养宠物的人，尤其是养狗的人，往往比不养宠物的人更健康。

  2. 02

    Studies show that having a pet is associated with lower blood pressure, a reduced risk of cardiovascular disease and lower rates of death after a heart attack or stroke. And a large review of studies published in 2019 found that owning a dog was associated with a 24 percent lower risk of dying from all causes over the course of 10 years.

    研究表明，养宠物与血压下降、心血管疾病患病风险降低，以及心脏病发作或中风后死亡率降低之间均存在关联。2019年发表的一项大型综述研究发现，在10年时间里，养狗的人因各种原因死亡的风险降低了24%。

    cardiovascular
    心血管的。cardiovascular disease 指心脏和血管疾病。
  3. 03

    The correlation is very convincing. Now the question is: Is this relationship causal?

    这种相关性非常有说服力。现在的问题是：这种关联是因果关系吗？

    correlation
    相关性。两件事在统计中一同出现或变化，尚不能说明谁是原因。
    causal
    有因果关系的。这里问的是养狗本身是否会使人更健康。
  4. 04

    Dr. Adrian Bauman, an emeritus professor of public health at the University of Sydney in Australia, published a meta-analysis in 2012 that found most dog owners who walked their dogs met the standard exercise guidelines of 150 minutes of moderately intense physical activity per week. But only 60 percent of owners actually walked them.

    澳大利亚悉尼大学公共卫生荣誉退休教授阿德里安·鲍曼博士（Dr. Adrian Bauman）2012年发表的一项荟萃分析发现，经常遛狗的养狗人，大多能达到指南建议的每周150分钟中等强度运动标准。但实际上只有60%的养狗人会遛狗。

    emeritus
    退休后保有名誉头衔的。emeritus professor 常译为荣休教授。
    meta-analysis
    荟萃分析。用统计方法把多个研究的结果放在一起估计。

    句子结构

    Adrian Bauman, an emeritus professor of public health at the University of Sydney in Australia, / published a meta-analysis in 2012 / that found most dog owners who walked their dogs met the standard exercise guidelines of 150 minutes of moderately intense physical activity per week.

    that found … 修饰 a meta-analysis，中间夹着时间状语 in 2012。found 后省略了宾语从句的连接词 that；这个从句的谓语是 met。who walked their dogs 只限定 dog owners，研究说的是遛狗的人，不能泛化成所有养狗的人。

  5. 05

    "We have to distinguish dog ownership — having a dog in your household — from dog walking," Dr. Bauman said.

    鲍曼博士说：“我们必须把养狗，即家里有狗，和遛狗区分开来。”

  6. 06

    Experts say another way dogs may benefit people's health and longevity is by improving mental well-being. People who are single or live alone appear to gain most from the companionship a pet offers.

    专家表示，狗对人类健康和长寿的另一个益处，在于能帮助人改善心理健康。单身或独居的人，似乎最能从宠物的陪伴中获益。

    longevity
    长寿。这里说的是宠物陪伴对人寿命可能有的影响。
    companionship
    陪伴。这里指宠物给独居者带来的陪伴感。
  7. 07

    Lest cat lovers feel left out, a few studies have also found that owning a cat was associated with a lower risk of dying from a heart attack or stroke, possibly by providing stress relief.

    我们也不要忘了爱猫人士，一些研究发现，养猫同样与较低的心脏病发作或中风死亡风险之间存在关联，这可能是因为养猫有助于缓解压力。

  8. 08

    Or the health benefits of pet ownership may simply be an effect of demographics. Dog owners tend to be younger and richer than non-owners, characteristics that correspond with better health. It's hard to tease apart whether dogs make people healthier or if healthier people are more likely to have dogs.

    又或者，养宠物带来的健康益处，可能只是因为养宠物的人群本身具有某些共同特征。养狗的人往往比不养狗的人更年轻、更富裕，而这些特质通常就对应着更好的健康状况。我们很难分清楚：到底是养狗让人更健康，还是更健康的人更有可能养狗。

    demographics
    人群的构成特征。这里涉及养狗人群与非养狗人群在年龄、经济状况上的差别。

背景补充

荟萃分析不等于证明因果

Meta-analysis 用统计方法合并多项研究的结果。合并之后，结论仍受原研究设计限制。如果原研究主要比较原本就养狗和不养狗的两群人，健康差异也可能和他们的生活方式有关。于是作者把「家里有狗」与「经常遛狗」分开讨论。
