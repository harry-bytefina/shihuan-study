04-2016天津，D

成长、韧性与心理调适

Failure is probably the most exhausting experience a person ever has. There is nothing more tiring than not succeeding.

We experience this tiredness in two ways: as start-up fatigue(疲惫)and performance fatigue. In the former case, we keep putting off a task because it is either too boring or too difficult.And the longer we delay it, the more tired we feel.

Such start-up fatigue is very real, even if not actually physical, not something in our muscles and bones. The solution is obvious though perhaps not easy to apply: always handle the most difficult job first.

Years ago, I was asked to write 102 essays on the great ideas of some famous authors. Applying my own rule, I determined to write them in alphabetical order(按字母顺序), never letting myself leave out a tough idea. And I always started the day's work with the difficult task of essay-writing.Experience proved that the rule works.

Performance fatigue is more difficult to handle. Though willing to get started, we cannot seem to do the job right. Its difficulties appear so great that, however hard we work, we fail again and again. In such a situation, I work as hard as I can—then let the unconscious take over.

When planning Encyclopaedia Britannica(《大英百科全书》), I had to create a table of contents based on the topics of its articles. Nothing like this had ever been done before, and day after day I kept coming up with solutions, but none of them worked. My fatigue became almost unbearable.

One day, mentally exhausted, I wrote down all the reasons why this problem could not be solved. I tried to convince myself that the trouble was with the problem itself, not with me. Relieved, I sat back in an easy chair and fell asleep.

An hour later, I woke up suddenly with the solution clearly in mind. In the weeks that followed, the solution which had come up in my unconscious mind proved correct at every step. Though I worked as hard as before, I felt no fatigue. Success was now as exciting as failure had been depressing.

Human beings, I believe, must try to succeed. Success, then, means never feeling tired.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1People with start-up fatigue are most likely to _____ .

  • A.delay tasks
  • B.work hard
  • C.seek help
  • D.accept failure
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1.A　细节理解题。根据第二段中的描述可知,第一种情况的疲惫(start-up fatigue)会使得人们不停地把工作任务往后推(keep putting off a task)。由此可知答案为A项。

2What does the author recommend doing to prevent start-up fatigue?

  • A.Writing essays in strict order.
  • B.Building up physical strength.
  • C.Leaving out the toughest ideas.
  • D.Dealing with the hardest task first.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2.D　细节理解题。根据第三段最后一句话的always handle the most difficult job first可知防止出现这种疲惫情况的方法就是总是先处理最困难的任务,所以答案为D项。

3On what occasion does a person probably suffer from performance fatigue?

  • A.Before starting a difficult task.
  • B.When all the solutions fail.
  • C.If the job is rather boring.
  • D.After finding a way out.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3.B　细节理解题。根据第五段中的however hard we work, we fail again and again可知这种疲惫是在我们一次次努力去解决问题,但是一次次失败了的情况下发生的。所以答案为B项:当所有的解决办法都行不通的时候。

4According to the author, the unconscious mind may help us _____ .

  • A.ignore mental problems
  • B.get some nice sleep
  • C.gain complete relief
  • D.find the right solution
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

4.D　细节理解题。根据倒数第二段中的the solution which had come up in my unconscious mind proved correct at every step可知,在我无意识的情况下想出来的解决办法在解决问题中每一步都证明是正确的,所以答案为D项。

5What could be the best title for the passage?

  • A.Success Is Built upon Failure
  • B.How to Handle Performance Fatigue
  • C.Getting over Fatigue:A Way to Success
  • D.Fatigue: An Early Sign of Health Problems
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

5.C　主旨要义题。文章第一段提出:没有什么事情比不成功更让人疲惫的了;后面几段分别描述了常见的两种疲惫以及各自的解决办法;最后总结:成功就意味着永远不会感到疲惫。所以本文的主题是:消除疲惫——通往成功之路。故答案为C项。

伦敦街头的无人驾驶出租车，离“无人”还有多远？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Uber launches UK's first robotaxis with a driver - here's what it's like to ride in one

    Uber推出英国首批配备安全驾驶员的无人出租车：乘坐体验如何？

  2. 02

    Londoners are able to hail the UK's first robotaxis - as Uber today becomes the first company to launch a self-driving minicab.

    伦敦市民如今可以叫到英国首批无人出租车了——Uber当天成为首家在英国推出自动驾驶网约车的公司。

    hail
    叫车。hail a taxi 指打车，可用于打车软件下单。
  3. 03

    Initially, it only has 15 autonomous vehicles with a licence to operate in the city, and each one must have a human safety driver behind the wheel ready to take control if anything goes wrong.

    初期，Uber只有 15 辆获准在伦敦运营的自动驾驶车辆；每辆车的方向盘后都必须坐着一名人类安全驾驶员，一旦出现问题，随时准备接管。

    autonomous
    自主运行的。autonomous vehicles 指能在一定条件下自己行驶的车辆。
  4. 04

    Uber said it surveyed London-based customers via the app and more than 100,000 said they would choose a self-driving taxi if one was available, despite incidents of driverless rides malfunctioning in US cities where they already operate.

    Uber表示，它曾通过 App 调查伦敦用户；尽管自动驾驶出租车在已投入运营的美国城市发生过故障事件，仍有超过 10 万人表示，如果有机会，他们愿意选择自动驾驶出租车。

    malfunction
    发生故障；不能正常工作。这里说美国部分无人驾驶服务出现过故障。
  5. 05

    As I found out when I became the first UK journalist to hail one, it was mostly smooth but the safety driver did intervene on one occasion.

    正如我在成为英国首位叫到这类车的记者后所体验到的：整段行程大体平稳，但有一次，安全驾驶员确实进行了干预。

    intervene
    介入。这里是安全员判断需要出手而接管车辆。
  6. 06

    The safety driver gave me a 30-second safety briefing, and then we were off, making our way down the busy streets of central London.

    安全驾驶员先用 30 秒向我说明安全事项，随后我们出发，驶入伦敦市中心繁忙的街道。

    briefing
    简短说明会。safety briefing 指出发前给乘客做的安全提示。
  7. 07

    For several minutes, we negotiated the traffic without incident, successfully navigating traffic lights and roadworks along the way. When a cyclist paused and waved us forward at a mini roundabout with his arm, the car complied.

    接下来的几分钟里，车辆一路应对车流，并无异常，顺利通过了红绿灯和施工路段。在一处小型环岛，一名骑行者停下来，用手臂示意我们先行，车辆也照着做了。

    comply
    照要求做。这里是骑车人招手示意，车辆顺着他的示意前进。
    negotiate
    设法通过难走的地方。negotiate the traffic 指在车流中顺利通过。
  8. 08

    But at one point the safety driver did have to take control of the car. Just as we were pulling off, the back doors of a van parked beside us on a narrow side road appeared to swing open. The car braked sharply, an internal alarm beeped, and the driver took the wheel.

    但有一个时刻，安全驾驶员确实不得不接管车辆。就在我们正要驶离时，狭窄支路旁一辆停着的厢式货车后门似乎突然向外打开。车辆猛地刹车，车内警报器发出蜂鸣，安全驾驶员随即握住了方向盘。

  9. 09

    The drama was all over in a couple of seconds - but it was perhaps the perfect illustration that while the industry may be in a rush, the UK's regulators are not.

    这场小小的惊险在几秒内就结束了；但它或许再恰当不过地说明：行业可以急于向前推进，英国的监管机构却并不急于放行。

    regulator
    监管机构或监管者。末句说行业赶着推进，英国监管部门没有急着放开。
