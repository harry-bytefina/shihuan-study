02-2024天津夏，C

生态、气候与城市自然

Research sometimes proves, with data, what we more or less already know (e.g. Exercise is good for you, and polluted air isn't). Still, sometimes our assumptions are incorrect, and scientific findings surprise researchers, along with the rest of us. A recent example is the phenomenon of disappearing lakes in parts of the frozen treeless Arctic (北极的) region.

You might think these lakes would be expanding, not disappearing. As climate change warms the region—melting (融化) surface snow and ice as well as the permafrost (永冻层)—there should be more surface water, forming larger lakes and even new ones. Recently, however, scientists have observed not just shrinking (缩小的) lakes but lakes that have completely gone away.

Many scientists shared the commonsense expectation that as permafrost melted, lakes would at first expand with meltwater flowing into them. Eventually, researchers made a prediction that progressive warming during the 21st and 22nd centuries would dry out the Arctic, and lakes would begin to shrink. But now it looks as if Arctic lakes are disappearing a century sooner than predicted. A case in point is that some large, age-old lakes shrank dramatically in what appears to have been a matter of months. Scientists have labeled this phenomenon “disastrous lake loss”.

What caused this? The frozen solid ground actually contains bits of rock, mineral and organic matter, leaving spaces in between. And because these spaces are filled with solid ice, liquid water cannot readily get inside, but it can when the permafrost melts, allowing more water to get through. Soon after that, around 192 lakes in northwestern Alaska are gone from sight.

Why did scientists miss this? One probable reason is that most climate models assume that the melting of permafrost is driven only by warming air. New evidence suggests, however, that rainfall—particularly increasing autumn rain—is now contributing significantly to permafrost loss. The rainfall carries heat into the ground. Yet none of the existing models includes such processes.

That is a good example of why—no matter how complex our models are or may one day be—we need direct observation of the natural world.

1What does Paragraph 1 mainly tell us?

  • A.General knowledge can at times turn out to be true.
  • B.Commonsense assumptions can sometimes prove wrong.
  • C.Research findings should be built on popular assumptions.
  • D.Scientific research is inspired by an unknown phenomenon.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1. B　主旨要义题。首句指出研究有时能用数据证明我们或多或少已经知道的事,如锻炼有益、被污染的空气有害等。接着通过Still一词进行转折,强调有时我们的假设是不正确的(our assumptions are incorrect),科学发现会让研究人员和我们其他人都感到惊讶,并提出一个最近的例子是北极地区湖泊消失这一现象。所以,第一段重点强调的是常识性假设有时可能是错误的。故选B。

2Contrary to the general expectation, climate change has caused _____ .

  • A.a rapid rise of water surface
  • B.the permafrost to melt faster
  • C.many Arctic lakes to grow larger
  • D.the disappearance of many Arctic lakes
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2. D　细节理解题。第二段提及人们通常认为随着气候变化,北极地区变暖,湖泊应该扩大。最后一句中通过however一词指出了相反的现象:科学家们观察到有些湖泊缩小,有些更是完全消失(have completely gone away)。故选D。

3The disastrous climatic impacts on the Arctic region have been _____ .

  • A.proved with emerging lakes
  • B.underestimated by scientists
  • C.well predicted by researchers
  • D.shown elsewhere in the world
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3. B　推理判断题。题干询问灾难性气候对北极地区的影响。根据第三段中的“But now it looks as if Arctic lakes are disappearing a century sooner than predicted.(但现在似乎北极湖泊消失的时间比预测的早了一个世纪)”可推断,科学家们低估了气候变化给北极带来的影响。故选B。

4Why is the frozen Arctic ground hard for water to get through?

  • A.Because of the solid ice within it.
  • B.Because of its unique composition.
  • C.Because it is far away from the waters.
  • D.Because snow rarely melts on frozen ground.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

4. A　细节理解题。根据第四段中的“And because these spaces are filled with solid ice,liquid water cannot readily get inside”可知,因为冻土层中的空间里充满了坚实的冰,导致液体水无法进入。故选A。

5What lesson can we learn from the passage?

  • A.Direct observation is a must for scientific research.
  • B.Scientific research is guaranteed by sufficient lab facilities.
  • C.New evidence should be found to back up research findings.
  • D.Scientific predictions should be mostly based on research models.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

5. A　主旨要义题。文章最后一段是对前文的总结,强调了在科学研究中,不能仅仅依赖复杂的模型,还需要对自然界进行直接观察(direct observation of the natural world)。故选A。

二手礼物，怎么就成了美国送礼新潮流？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    More Shoppers Are Buying Secondhand Gifts This Holiday Season

    这个节日季，越来越多消费者选择购买二手礼物

  2. 02

    This year, some of Santa's gifts have been given before. More consumers are buying used jewelry, toy cars and other gifts than in previous years, risking the disappointment of extended family and friends to keep their budgets in check because of inflation and other financial pressures.

    今年，圣诞老人送出的部分礼物其实并非全新。相比往年，今年有更多消费者购买二手珠宝、玩具车和其他礼物，哪怕可能让一些亲戚朋友感到失望，也要在通胀与其他财务压力下把预算控制住。

    inflation
    通货膨胀；总体物价上涨。这里是家庭买礼物时控制开支的一个原因。

    句子结构

    More consumers are buying used jewelry, toy cars and other gifts than in previous years, / risking the disappointment of extended family and friends / to keep their budgets in check because of inflation and other financial pressures.

    More … than in previous years 比的是买二手礼物的人数。risking 的逻辑主语仍是消费者，说明他们承担什么风险。to keep 表目的：为了控制预算。because of 后接名词短语，解释为什么要控制预算。

  3. 03

    The shift is lifting ThredUp, Savers Value Village and other thrift retailers, which usually see sales slow during the holidays and have been taking steps to reduce the stigma around used items.

    这一变化为ThredUp、Savers Value Village等二手商品零售商的业绩带来了提振，以往这类商家在节假日期间的销量通常会下滑。如今它们也在采取各种措施，努力减少人们对二手商品的偏见。

    stigma
    社会偏见带来的负面标签。这里指有人认为送二手礼物不体面。
    thrift
    节俭；买卖二手货的语境里，thrift retailers 指二手零售商。
  4. 04

    Some 82% of roughly 1,800 U.S. shoppers surveyed by eBay said they are more likely to purchase secondhand gifts this year compared with last year.

    易贝（eBay）针对约1800名美国消费者开展了一项调研，约82%的受访者表示，与去年相比，今年自己更倾向于购买二手礼品。

  5. 05

    For years, most American consumers shunned giving secondhand gifts. Family and friends considered getting used items an insult.

    多年来，大多数美国消费者都不愿赠送二手礼物。在亲友眼中，收到二手物品是一种侮辱。

    shun
    刻意回避。这里指很多消费者以前不愿意送旧物当礼物。
  6. 06

    Yet thrifting has been growing in popularity, even beyond the increasing numbers who value the treasure hunt and want to help the environment by keeping clothes and other items out of landfills. Items that cost $40 new can go for $5 used, consumers said.

    然而，淘二手货的热度正持续攀升，青睐这一消费方式的群体，早已不局限于那些热衷于淘二手好物、希望通过减少衣物及其他物品的垃圾填埋量来保护环境的人。消费者表示，一件售价40美元的全新商品，二手价可能只要5美元。

    landfill
    垃圾填埋场。把旧物继续用下去，就能少一些东西被当成垃圾扔掉。
  7. 07

    To avert any awkwardness that could come with gifting other people's castoffs, Elizabeth Rooney tries to address stereotypes friends might have about the items.

    为了避免赠送“他人弃置物品”可能带来的尴尬，伊丽莎白·鲁尼（Elizabeth Rooney）会设法打破朋友们对二手礼物可能存在的刻板印象。

    avert
    防止不好的事发生。avert awkwardness 指事先避免送礼时的尴尬。
    stereotype
    先入为主的看法。这里指朋友可能没看东西就觉得二手货不干净。
    castoff
    别人不再要的旧物。和常见的 secondhand 相比，这个词更强调东西被原主人舍弃。
  8. 08

    "You walk them through, like, how you clean the item you're giving," said Rooney, 27, a content creator in Albany, N.Y. "I think that takes away a lot of the dirtiness of it because, you know, you're wiping it down for them, you're cleaning it before you give them something."

    “我会详细告诉他们，自己是怎么清洁这件礼物的。”27岁的鲁尼是纽约州奥尔巴尼市的一名内容创作者，她解释道，“我觉得这样能打消大家对二手物品‘不干净’的顾虑，毕竟你已经替他们把东西擦拭干净，在送出礼物前做好了全面清洁。”
