阅读理解

07-2018课标全国Ⅱ，C

学习、阅读与教育方式

Teens and younger children are reading a lot less for fun, according to a Common Sense Media report published Monday.

While the decline over the past decade is steep for teen readers, some data in the report shows that reading remains a big part of many children's lives, and indicates how parents might help encourage more reading.

According to the report's key findings, “the proportion(比例)who say they ‘hardly ever’read for fun has gone from 8 percent of 13⁃year⁃olds and 9 percent of 17⁃year⁃olds in 1984 to 22 percent and 27 percent respectively today.”

The report data shows that pleasure reading levels for younger children, ages 2—8, remain largely the same. But the amount of time spent in reading each session has declined, from closer to an hour or more to closer to a half hour per session.

When it comes to technology and reading, the report does little to counsel(建议) parents looking for data about the effect of e⁃readers and tablets on reading. It does point out that many parents still limit electronic reading, mainly due to concerns about increased screen time.

The most hopeful data shared in the report shows clear evidence of parents serving as examples and important guides for their kids when it comes to reading. Data shows that kids and teens who do read frequently, compared to infrequent readers, have more books in the home, more books purchased for them, parents who read more often, and parents who set aside time for them to read.

As the end of school approaches, and school vacation reading lists loom(逼近) ahead, parents might take this chance to step in and make their own summer reading list and plan a family trip to the library or bookstore.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What is the Common Sense Media report probably about?

  • A.Children's reading habits.
  • B.Quality of children's books.
  • C.Children's after⁃class activities.
  • D.Parent⁃child relationships.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1.A　主旨要义题。本文为说明文,第一段为本文的主旨段。根据第一段(根据周一出版的“常识媒体”的一个报告,十几岁的孩子和更小的孩子进行趣味阅读的量少多了。)可知该报告主要讲的是孩子们的阅读习惯的变化,故答案为A项。

2Where can you find the data that best supports “children are reading a lot less for fun”?

  • A.In paragraph 2.
  • B.In paragraph 3.
  • C.In paragraph 4.
  • D.In paragraph 5.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

2.B　细节理解题。本文第一段提出了本文主题,即孩子们进行趣味阅读的量越来越少。然后在第三段通过数据(...has gone from 8 percent of 13⁃year⁃olds and 9 percent of 17⁃year⁃olds in 1984 to 22 percent and 27 percent respectively today.)来支撑这一说法,故答案为B项。

3Why do many parents limit electronic reading?

  • A.E⁃books are of poor quality.
  • B.It could be a waste of time.
  • C.It may harm children's health.
  • D.E⁃readers are expensive.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

3.C　细节理解题。根据第五段最后一句(……许多家长仍然限制电子阅读,主要是因为他们担心那会增加孩子面对屏幕的时间。)可知家长限制电子阅读的原因是担心孩子们的健康,故答案为C项。

4How should parents encourage their children to read more?

  • A.Act as role models for them.
  • B.Ask them to write book reports.
  • C.Set up reading groups for them.
  • D.Talk with their reading class teachers.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

4.A　细节理解题。根据第六段第一句(报告中分享的最给人以希望的数据清楚地证明了家长给孩子们做出榜样并在阅读上进行重要的指导。)可知答案为A项(为他们树立榜样)。

阅读难度参考：CSE 6

有兄弟姐妹的童年，有什么不一样？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    I trained as an economist in the years when smaller families were widely treated as good news. The assumption in the field was straightforward: Fewer children meant more investment in each. Quality over quantity.

    我求学深造经济学的那段时期，少子女家庭普遍被视作一件好事。经济学界的观点十分直白：孩子越少，父母能投入在每个孩子身上的资源就越多。质量重于数量。

  2. 02

    But after years of interviewing parents of large families, I've come to think that that calculus was incomplete. Some of what shapes a child can't be supplied by parents at all, no matter how attentive or well resourced. It comes from siblings.

    但多年走访多子女家庭的父母后，我渐渐认为这套计算逻辑并不完整。有一部分塑造孩子人格的东西，无论父母多用心、家境多优渥，都无法给予，这份成长只能来自兄弟姐妹。

    attentive
    adj. 无微不至的，注意力高度集中的
  3. 03

    In many large families, everybody shares a room; small disputes have to be hashed out; older children naturally take responsibility for younger ones. "There are tons of opportunities for them to be self-sacrificing and patient and charitable," said one mother, a former litigator with six children, adding, "just endless opportunities for conflict and resolution."

    很多多子女家庭里，每个人都要和其他人共用房间，小矛盾必须自行解决，年长的孩子也会自然而然为弟弟妹妹承担起责任。一位育有六个孩子、曾是诉讼律师的母亲说道：“孩子们有无数机会学会自我牺牲、耐心与善良，也时时刻刻都在经历矛盾、学着化解矛盾。”

    litigator
    n. 诉讼律师
  4. 04

    Parents in small families have to manufacture, through summer programs and sports leagues and carefully cultivated extracurriculars, the kind of character-forming experiences that large households once provided gratis.

    独生子女或小家庭的的父母只能靠夏令营、体育社团、精心安排的各类课外班，人为创造那些多子女家庭天然就能免费获得的品格历练。

    manufacture
    v. 批量制造，生产
    gratis
    adv. 免费地（for free）
  5. 05

    We hear constantly that Americans are more atomized, more polarized, more isolated than ever. The problem isn't freedom. It's that we have lost the primary institution where virtues are formed—the household in which children form one another. Quantity is a quality of its own when it comes to human beings.

    我们总听闻，如今美国人比以往任何时候都更原子化、更两极分化、更孤立。问题不在于自由，而是我们弄丢了培育美德的首要场所 —— 兄弟姐妹互相塑造的大家庭。谈到人类时，数量本身就是一种质量。

    atomized
    adj. 原子化的
    isolated
    adj. 被孤立的
  6. 06

    This wisdom explains, too, why modern parenting has become paradoxically harder. Siblings were doing some of the work. And in our haste to make our children's lives better, we accidentally took credit for a value they alone provided.

    这个道理也能解释一个矛盾现象：为什么如今养育孩子反而变得更难。过去，兄弟姐妹本会分担一部分成长教育的作用；而我们一心想给孩子更好的生活，却无意间忽略了这份只有手足才能带来的独特价值，把所有成长重担都揽在了自己身上。

    haste
    n. 仓促，急忙
    paradoxically
    adv. 自相矛盾地，似是而非地
