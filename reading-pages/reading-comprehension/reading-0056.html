学习、阅读与教育方式

When you choose a higher education course, here are some tips for you to follow.

Choosing your course

There are a range of subjects and courses available. Many are work-related; others are academic. You'll need to look beyond the course title because courses with almost the same name may differ enormously. For example, if you're interested in construction but wish to use your creative skills, you may be better suited to a building design management course than a building project management course.

Style of learning

Think about the style of learning that best suits you. It's important to study a subject that you particularly enjoy, but if you want to follow a career in a different area, you may consider taking the subjects you want to learn and joining them into one degree. Many degree courses are made up of different subject modules which deal with particular areas of interest.

Choosing where to study

Where you study can be almost as important as what you study. One thing to consider is whether you want to live at home or move away. For some people, living at home offers the best of both worlds, but it may make sense to move if you want to study a particular course at a specific university or college. Many students value the experience of living on campus or in a different part of the country as an important part of university life.

Finding out more about courses

Once you've got a list of courses that appeal to you, it's worth looking at some other sources of information about them, like independent reports. These provide information on the numbers of students who finished the course, student satisfaction and more.

Open days

Open days usually take place in spring or autumn. Although you may have an idea about what a course is like, there's no substitute（替代）for attending a class yourself. If you can't come on the open days, you can sign up for a guided tour instead.

成功人士都有一个共同习惯，为什么很多人却羞于去做？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    I Study Successful People. They Have One Habit in Common.

    我研究了成功人士：他们都有一个共同习惯

  2. 02

    I was once stranded with my 86‑year‑old mother in the Atlantic Ocean. What saved my mother’s life that day, and mine, too, was not fighting the current on my own. It was calling for help.

    我曾和 86 岁的母亲一起被困在大西洋上。那天，救了我母亲、也救了我一命的，不是我独自与海流搏斗，而是开口呼救。

    current
    n. 水流，洋流
    stranded
    adj. 受困的，滞留的
  3. 03

    Most people understand the importance of asking for help in a life‑or‑death crisis. But in our personal and professional lives, Americans are encouraged to pursue rugged individualism over interdependency.

    大多数人都明白，在生死攸关的危机中，求助非常重要。但在个人生活和职业生活中，美国人却被鼓励去追求彪悍的个人主义，而不是相互依存。

    interdependency
    n. 相互依存
    rugged
    adj. 形容人粗犷坚韧的
  4. 04

    Without exception, every high performer I’ve studied credits a host of supporters for success. Research has found that a willingness to ask for help is crucial to achievement.

    无一例外，我研究过的每一位高成就者，都把自己的成功归功于许多支持过他们的人。研究发现，愿意开口求助，对取得成就至关重要。

  5. 05

    Knowing when to ask for help is an essential skill for children’s learning and achievement. Research also shows that entrepreneurs are more successful when brought together to learn from one another. “Even if you could do all the work yourself, you need colleagues to brainstorm with, to talk you out of stupid decisions and to cheer you up when things go wrong,” Paul Graham, one of Y Combinator’s founders, once wrote.

    知道何时求助，是儿童学习和取得成就的一项重要能力。研究还表明，创业者聚在一起相互学习时，会更加成功。Y Combinator 的联合创始人之一保罗·格雷厄姆（Paul Graham）曾写道：“即使你能独自完成所有工作，你也需要能与你一起头脑风暴的同事；需要有人劝你别做蠢决定；也需要有人在事情出错时为你打气。”

    essential
    adj. 不可缺少的
    brainstorm
    v. 头脑风暴，碰撞想法
  6. 06

    From an early age, we suffer from what cognitive psychologists call the illusion of self‑sufficiency, overlooking how much we owe our skills to other people. Don’t assume you’re being a burden when you reach out asking for help. Research shows we consistently underestimate how happy it makes others to lend a hand.

    从很小的时候起，我们就受困于认知心理学家所说的“自给自足的错觉”：忽视了自己的能力在多大程度上得益于他人。当你主动联系别人、请求帮助时，不要以为自己是在给人添麻烦。研究表明，我们总是低估：伸出援手，会给别人带来多少快乐。

    illusion
    n. 幻觉，错觉
    self-sufficiency
    n. 自给自足，无须外援
  7. 07

    “Do it yourself” is no way to live. Take it from my mom, who, five years after a cry for help saved her life, recently celebrated her 91st birthday.

    “自己搞定一切”并不是一种可取的生活方式。听听我母亲的亲身经历吧：五年前，一声呼救挽救了她的生命；而就在不久前，她庆祝了自己的 91 岁生日。
