02-2015课标全国Ⅰ，B

食物、农业与地方身份

The freezing Northeast hasn't been a terribly fun place to spend time this winter,so when the chance came for a weekend to Sarasota,Florida,my bags were packed before you could say“sunshine”.I left for the land of warmth and vitamin C(维生素C),thinking of beaches and orange trees.When we touched down to blue skies and warm air,I sent up a small prayer of gratefulness.Swimming pools,wine tasting,and pink sunsets(at normal evening hours,not 4 in the afternoon)filled the weekend,but the best part—particularly to my taste,dulled by months of cold-weather root vegetables—was a 7 a.m.adventure to the Sarasota farmers' market that proved to be more than worth the early wake-up call.

The market,which was founded in 1979,sets up its tents every Saturday from 7 a.m.to 1 p.m.,rain or shine,along North Lemon and State streets.Baskets of perfect red strawberries;the red-painted sides of the Java Dawg coffee truck;and most of all,the tomatoes:amazing,large,soft and round red tomatoes.

Disappointed by many a broken,vine-ripened(蔓上成熟的)promise,I've refused to buy winter tomatoes for years.No matter how attractive they look in the store,once I get them home they're unfailingly dry,hard,and tasteless.But I homed in,with uncertainty,on one particular table at the Brown's Grove Farm's stand,full of fresh and soft tomatoes the size of my fist.These were the real deal—and at that moment,I realized that the best part of Sarasota in winter was going to be eating things that back home in New York I wouldn't be experiencing again for months.

Delighted as I was by the tomatoes in sight,my happiness deepened when I learned that Brown's Grove Farm is one of the suppliers for Jack Dusty,a newly opened restaurant at the Sarasota Ritz Carlton,where—luckily for me—I was planning to have dinner that very night.Without even seeing the menu,I knew I'd be ordering every tomato on it.

1What did the author think of her winter life in New York?

  • A.Exciting.
  • B.Boring.
  • C.Relaxing.
  • D.Annoying.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1.B　推理判断题。根据第一段第一句The freezing Northeast hasn't been a terribly fun place to spend time this winter...可以看出作者认为寒冷的东北地区是一个无趣的地方,故B项正确。

2What made the author's getting up early worthwhile?

  • A.Having a swim.
  • B.Breathing in fresh air.
  • C.Walking in the morning sun.
  • D.Visiting a local farmers' market.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2.D　细节理解题。根据第一段最后一句...was a 7 a.m.adventure to the Sarasota farmers' market that proved to be more than worth the early wake-up call.可知,作者认为Sarasota农场之行使得早起非常值得。

3What can we learn about tomatoes sold in New York in winter?

  • A.They are soft.
  • B.They look nice.
  • C.They taste great.
  • D.They are juicy.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3.B　细节理解题。根据第三段第二句No matter how attractive they look in the store...可以看出纽约冬天出售的西红柿看起来很吸引人,故B项正确。

4What was the author going to do that evening?

  • A.Go to a farm.
  • B.Check into a hotel.
  • C.Eat in a restaurant.
  • D.Buy fresh vegetables.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

4.C　细节理解题。根据最后一段中的...a newly opened restaurant...where—luckily for me—I was planning to have dinner that very night.可知作者打算在那天晚上去饭店美餐一顿。

镜像时代：我们看自己越多，为什么反而越不自在？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    All This Staring at Our Faces Is Messing With Our Heads

    总盯着自己的面孔看，正在扰乱我们的心智

  2. 02

    Everything is strange. We socialize online. Our private lives are increasingly public. People pretend to be brands; brands pretend to be people. We optimize our bodies until they break. And the more we know, the less secure we feel.

    一切都变得怪异。我们在网上社交，私人生活却日益公开化。个人把自己包装成品牌，品牌却要打造自己的“人”感。我们不断优化自己的身体，直至把身体搞垮。我们知道得越来越多，内心反而越来越没有安全感。

  3. 03

    To know the truth of ourselves, we begin at what feels like a natural place: the mirror. We are constantly confronted with our own image: on our phones, front-facing videos, public spaces, our social media profiles, Zoom calls. But we can forget that mirrors both duplicate reality and reverse it. None show you exactly as you are.

    为了认清真实的自我，我们会从一个看似理所当然的地方着手：镜子。我们不断看见自己的影像：手机屏幕、前置摄像头录制的视频、公共场合、社交媒体个人主页、线上视频会议。但我们有时会忘记，镜子既复刻现实，也会颠倒现实。没有一面镜子能呈现你真实的模样。

    duplicate
    v. 复制
    profile
    n.（社交媒体上）个人资料；简介
  4. 04

    The historian Christopher Lasch wrote that “for the narcissist, the world is a mirror.” Almost 50 years later, this remains a tidy diagnosis, but it doesn’t quite capture the extent of the spiritual crisis that we’re now facing.

    历史学家克里斯托弗・拉斯奇（Christopher Lasch）曾写道：“对自恋者而言，整个世界就是一面镜子。” 近五十年过去，这依旧是一个精辟的论断，但它已然没法涵盖我们当下面临的这场精神危机的严重程度。

  5. 05

    I believe the room of mirrors that constantly surrounds us conjures something unsettling, something dark. It is a spiritual trap in which the imagined displaces the real: We choose a fantasy of ourselves over the reality of others. And that trap is baited and facilitated by technology.

    我认为时刻将我们包围的镜像空间，会催生出某种令人不安、阴郁的东西。这是一处精神陷阱：想象取代了现实，我们宁愿沉溺于自我幻想，也不愿接纳他人的真实。而科技为这个陷阱设置诱饵，并推波助澜。

    displace
    v. 取代
    bait
    v. 布置诱饵
    facilitate
    v. 使便利，促进
  6. 06

    As more and more of our lives take place in a virtual realm, and we increasingly identify with the fantasies of our digital doppelgängers, we begin to dehumanize ourselves, and when we do this, it is undoubtedly easier to dehumanize others.

    随着我们越来越多地活在虚拟世界里，我们会愈发认同自己数字化“分身”所承载的幻想，我们开始剥离自身的人性；一旦如此，我们无疑也更容易去漠视他人的人性。

    realm
    n. 领域，王国
    virtual
    adj. 虚拟的
  7. 07

    When we look solely at ourselves, we fail to encounter anything that resists us; there are no contradictions, no interruptions. We need the honest gaze: the gaze of others and the gaze toward others. To see ourselves clearly, we don’t use mirrors. We use relationships.

    当我们只盯着自身，就遇不到任何与自己相悖的事物；没有矛盾，也没有来自外界的打断。我们需要真诚的注视：来自他人的注视，以及投向他人的注视。想要看清自我，依靠的不是镜子，而是人与人之间的关系。

    encounter
    遇到，碰到
  8. 08

    Mirrors encourage us to choose the fantasies of ourselves over the realities of others. It’s best we look away.

    镜子怂恿我们沉溺自我幻想，无视他人的真实。我们最好移开目光。
