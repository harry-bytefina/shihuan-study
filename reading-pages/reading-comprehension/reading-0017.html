STUDENT EMPLOYMENT ON CAMPUS

All students may apply to work on campus.

Getting started

Before starting any position, all students need to complete required tax forms and show identification. International students also need:

*Social Security card　　　*Bank account

*Valid passport　　　*Official work permit

*Letter of support from the office of International Student Life

Looking for a job on Handshake

Handshake is an online job search platform for college students. With a Handshake account, students can receive information about career events and personalized job recommendations. To use Handshake, just follow these steps:

*Log in to Handshake using your email address already provided by the college.

*Complete personalization of your profile.

*Start your job search by clicking “Jobs” button.

*Fill out desired information: location, job type, etc.

*Apply directly by clicking “Apply Now” button.

You can also make appointments to meet with a career coach, access career resources, and explore careers that interest you.

On-campus jobs available for students

*Academic department assistant (Flexible working hours on Monday/Thursday)

*Dining services (Lunchtime on weekdays)

*Library assistant (Flexible working hours on Monday/Wednesday/Friday)

*Bookstore/mailroom (9:00-12:00 at weekends)

*Athletic department (18:00-22:00 on weekdays)

Maximum working hours

*During the semester(学期), domestic students can work a maximum of 10 hours a week; international students can work a maximum of 20 hours a week.

*During the summer break, a student may not work more than 30 hours a week.

*The total number of hours may not exceed(超过) the maximum hours allowed even if a student has more than one job.

For more information, find us at Career Development Office, 211 Hughes Hall; careerdevelopment@dreamcollege.edu, 555-520-1314.

阅读难度参考：CSE 6

交出你的糗事：过度分享，其实是建立深度关系的起点

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    I Thought Oversharing Was Career Suicide. Then I Tried It.

    我曾以为过度分享会毁掉职业前程。后来我试了一次。

    overshare
    v. 过度分享
  2. 02

    For most of my early career, I believed respect came from being polished, discreet and tightly self-edited, if a little dull. Credibility, I assumed, was built by projecting competence and composure, not by exposing flaws.

    在职业生涯早期的多数时候，我一直认为，想要获得他人尊重，就要处事得体、谨慎低调、严格约束自己，哪怕显得有些无趣。我曾经以为，个人信誉来自于展现专业和沉稳，而不是暴露自己的缺点。

    discreet
    adj. 谨慎的，谨言慎行的
    flaw
    n. 缺点，人身上的缺陷和不完美之处
  3. 03

    Which is why what happened next still surprises me.

    正因如此，接下来发生的事，至今让我感到意外。

  4. 04

    While attending an academic conference that included senior scholars, someone suggested we go around the circle and share our most embarrassing story.

    在参加一场有资深学者出席的学术会议上，有人提议大家轮流分享自己最尴尬的故事。

  5. 05

    A few fellow junior academics went first. Their stories were safe and strategic—humblebrags masquerading as embarrassment.

    几位年轻学者率先分享，他们的故事都很稳妥、很有分寸，看似自我调侃，实则是变相炫耀。

    humblebrag
    n. 低调的显摆
  6. 06

    Then it was my turn. Instead of choosing something sanitized, I blurted out my actual most embarrassing moment.

    轮到我时，我没有挑选那些经过美化的事情，而是直接说出了自己最真实、最尴尬的经历。

  7. 07

    Back in college, I’d been cast in a dark, satirical German play. In one scene, my character gets spectacularly drunk. I threw myself into the role.

    大学时候，我参演过一部德国黑色讽刺剧，在其中一幕，我扮演的角色需要表现得酩酊大醉。我当时非常投入地演绎这个角色。

  8. 08

    The audience roared. I started laughing too—so hard that I peed myself. Onstage. In front of 500 people.

    观众哄堂大笑，我也跟着大笑，笑太猛了以至于，当场失禁，就在舞台上，当着五百名观众的面。

  9. 09

    After telling my story at the conference, the group erupted in laughter.

    我在会上讲完这件事后，在场的人全都爆笑。

  10. 10

    I laughed too—until a wave of dread set in. The next morning, I replayed the moment on a loop, convinced I’d committed irreparable reputational damage.

    我一开始也跟着笑，但很快就开始满心焦虑。第二天早上，我反复回想这件事，觉得自己的名声彻底毁了，再也无法挽回。

  11. 11

    One of the senior scholars went on to become a mentor and collaborator. Another senior scholar also began inviting me into rooms and conversations I had no idea existed.

    其中一位资深学者后来成了我的导师和合作伙伴，另一位资深学者也开始带我进入我从前接触不到的圈子和交流场合。

    mentor
    n. 导师
  12. 12

    I now understand this didn’t happen despite my embarrassing disclosure; it happened because of it.

    我现在才明白，并不是“居然在我尴尬的坦诚分享之后，还能有这些机遇”，而是“恰恰因为我的坦诚分享，才有了这些机遇”。

    disclosure
    n. 袒露，暴露
  13. 13

    Oftentimes, when we cringe after an overshare, the other person is grateful to have been trusted. Undersharing, on the other hand, quietly costs us: friendships that never deepen, colleagues who never quite trust us, romances that never spark.

    很多时候，我们会因为说得太多、暴露自我而尴尬，但对方反而会感激我们的信任。相反，刻意隐瞒、不愿交心，会让我们默默付出代价：友情无法深入、同事心存隔阂、感情没有火花。

    undershare
    v. 刻意隐瞒，刻意少分享

    句子结构

    Undersharing, on the other hand, quietly costs us: / friendships that never deepen, / colleagues who never quite trust us, / romances that never spark.

    冒号后列出三个名词短语，解释 costs us 所说的代价。that never deepen 修饰 friendships，who … 修饰 colleagues，最后的 that 修饰 romances。这些从句各管前面的一个名词，不是三个独立分句。

  14. 14

    Oversharing isn’t always wise, but in the right contexts, revealing something real about yourself can unlock a deeper level of trust. Among people who already have status, polish is cheap. Vulnerability is informative.

    过度分享并不总是明智的，但在合适的场景中，展现真实的自己，能收获更深的信任。在真正有地位、有阅历的人眼中，刻意粉饰很廉价，敢于展露脆弱反而更能提供信息、更有价值。

    informative
    adj. 提供了有效信息的，有价值的
  15. 15

    I’d always thought my credibility came from control. That night, I learned it sometimes comes from letting go.

    我从前一直以为，个人的公信力来自于自我掌控。那晚之后我明白了，有时候，放下防备，才能真正建立信任。
