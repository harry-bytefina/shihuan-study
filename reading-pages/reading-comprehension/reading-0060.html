阅读理解

01-2017天津夏，A

学习、阅读与教育方式

Suppose you're in a rush, feeling tired, not paying attention to your screen, and you send an email that could get you in trouble.

Realisation will probably set in seconds after you've clicked “send”. You freeze in horror and burn with shame.

What to do?Here are four common email accidents, and how to recover.

Clicking “send”too soon

Don't waste your time trying to find out if the receiver has read it yet. Write another email as swiftly as you can and send it with a brief title explaining that this is the correct version and the previous version should be ignored.

Writing the wrong name

The sooner you notice, the better. Respond quickly and briefly, apologising for your mistake. Keep the tone measured:don't handle it too lightly, as people can be offended, especially if your error suggests a misunderstanding of their culture(i.e. incorrect ordering of Chinese names).

Clicking “reply all”unintentionally

You accidentally reveal(透露)to the entire company what menu choices you would prefer at the staff Christmas dinner, or what holiday you'd like to take. In this instance, the best solution is to send a quick, light-hearted apology to explain your awkwardness. But it can quickly rise to something worse, when everyone starts hitting “reply all” to join in a long and unpleasant conversation. In this instance, step away from your keyboard to allow everyone to calm down.

Sending an offensive message to its subject

The most awkward email mistake is usually committed in anger. You write an unkind message about someone, intending to send it to a friend, but accidentally send it to the person you're discussing. In that case, ask to speak in person as soon as possible and say sorry. Explain your frustrations calmly and sensibly—see it as an opportunity to clear up any difficulties you may have with this person.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1After realising an email accident, you are likely to feel _____ .

  • A.curious
  • B.tired
  • C.awful
  • D.funny
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

1.C　细节理解题。根据第二段中的 “You freeze in horror and burn with shame.”可知,在你意识到发错了邮件时,你会惊恐地愣住,羞得脸颊发烫。由此可知你会感觉很糟糕。

2If you have written the wrong name in an email, it is best to _____ .

  • A.apologise in a serious manner
  • B.tell the receiver to ignore the error
  • C.learn to write the name correctly
  • D.send a short notice to everyone
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

2.A　推理判断题。根据Writing the wrong name部分中的“Respond quickly and briefly, apologising for your mistake. Keep the tone measured:don't handle it too lightly”可以推断出,在写错名字时,要立即道歉,并且说话的语调要有分寸,不能太轻率,也就是你要严肃认真地道歉,所以答案为A项。

3What should you do when an unpleasant conversation is started by your “reply all”email?

  • A.Try offering other choices.
  • B.Avoid further involvement.
  • C.Meet other staff members.
  • D.Make a light-hearted apology.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3.B　推理判断题。根据Clicking “reply all” unintentionally部分中的 “In this instance, step away from your keyboard to allow everyone to calm down.”可以推断出,出现这种情况时,你要离开键盘,让每个人都冷静下来,也就是不要继续参与讨论,所以答案为B项。

4How should you deal with the problem caused by an offensive email?

  • A.By promising not to offend the receiver again.
  • B.By seeking support from the receiver's friends.
  • C.By asking the receiver to control his anger.
  • D.By talking to the receiver face to face.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

4.D　细节理解题。根据最后一段中的 “In that case, ask to speak in person as soon as possible and say sorry.”可知,出现这种情况时,要亲自向收件人道歉,所以答案为D项。

5What is the passage mainly about?

  • A.Defining email errors.
  • B.Reducing email mistakes.
  • C.Handling email accidents.
  • D.Improving email writing.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

5.C　主旨要义题。根据第三段 “What to do? Here are four common email accidents, and how to recover.”可知,本文的主题就是当发错邮件时你应该如何补救。本文列举了四种常见的错误以及相应的补救措施,所以答案为C项。

  1. 01

    Discount Caviar Is Everywhere—and the Luxury Food Is Having an Identity Crisis

    平价鱼子酱随处可见 —— 这种顶奢美食正遭遇身份危机

    caviar
    n. 鱼子酱（由鲟鱼鱼卵腌渍而成的美食）
  2. 02

    Promo codes. Free shipping. Secret Santa value set.

    优惠券、免运费、圣诞惊喜超值礼盒。

  3. 03

    These are not phrases one typically associates with caviar. But they feature in the marketing for a cluster of trendy new direct‑to‑consumer (DTC) brands that offer cutely branded tins at prices so approachable they would appall an oligarch.

    这些词通常不会和鱼子酱联系在一起。但一批新潮的直面消费者（DTC）品牌的营销文案里，满是这类话术；它们出售包装精致的鱼子酱小罐，价格亲民到会让寡头富豪咋舌。

    appall
    v. 使震惊
    oligarch
    n. 寡头；极度有钱有势的权贵
    approachable
    adj. 价格亲民的
  4. 04

    For bang‑for‑buck, few can compete with Quince, the San Francisco purveyor of eyewateringly cheap cashmere. Its Osetra caviar, which has racked up a 4.9 rating from more than 1,500 reviews, costs $125 for a 100g tin.

    要说性价比，很少有品牌能比得上昆斯（Quince），这家旧金山品牌以价格低到惊人的羊绒制品闻名。它的奥西特拉鱼子酱（Osetra caviar）收获 1500 多条评价，评分高达 4.9 分，100 克装一罐售价 125 美元。

    cashmere
    n. 羊绒衫，羊绒
  5. 05

    Recent trends pairing caviar with chicken nuggets and hot dogs had already created something of an identity crisis for the snooty food.

    近来流行把鱼子酱搭配鸡块、热狗一起食用，这已然让这份曾经高高在上的美食陷入了某种身份危机。

    snooty
    adj. 高傲自大、自视高人一等的
  6. 06

    Caviar is the name for salted roe (or unfertilized egg) sourced from sturgeon, a large fish that dates back to the dinosaurs. It has remained expensive relative to other foods, partly because it can take a sturgeon many years to produce eggs. Over the last decade, fish farmed in China have come to maturity.

    鱼子酱指的是鲟鱼产出的腌制鱼籽（未受精的鱼卵）。鲟鱼是一种早在恐龙时代就存在的大型鱼类。它的价格一直高于其他食材，部分原因是鲟鱼需要生长很多年才能产出鱼卵。过去十年间，中国养殖的鲟鱼陆续成熟。

  7. 07

    “There’s so much Chinese caviar available right now, and a lot of it is very cheap and a lot of it is very good,” said Gabriella Gershenson, author of the forthcoming book “Caviar (& Champagne).”

    即将出版《鱼子酱（与香槟）》一书的作者加布里埃拉・格森（Gabriella Gershenson）说：“现在市面上有大量中国产鱼子酱，很多价格低廉，品质也很不错。”

  8. 08

    Lately Mallory Aylesworth, a 34‑year‑old working in senior‑living sales in St. Charles, Ill., has bought several tins from Quince. Their price and size mean she and her husband can indulge a little more freely. Aylesworth has even given her darling two‑year‑old an occasional mouthful.

    34 岁的马洛里・艾尔斯沃思（Mallory Aylesworth）在伊利诺伊州圣查尔斯从事养老行业销售工作，最近她已经从昆斯买了好几罐鱼子酱。实惠的价格和合适的分量，让她和丈夫可以更随性地享受这份美味。艾尔斯沃思甚至偶尔喂她可爱的两岁小宝宝尝上一口。

    indulge
    v. 放纵，沉溺于
    mouthful
    n. 满口，一口
