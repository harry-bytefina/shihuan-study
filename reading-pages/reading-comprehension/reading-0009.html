阅读理解

01-2024天津夏，B

学习、阅读与教育方式

Our teacher, Miss Chevalier was a small woman, with a moon face, fatty fingers and curls that sprang straight up from her head, hence the funny name “Poodle (卷毛狗)”. She taught in our reading club. Sometimes she would look in and ask what we were reading—not to check but to know.

That's what happened the day my club was reading aloud a poem by Henry Longfellow. I guess I was better than the others, for Miss Chevalier asked a while later, “Addie, I was wondering if you would recite the whole poem to the Saturday evening's club.” She said a famous professor was going to give a lecture about the poet, and a presentation of that poem would be a fresh way to start.

She asked me to memorize it. “But that shouldn't be a problem for a child of your ability,” she added. I'm telling you, my happy feet barely touched the ground all the way home. To me, that was the biggest thing ever and I soon learned the whole poem by heart, well prepared for our first rehearsal.

I was desperately nervous when I went to her office the next day. But the good thing was she always had the smile that would make you feel like you just did something right.

Halfway through the poem, Miss Chevalier stopped me and asked if I knew what “impetuous” meant. Despite her encouraging smile, I wanted to sink through the floor because I was unsure about its pronunciation as well as its meaning. Miss Chevalier pretended not to have noticed my red face and handed me a dictionary, asking me to read its definition aloud. “Impetuous has two definitions: rushing with great force or violence; acting suddenly, with little thought.”

She asked me which one fitted the poem. I read both definitions over again, trying to figure it out, but Miss Chevalier must have read my mind. “There is no wrong answer,” she said, “I want to know your opinion, Addie.” Hesitantly, I said, “Maybe...both...”

She liked that. “One has to be impetuous both ways or they wouldn't dare to face up to any challenge. Would you call yourself impetuous?”

I knew she was asking for an opinion. “My mother thinks being impetuous for girls is improper, anyway.”

She said my mom was somewhat right about that. “But girls should also be wise to take up challenges when needed. I believe you are such a girl.”

After that, I would never call Miss Chevalier “Poodle” again.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1Why was Addie asked to recite Longfellow's poem to the Saturday evening's club?

  • A.To help with her performance.
  • B.To get the attention of her classmates.
  • C.To set an example for the rest of the class.
  • D.To try a new way to warm up the coming lecture.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

1. D　细节理解题。根据题干关键词Why和recite Longfellow's poem定位到第二段最后两句。根据“She said a famous professor was going to give a lecture about the poet, and a presentation of that poem would be a fresh way to start.”可知,老师希望作者以诗歌朗诵的形式为周六晚上的演讲活动来暖场。D项(尝试一种新的方法来为即将到来的讲座暖场)是作者被要求朗诵的原因。故选D。

2Why did Addie feel happy on her way home?

  • A.An event was going to happen.
  • B.She was fully ready for her first rehearsal.
  • C.She gained recognition for her performance.
  • D.She memorized the poem sooner than others.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

2. C　推理判断题。根据题干关键词feel happy on her way home定位到第三段第三句。根据老师对作者说的话“But that shouldn't be a problem for a child of your ability”可知,老师信任作者的能力,因此她感到非常高兴。C项中gained recognition for her performance表示“表现获得认可”。故选C。

3What can be learned about Miss Chevalier's personality from the way she treated Addie?

  • A.Caring and considerate.
  • B.Easygoing and carefree.
  • C.Curious and open-minded.
  • D.Disciplined and strong-willed.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

3. A　推理判断题。题干询问老师的性格特点,需综合多处信息进行推断。根据文章第四段中“the good thing was she always had the smile that would make you feel like you just did something right”和第五段中“Miss Chevalier pretended not to have noticed my red face”等描述可推断出老师对作者的关爱和耐心指导,由此推出她是关心人的(caring)和体贴的(considerate)。故选A。

4How did Miss Chevalier succeed in making Addie truly understand the word “impetuous”?

  • A.By asking Addie to define it in her own way.
  • B.By interpreting the two definitions separately.
  • C.By consulting the dictionary for its definitions.
  • D.By linking its definitions to Addie's situations.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

4. D　推理判断题。文章第五至九段是老师引导作者理解“impetuous”的过程。第五段中描述了老师让作者从字典中大声读出该单词的定义(read its definition aloud)。第六段中老师询问作者哪个定义适合这首诗。在作者犹豫地给出答案后,老师又进一步引导,通过提问“Would you call yourself impetuous?”将单词的含义与Addie的实际情况联系起来。第九段老师鼓励Addie要在需要时勇敢接受挑战(take up challenges when needed)。综上可知,老师是通过将“impetuous”的定义与 Addie 的情况联系起来,成功地让 Addie 真正理解了这个单词。故选D。

5What is the probable reason that Addie would never call Miss Chevalier “Poodle” again?

  • A.She followed her mother's advice.
  • B.She made up her mind to be a polite girl.
  • C.She was touched by Miss Chevalier's trust.
  • D.She felt Miss Chevalier's teaching funny.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

5. C　推理判断题。根据第九段中的“But girls should also be wise to take up challenges when needed. I believe you are such a girl.”可知,老师认为作者是一个敢于接受挑战的女孩。再结合第二段老师让作者朗诵诗歌可以推断,作者感受到了老师的信任。这种信任使 Addie 对老师的态度发生了改变,不再用之前带有调侃意味的名字称呼老师。故选C。

外刊延伸

为什么今天的美国人，不再愿意为了工作搬家？

By Konrad Putzier and Rachel Louise Ensign

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Nobody's Buying Homes, Nobody's Switching Jobs—and America's Mobility is Stalling

    无人买房，无人跳槽——美国的流动性正陷入停滞

    stall
    v. 停滞
  2. 02

    Americans are stuck in place. People are moving to new homes and new cities at around the lowest rate on record.

    如今的美国人正被困在原地。无论是搬家到新住所，还是迁徙到新城市，其比例都接近有记录以来的最低水平。

  3. 03

    Josue Leon, who recently graduated from the University of Pennsylvania with an engineering degree, applied for over 200 jobs since April. In many cases, he didn't even get a reply.

    霍苏维·利昂（Josue Leon）最近刚从宾夕法尼亚大学毕业，获得了工程学学位。自今年4月以来，他已经投递了200多份工作申请，然而很多时候，连一句回复都没收到。

  4. 04

    But when the Fort Worth, Texas, resident finally got a job offer, he turned it down: The job would have required a move to Massachusetts, the company didn't offer relocation assistance and the five-figure salary wouldn't stretch far.

    不过，当这位来自得克萨斯州沃斯堡市（Fort Worth, Texas）的年轻人终于得到一份工作机会时，他却选择了拒绝。原因是这份工作要求他搬到马萨诸塞州（Massachusetts），可公司不提供搬家补贴，而且那份五位数的薪水在当地也实在不够花。

    stretch
    v. 勉强够用
  5. 05

    "Moving to Massachusetts with almost no money is very difficult," Leon said. Eventually he landed a job as a magnet technology engineer in Fort Worth, keeping him close to home.

    “没什么钱还要搬到马萨诸塞州，这太难了，”利昂表示。最终，他在沃斯堡找到了一份磁体技术工程师的工作，留在了离家近的地方。

  6. 06

    For generations, Americans have chased opportunity by moving from city to city, state to state. But that defining mobility has stalled, leaving many people in homes that are too small, in jobs they don't love or in their parents' basements looking for work.

    几代人以来，美国人一直通过跨城跨州迁徙来寻求更好的发展机会。但这种标志性的人口流动性如今却陷入了停滞。这导致许多人被困在狭小的房子里，做着不喜欢的工作，或是窝在父母家的地下室里找工作。

    chase
    v. 追随，追赶
    defining
    adj. 决定性的、标志性的，常用来形容某种特质足以代表某人、某事或某个时代

    句子结构

    But that defining mobility has stalled, / leaving many people in homes that are too small, / in jobs they don't love / or in their parents' basements looking for work.

    leaving … 表示流动性减弱的结果，相当于「结果让许多人……」。三个 in 短语并列，分别写处境。that are too small 修饰 homes；they don’t love 修饰 jobs，省略了作 love 宾语的 that。最后 looking for work 说的是住在父母家里的人。

  7. 07

    This immobility has economic consequences for everyone. The frozen housing market means growing families can't upgrade, empty-nesters can't downsize and first-time buyers are all but locked out. When people can't move for a job offer, or to a city with better job opportunities, they often earn less.

    这种流动性停滞带来的经济后果影响着每一个人。冻结的房地产市场意味着：人口渐增的家庭无法换一个大房子，子女离家的“空巢老人”无法换一个小房子，而首次购房者则几乎完全被拒之门外。当人们无法为了一份工作机会搬家，也无法搬到就业机会更多的城市时，他们的收入往往会更低。

    empty-nesters
    空巢老人
    immobility
    n. 社会流动性的缺乏
    downsize
    v. 减小尺寸；公司裁员
  8. 08

    Today, the job market has slowed notably. Leon never expected his job search to be so hard.

    如今，就业市场的放缓趋势已十分明显。利昂从未想过，自己找工作会这么难。

  9. 09

    Others, like Grace Ahn, are stuck in jobs they feel overqualified for. She applied for about 20 jobs a day. " At first I was so naive, so excited. I was like, the whole world is my oyster," Ahn said. " The oyster has now expired."

    还有些人，比如格蕾丝·安（Grace Ahn），正被困在一份“大材小用”的工作里。她曾每天投递约20份简历。“一开始我特别天真，也特别兴奋，觉得‘整个世界都是我的牡蛎’，”安说，“可现在，这只牡蛎已经坏掉了。”

    overqualified
    adj. 资历过高的，大材小用的
