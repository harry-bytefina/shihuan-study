阅读理解

04-2024全国甲，D

学习、阅读与教育方式

“I didn't like the ending,” I said to my favorite college professor. It was my junior year of undergraduate, and I was doing an independent study on Victorian literature. I had just finished reading The Mill on the Floss by George Eliot, and I was heartbroken with the ending. Prof. Gracie, with all his patience, asked me to think about it beyond whether I liked it or not. He suggested I think about the difference between endings that I wanted for the characters and endings that were right for the characters, endings that satisfied the story even if they didn't have a traditionally positive outcome. Of course, I would have preferred a different ending for Tom and Maggie Tulliver, but the ending they got did make the most sense for them.

This was an ah-ha moment for me, and I never thought about endings the same way again. From then on, if I wanted to read an ending guaranteed to be happy, I'd pick up a love romance. If I wanted an ending I couldn't guess, I'd pick up a mystery(悬疑). One where I kind of knew what was going to happen, historical fiction. Choosing what to read became easier.

But writing the end—that's hard. It's hard for writers because endings carry so much weight with readers. You have to balance creating an ending that's unpredictable, but doesn't seem to come from nowhere, one that fits what's right for the characters.

That's why this issue(期) of Writer's Digest aims to help you figure out how to write the best ending for whatever kind of writing you're doing. If it's short stories, Peter Mountford breaks down six techniques you can try to see which one helps you stick the landing. Elizabeth Sims analyzes the final chapters of five great novels to see what key points they include and how you can adapt them for your work.

This issue won't tell you what your ending should be—that's up to you and the story you're telling—but it might provide what you need to get there.

1Why did the author go to Prof. Gracie?

  • A.To discuss a novel.
  • B.To submit a book report.
  • C.To argue for a writer.
  • D.To ask for a reading list.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1. A　推理判断题。本题问作者去找Gracie教授的原因。根据第一段第三句“I had just finished reading The Mill on the Floss by George Eliot, and I was heartbroken with the ending.”可知,作者刚刚读完一本小说并且不喜欢它的结局。第五句表明Gracie教授建议作者思考喜欢的结局与原文的结局有何不同。再结合第一段最后一句“...but the ending they got did make the most sense for them.”可知,作者同意原文的结局更合理。由此可推断,作者和Gracie教授在讨论一本小说。故选A。

2What did the author realize after seeing Prof. Gracie?

  • A.Writing is a matter of personal preferences.
  • B.Readers are often carried away by characters.
  • C.Each type of literature has its unique ending.
  • D.A story which begins well will end well.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

2. C　细节理解题。根据第二段首句中的“This was an ah-ha moment for me”可知,作者与Gracie教授谈话后突然开窍:想要高兴的结局就读爱情故事(a love romance);想要猜不到的结局就读悬疑小说(a mystery);想要已知的结局就读历史小说(historical fiction)。由此可知,作者意识到每种类型的文学作品都有其独特的结局。故选C。

3What is expected of a good ending?

  • A.It satisfies readers' taste.
  • B.It fits with the story development.
  • C.It is usually positive.
  • D.It is open for imagination.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3. B　细节理解题。根据第三段最后一句“You have to balance creating an ending that's unpredictable, but doesn't seem to come from nowhere, one that fits what􀆳s right for the characters.”可知,符合角色设定的结局才是好的结局,而角色的设定是体现在故事的发展中的。故选B。

4Why does the author mention Peter Mountford and Elizabeth Sims?

  • A.To give examples of great novelists.
  • B.To stress the theme of this issue.
  • C.To encourage writing for the magazine.
  • D.To recommend their new books.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

4. B　推理判断题,考查写作意图推断。根据题干关键词Peter Mountford和Elizabeth Sims可定位到倒数第二段。根据“If it's short stories, Peter Mountford breaks down six techniques...”以及“Elizabeth Sims analyzes the final chapters...”可知,这两个人能帮助你写出好的结局。根据本段首句“That's why this issue of Writer's Digest aims to help you figure out how to write the best ending for whatever kind of writing you're doing.”可知,这期《作家文摘》的主题是帮你弄清楚如何写出最好的结局。分析段落结构可知,首句为本段主旨句。因此作者提到这两个人是为了强调这期《作家文摘》的主题。故选B。

育儿十八年，按下暂停键：如何面对孩子上大学后的“空巢期”？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    The College Drop-Off Is Done and the Nest Is Empty. Now What?

    孩子送去上大学了，家里空了。接下来怎么办？

  2. 02

    The kids are off to college, which means you are essentially on leave from the biggest job you have held for the past 18 years.

    孩子们都去上大学了，这意味着你基本上从过去18年来最庞大的一份“工作”中，解脱出来了。

    essentially
    adv. 本质上，基本上
  3. 03

    They get their own meals, do their own laundry, make and keep appointments. You are still their parents and always will be, but your daily oversight, management and maintenance isn't needed or necessarily wanted.

    他们自己解决三餐，自己洗衣服，会自己安排和遵守各种日程。你依然是他们的父母，这一点永远不会变，但你对他们日常的监督、操心和照料，不再是必须的了，甚至未必是孩子们想要的。

    maintenance
    n. 维持，保持
  4. 04

    Close to 66% of householders aged 45 and older who are parents don't have a child at home, according to data from the National Center for Family and Marriage Research at Bowling Green State University. That translates into about 46.5 million empty nests.

    根据鲍林格林州立大学关于家庭与婚姻的国家研究中心（National Center for Family and Marriage Research）的数据，在45岁及以上的父母中，约66%的家庭里已经没有孩子同住。这意味着美国约有4650万个“空巢家庭”。

  5. 05

    Some parents find newly acquired free time liberating, but many are blindsided by how much their children's departure affects just about everything, from their friendships to spending and dinner conversations. The dog begins to mope.

    有些父母觉得这突如其来的空闲让他们得到了解放，但也有许多人被打了个措手不及——从交友到消费，再到晚餐时的谈话内容，孩子们的离开几乎改变了一切。就连家里的狗，也变得无精打采。

    mope
    v. 闷闷不乐
    departure
    n. 离开，启程
  6. 06

    Parents are generally more involved in their kids' lives than in previous generations. Social media plays a big role, as do concerns about anxiety and depression, which prompt many parents to keep closer tabs.

    当今的父母普遍比前几代更深入地参与到孩子的生活。其中社交媒体发挥了很大作用，另一大因素是对焦虑和抑郁的担忧，这促使许多父母更加密切地关注孩子们的状况。

  7. 07

    Midlife transitions like children leaving home can strain or strengthen marriages. Couples may find they have little to talk about over dinner, now that they aren't coordinating kids' schedules. Or they start planning which new restaurants to try with added discretionary income.

    像孩子离家这样的中年转折，可能给婚姻关系带来压力，也可能让感情更牢固。夫妻俩可能发现，既然没有孩子的行程需要协调，他们在餐桌上便几乎无话可说；或者，他们会发现自己多了一些可支配收入，于是开始计划去哪些新餐厅尝鲜。

    coordinate
    v. 使……协调一致；统筹，安排
    strain
    v. 拉紧，施压
    discretionary
    adj.（钱、时间、权利等）可自由支配的
  8. 08

    Another big challenge is figuring out what to do next. Dick, of CivicScience, a consumer-research company, says he and his wife talk about how to spend their free time. "We don't want to get into the rut of getting home, having dinner and plopping down on the couch to watch TV." They are still mulling the issue.

    对于家长而言，另一项重大挑战是想清楚接下来该做什么。消费者市场调研公司CivicScience的迪克（Dick）说，他和妻子常讨论如何安排空闲时间。“我们可不想陷入那种下班回家、吃完晚饭就瘫在沙发上看电视的老套循环中。”不过他们仍在琢磨这个问题。

    plop
    v. 重重地坐下
