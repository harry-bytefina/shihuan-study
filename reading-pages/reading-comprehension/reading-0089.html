睡眠、运动与身心健康

Kathy Ho teaches high school inside Lucile Packard Children's Hospital Stanford (LPCH). “Sometimes I don't like saying that I'm a teacher,” says Ho. “People get in their minds an idea of what teachers do, but that's not really what it is here.”

“Here” is room 386, where each year, about 500 LPCH patients also become students. The hospital school is free of parents, doctors, and medical procedures. It's a place of learning. About half of Ho's students stay for a week or less; others are there for more than a year. Most of Ho's students will recover, which means that preparing them to return to school is an increasingly important component of care.

Still, in room 386, academics don't come first. Physical health and mental health are the priority. “If you're scared about something and thinking only about that, there's no way you're going to be able to learn,” Ho says. “I'm a coach, an adviser, and a comforter, and that's what it means to be a hospital teacher.”

There are up to 30 students at any given time in Ho's class. She generally works with their regular teachers to get lessons and tests being used at their home schools. Some teachers don't give the kids any assignments; they express sympathy instead. “I feel like it is a disservice to the kids,” Ho says. “They think their teachers don't care about their schoolwork.”

Ho recognizes the psychological benefit of helping kids keep up with their peers(同龄人) outside the hospital. “I actually think the medicine is only a small piece for some problems,” says Julie Good, director of pain management services at LPCH. “It's about problem-solving around what it means to have a full life. Those kids have dreams. School can keep those dreams alive by giving kids a way to learn and grow.”

全部题目与逐题讲解

1Who does Ho teach at LPCH?

  • A.Sick children.
  • B.Young nurses.
  • C.Medical students.
  • D.Patients' parents.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

1. A　细节理解题。根据第一段中的“Kathy Ho teaches high school inside Lucile Packard Children's Hospital Stanford (LPCH).”和第二段中的“‘Here’ is room 386, where each year, about 500 LPCH patients also become students.”可知,Ho的教学对象是生病的孩子。故选A。

2What is a characteristic of Ho's job?

  • A.Prioritizing academics.
  • B.Encouraging innovation.
  • C.Treating various diseases.
  • D.Playing multiple roles.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

2. D　推理判断题。根据第三段中的“I'm a coach, an adviser, and a comforter, and that's what it means to be a hospital teacher.”可知,Ho 称自己是教练、顾问、安慰者,体现其工作需扮演多重角色(Playing multiple roles)。故选D。

3What does the underlined word “it” refer to in paragraph 4?

  • A.Offering regular lessons.
  • B.Paying extra attention.
  • C.Assigning no schoolwork.
  • D.Showing no sympathy.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

3. C　推测词义题。it指代上文出现过的内容。根据画线词后一句“They think their teachers don't care about their schoolwork.”可知,孩子们会以为老师不关心他们的学业。再结合“Some teachers don't give the kids any assignments; they express sympathy instead. ‘I feel like it is a disservice to the kids,’ Ho says.”可知,Ho认为老师不给学生布置作业的做法对他们有损害。 “it”指代上文“Some teachers don't give the kids any assignments”这一行为。故选C。

4How does the hospital school benefit the students according to Good?

  • A.It eases peer pressure.
  • B.It helps them live in hope.
  • C.It frees them from aches.
  • D.It entertains them with stories.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

4. B　推理判断题。根据最后一段中的“It's about problem-solving around what it means to have a full life. Those kids have dreams. School can keep those dreams alive by giving kids a way to learn and grow.”可知,Good认为学校通过让孩子学习成长、维系梦想,帮助其生活在希望中(live in hope)。故选B。

想健身又怕中暑，夏天到底该怎么运动？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Staying active in hot weather can be challenging. It's one reason why players at the World Cup 2026 in the US, Canada and Mexico are being given additional, but controversial hydration breaks 22 minutes into each half.

    高温天坚持运动并非易事。这也是 2026 年美加墨世界杯赛事做出一项颇具争议安排的原因：比赛每半场进行到第 22 分钟时，球员会额外获得补水休息时间。

    hydration
    补充水分；hydration breaks 指比赛中专门安排的补水暂停。
  2. 02

    Pushing through a run or football match in extreme heat can not only be uncomfortable but also dangerous, putting us at risk of heatstroke.

    在酷热环境下硬撑着跑步、踢足球，不仅体感难受，还存在健康隐患，可能引发中暑。

    heatstroke
    热射病，严重的中暑；at risk of heatstroke 说明高温运动可能带来的危险。
  3. 03

    "Walking, cycling, exercising outdoors and even daily routines like commuting on foot become more physically demanding and less comfortable when temperatures are high," says Christian García-Witulski, an environmental lifestyle epidemiologist.

    环境生活流行病学家克里斯蒂安・加西亚 - 维图尔斯基（Christian García-Witulski）表示：“气温升高后，步行、骑行、户外锻炼，甚至走路通勤这类日常活动，都会让人身体负担加重，体感也更难熬。”

    commuting
    通勤；commuting on foot 特指步行上下班等日常往返。
  4. 04

    The single most impactful change most people can make is to exercise when it is cooler, such as in the morning or evening, if this is possible.

    普通人最有效的应对办法，就是尽量选择凉爽时段运动，比如清晨或傍晚。

  5. 05

    You could also pick times when the area where you like to exercise is in the shade as opposed to the full glare of the Sun.

    你也可以挑选锻炼场地处于阴凉遮蔽处的时段，避开烈日直晒。

    glare
    刺眼的强光；the full glare of the Sun 和 in the shade 对比，指阳光直晒。
  6. 06

    Humidity matters too. Your body's primary way of losing heat is through sweat evaporating from the skin, which helps to lower your body temperature. But in humid conditions, that process is affected as the evaporation that cools your body reduces.

    湿度同样关键。人体主要依靠皮肤汗液蒸发散热，以此降低体温。但高湿度环境会阻碍汗液蒸发，身体降温效率大幅下降。

    humidity
    空气湿度；原文说潮湿时汗液蒸发减少，散热变慢。
    evaporating
    蒸发；sweat evaporating from the skin 指汗液从皮肤表面变成水蒸气。
  7. 07

    Wind speed also plays a role, which is why running in enclosed spaces with little airflow makes heat stress a bigger risk.

    风速也会影响体感，这也是在密闭、通风差的区域跑步，更容易出现高温不适的原因。

    enclosed
    封闭或被围起来的；enclosed spaces 在这里指空气流通受限的运动场地。
  8. 08

    "Sometimes a shorter walk in the morning, or some light indoor movement, is safer and more realistic than trying to stick rigidly to the same routine," says García-Witulski.

    加西亚 - 维图尔斯基提到：“与其死板坚持原有运动计划，不如早上短时间散步，或是做一些轻度室内运动，这样更安全、也更贴合实际。”

    rigidly
    不知变通地；stick rigidly to a routine 指天气变了仍坚持照原计划运动。
