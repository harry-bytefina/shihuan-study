阅读理解

03-2013天津，C

动物行为与动物认知

Poet William Stafford once said that we are defined more by the detours(绕行路)in life than by the narrow road toward goals.I like this image.But it was quite by accident that I discovered the deep meaning of his words.

For years we made the long drive from our home in Seattle to my parents' home in Boise in nine hours.We traveled the way most people do:the fastest,shortest,easiest road,especially when I was alone with four noisy,restless kids who hate confinement(限制)and have strong opinions about everything.

Road trips felt risky,so I would drive fast,stopping only when I had to.We would stick to the freeways and arrive tired.

But then Banner,our lamb was born.He was rejected by his mama days before our planned trip to Boise.I had two choices:leave Banner with my husband,or take him with me.My husband made the decision for me.

That is how I found myself on the road with four kids,a baby lamb and nothing but my everlasting optimism to see me through.We took the country roads out of necessity.We had to stop every hour,let Banner shake out his legs and feed him.The kids chased him and one another.They'd get back in the car breathless and energized,smelling fresh from the cold air.

We explored side roads,catching grasshoppers in waist-high grass.Even if we simply looked out of the car windows at baby pigs following their mother,or fish leaping out of the water,it was better than the best ride down the freeway.Here was life.And new horizons(见识).

We eventually arrived at my parents' doorstep astonishingly fresh and full of stories.

I grew brave with the trip back home and creative with my disciplining technique.On an empty section of road,everyone started quarreling.I stopped the car,ordered all kids out and told them to meet me up ahead.I parked my car half a mile away and read my book in sweet silence.

Some road trips are by necessity fast and straight.But that trip with Banner opened our eyes to a world available to anyone adventurous enough to wander around and made me realize that a detour may uncover the best part of a journey—and the best part of yourself.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1Why did the author use to take freeways to her parents' home?

  • A.It was less tiring.
  • B.It would be faster and safer.
  • C.Her kids would feel less confined.
  • D.She felt better with other drivers nearby.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1.B　细节理解题。第三段最后一句提到作者坚持走高速公路,到达的时候会比较累。A项与此相反,故排除。C和D项文中没有涉及。

2The author stopped regularly on the country roads to _____ .

  • A.relax in the fresh air
  • B.take a deep breath
  • C.take care of the lamb
  • D.let the kids play with Banner
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

2.C　细节理解题。从第五段中的第二、三句得知:作者出于需要要走乡间小路。他们不得不每小时停下让Banner伸伸腿并且喂它。故选C项(停车去照顾小羊羔)。

3What does the author discover from the trip according to Paragraph 6?

  • A.Freeways are where beauty hides.
  • B.Getting close to nature adds to the joy of life.
  • C.Enjoying the beauty of nature benefits one's health.
  • D.One should follow side roads to watch wild animals.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3.B　推理判断题。作者通过看到的自然景象得出感慨:这样的旅行要比高速公路上最好的旅行还要好,因为这才是生活,而且增长了见识。故选B项(接近自然可以增加生活的乐趣)。

4Why did the author ask the kids to get out of the car on their way back home?

  • A.To give herself some time to read.
  • B.To order some food for them.
  • C.To play a game with them.
  • D.To let them cool down.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

4.D　细节理解题。第八段中提到,在回去的路上,孩子们在车上争吵,所以作者就命令他们下车到前面会合,也就是要孩子们冷静下来不要争吵。故选D项。

5What could be the best title for the passage?

  • A.Charm of the Detour
  • B.The Road to Bravery
  • C.Creativity out of Necessity
  • D.Road Trip and Country Life
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

5.A　主旨要义题。作者以诗人William Stafford对绕行道的理解引题,加上自己的亲身经历,进一步理解了绕行道更深刻的意义——它会让你发现旅行中最好的部分,也发现最好的自己。故选A项——绕行道的魅力。

阅读难度参考：CSE 7

旅行的尽头，为什么变成了逛当地超市？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Why travellers are obsessed with foreign supermarkets

    为什么游客痴迷于逛国外的超市

  2. 02

    It's 10:00 in a Tokyo 7-Eleven, and I am standing shoulder to shoulder with a dozen tourists, all clutching baskets loaded with the same things: fluffy egg mayo sandwiches, katsu curry buns, matcha Kit Kats and warm cans of Boss coffee. Behind me, someone photographs their haul before they've even reached the till. Nobody is here because they have run out of toothpaste. We are all, quite openly, shopping for the experience.

    东京一家 7‑11 便利店，此刻是上午十点。我和十几名游客摩肩接踵，大家手里攥着购物篮，装的都是同款商品：松软的蛋黄酱鸡蛋三明治、炸猪排咖喱包、抹茶味奇巧巧克力，还有热罐装 Boss 咖啡。我身后，有人还没走到收银台，就先拍下自己的战利品。没人是因为牙膏用完了才来这里。我们所有人，无需掩饰，都是为这份体验而来消费。

    haul
    一大批到手的东西；photograph their haul 指给刚选中的一堆商品拍照。
    clutching
    紧紧抓着；clutching baskets 写游客把装得满满的购物篮攥在手里。
    till
    收银台；英式英语里 reach the till 指走到结账处。
  3. 03

    Away from traditional sightseeing, ducking into a local grocery shop remains a surprisingly raw way to travel. Nothing on the shelves has been curated for out-of-towners or stamped with a tourist board's seal of approval. In an era of timed-entry museums and viral photo spots, wandering the aisles of a neighbourhood grocery store feels wonderfully unscripted.

    抛开传统观光，逛一逛本地杂货店，是一种格外真切的旅行方式。货架上没有任何商品是专门为外来游客挑选的，也没有盖上旅游部门的认证印章。在这个博物馆需要预约限时入场、网红打卡点遍地的时代，在社区杂货店的过道间闲逛，有种难得的随性自在。

    unscripted
    没有预先编排的；作者觉得逛普通超市不像参加事先安排好的景点活动。
    curated
    经过挑选、安排的；这里说货架没有特意按外地游客的喜好摆货。
  4. 04

    Monuments reveal how a country remembers its past, but a supermarket shows how it lives today. The person queuing beside you isn't there to sightsee. They're simply buying dinner. For a few minutes, tourists and locals occupy the same space, following exactly the same routines.

    历史古迹展现一个国家如何铭记过往，而超市则展现它当下的生活模样。排在你身旁的人不是来观光的，只是来买晚餐。短短几分钟里，游客与本地人身处同一空间，遵循着完全一样的日常。

  5. 05

    And there's a scientific explanation for why those convenience store snacks seem to taste so good. Oxford experimental psychologist Professor Charles Spence points to a psychological phenomenon whereby a wine tasted on one's summer holidays never quite matches up when opened back home on a cold and rainy winter's night. In the supermarket aisle, context is everything.

    便利店零食之所以吃起来格外美味，是有科学依据的。牛津大学实验心理学家查尔斯・斯彭斯教授提到一种心理现象：夏日度假时喝过的葡萄酒，回到家在阴冷多雨的冬夜再开瓶品尝，味道总是差一截。在超市的过道里，环境氛围决定一切。

    whereby
    引出某种现象怎样表现；文中接上例子，说同一款酒换了环境就喝不出原来的感觉。
  6. 06

    Consumer psychologist Kate Nightingale believes the supermarket trend reflects a broader shift in what travellers are looking for. Increasingly, she says, we're seeking moments that feel authentic, participatory and accessible. Rather than simply observing a destination, we want to feel part of it.

    消费心理学家凯特・南丁格尔认为，逛超市这股潮流，反映出游客的追求发生了更广泛的转变。她表示，我们越来越多地去追寻真实、可参与、触手可及的体验时刻。我们不只想单纯观赏一处目的地，更希望真切融入其中。

    participatory
    能让人亲自参与的；指游客做当地人也在做的事，获得参与感。
    authentic
    真实的；在这里指能感受到当地人的日常生活，而非专为游客安排的活动。
