生态、气候与城市自然

When a leafy plant is under attack, it doesn't sit quietly. Back in 1983, two scientists, Jack Schultz and Ian Baldwin, reported that young maple trees getting bitten by insects send out a particular smell that neighboring plants can get. These chemicals come from the injured parts of the plant and seem to be an alarm. What the plants pump through the air is a mixture of chemicals known as volatile organic compounds, VOCs for short.

Scientists have found that all kinds of plants give out VOCs when being attacked. It's a plant's way of crying out. But is anyone listening?Apparently. Because we can watch the neighbors react.

Some plants pump out smelly chemicals to keep insects away. But others do double duty. They pump out perfumes designed to attract different insects who are natural enemies to the attackers. Once they arrive, the tables are turned. The attacker who was lunching now becomes lunch.

In study after study, it appears that these chemical conversations help the neighbors. The damage is usually more serious on the first plant, but the neighbors, relatively speaking, stay safer because they heard the alarm and knew what to do.

Does this mean that plants talk to each other?Scientists don't know. Maybe the first plant just made a cry of pain or was sending a message to its own branches, and so, in effect, was talking to itself. Perhaps the neighbors just happened to“overhear”the cry. So information was exchanged, but it wasn't a true, intentional back and forth.

Charles Darwin, over 150 years ago, imagined a world far busier, noisier and more intimate(亲密的)than the world we can see and hear. Our senses are weak. There's a whole lot going on.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What does a plant do when it is under attack?

  • A.It makes noises.
  • B.It gets help from other plants.
  • C.It stands quietly.
  • D.It sends out certain chemicals.
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

1.D　细节理解题。根据第一段枫树的例子可知,当其受到攻击时,受到伤害的部位会释放出被称为VOCs的化学物质,由第二段第一句可知所有的植物受到攻击时都会做出这种反应,故D项正确。植物被攻击时释放出的是化学物质而不是噪音,故排除A;释放出的化学物质是对其他植物的警示,而不是从其他植物那里得到帮助,故排除B;根据第一段首句可排除C项。

2What does the author mean by“the tables are turned”in paragraph 3?

  • A.The attackers get attacked.
  • B.The insects gather under the table.
  • C.The plants get ready to fight back.
  • D.The perfumes attract natural enemies.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

2.A　推理判断题。根据第三段可知,某些植物受到攻击时,会释放出某种香味,将攻击者的天敌吸引过来,由此可知当它们到来时,情况发生了反转,原先享用午餐(侵害树木)的攻击者将会成为其天敌的午餐,即成为被攻击者,故A项正确。

3Scientists find from their studies that plants can _____ .

  • A.predict natural disasters
  • B.protect themselves against insects
  • C.talk to one another intentionally
  • D.help their neighbors when necessary
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

3.B　推理判断题。根据文章前三段所描述的树木在遭到昆虫袭击后的反应,加之第四段所介绍的树木的损伤情况和周边树木接到警报后的反应,可以概括出植物能够保护自身不受昆虫侵害这一点。故正确答案为B。

4What can we infer from the last paragraph?

  • A.The world is changing faster than ever.
  • B.People have stronger senses than before.
  • C.The world is more complex than it seems.
  • D.People in Darwin's time were more imaginative.
查看答案与讲解

答案：C

4.C　推理判断题。最后一段提到,150多年前,达尔文就曾想到生物世界比我们所看到、听到的更加繁忙,更加吵闹,也更加亲密,我们的感官不够强大,很多东西未被感知到。由此可推断出,这个世界比其表面看上去更加复杂,故C项正确。

英国人一年说3000次“sorry”，到底在想什么？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    What British people really mean when they say 'sorry'

    英国人说“sorry”时，到底是什么意思？

  2. 02

    Sorry. Sorry to bother you. Sorry for the delay. Sorry about the weather. Sorry for all of the above.

    对不起。不好意思，打扰了。抱歉耽搁了。天气真不好。为以上所有事道歉。

  3. 03

    In the UK, sorry is not simply an apology, it's a cultural reflex – a five-letter pressure valve used to soften requests, smooth over awkwardness, fill conversational gaps and avoid the national horror of seeming rude. It is perhaps no coincidence that such famously polite characters as Paddington and Mary Poppins are British.

    在英国，“sorry”不仅仅意味着道歉，更是一种文化本能反应——它就像一个由五个字母组成的“减压阀”，用来软化请求、化解尴尬、填补交谈中的冷场，以及避开全英国人都害怕落入的境地：显得无礼。像帕丁顿熊（Paddington）和玛丽·波平斯（Mary Poppins）这样以彬彬有礼著称的角色都源自英国，也许并非巧合。

    valve
    阀门；pressure valve 在此是比喻，指用一句 sorry 缓解交谈中的紧张。
    awkwardness
    交往中的尴尬、不自在；smooth over awkwardness 是缓和尴尬局面。
    reflex
    下意识的反应；cultural reflex 指说 sorry 已成一种习惯性社交反应。
  4. 04

    Brits say the word on average nine times per day – more than 3,000 times a year. But for visitors, the puzzle is not how often they hear it, it is working out what sorry actually means. Because in Britain, sorry can mean regret. It can also mean excuse me, move over, I disagree, hurry up, you're blocking the aisle, I didn't hear you or I am trying very hard to not sound annoyed.

    英国人平均一天会说九次“sorry”——一年下来超过3000次。但对于游客而言，真正的困惑并不在于听到这个词的频率有多高，而是弄明白它究竟是什么意思。因为在英国，“sorry”既可以表示歉意，也可以用来表示“打扰了”“麻烦让让”“恕我不敢苟同”“请快一点”“你挡住道了”“我没听清”或“我正在非常努力地不让自己听起来不耐烦”。

    aisle
    过道；you're blocking the aisle 指对方挡了通往座位或出口的过道。
  5. 05

    While these uses are not unique to the UK, the frequency, tone and the tiny social calculations often are. Britain is often known as a conflict-avoidant society, and sorry has become one of its most versatile tools.

    虽然这些用法并非英国独有，但其使用频率、语气，以及背后那些微妙的社交考量，往往带有鲜明的英式特色。英国常被视为一个回避冲突型社会，而“sorry”已然成为这个社会中最万能的社交工具之一。

    versatile
    有多种用途的；用来形容 sorry 除道歉外还可以用于让路、催促等场合。
    conflict-avoidant
    倾向避开直接冲突的；文中概括人们为何用比较委婉的话调节交往。
  6. 06

    For visitors, learning to decode it can be the difference between a friendly exchange and a baffling British misunderstanding.

    对游客来说，学习理解这个词的真正含义，往往决定了你与当地人的互动是一次友好的交流，还是一场令人费解的英式误会。

    baffling
    让人摸不着头脑的；形容访客按字面理解 sorry 时可能遇到的困惑。
    decode
    解读表面之下的意思；指听懂一句 sorry 具体在传递什么。
