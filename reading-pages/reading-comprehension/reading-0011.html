阅读理解

03-2024天津夏，D

学习、阅读与教育方式

Staring at the bookcases in my study, packed with so many great books that had remained unread, I heard a loud voice in my head—“Shame on you! How can you leave these masterpieces unread?”

The first book I picked up was Montaigne's Essays. To my surprise I discovered in the margins (页边空白) what clearly was my own faded handwriting. So I was actually reading it again, but what I was rereading seemed entirely new. I also found sentences underlined. Only this time I wondered: Why did I underline this sentence? It's the next one that is important!

Clearly, my way of reading the text had shifted, and I myself had changed over the years. This raised the larger question of rereading. It comes in many forms. There's voluntary rereading, the result of a willful decision to revisit a book one has admired, or a book that has left one with some unanswered questions. This kind of planned revisit could also be for confirming certain details in the text, or for checking on the moves of a given character. A devoted teacher might also wish to refresh his closeness to a work, and thus avoid teaching through the same old written notes with soporific (让人瞌睡的) effects.

Contrarily, there's involuntary rereading. The original reading was either forgotten or so totally absorbed that the new chance encounter with the text produced surprise and astonishment. My reaction to the renewed reading no longer corresponded to the original experience, and I was no longer sure that I recognized myself as the same reader.

Then there are what one might call subconscious (潜意识的) rereadings, those that occur without the specific act of reading, much as the memory of a tune can keep coming back to the mind without its actually being heard again. This form of remembered contact with a book can accompany us during a lifetime and continue to strengthen and shape us. Much in the same manner, we may over the years recite to ourselves poems learnt by heart long ago, which have become part of our self-recognition.

All of these ways of reading are valuable. Renewed contact with a novel or a poem can activate the search for a better knowledge of the self. The new reading, a form of revision, uncovers the change in us. The newness is not in the text. It is we who have evolved. In the process of rereading, our outlook has also been significantly changed. Rereading makes it possible for us to see the world around us, ourselves included, in a new light.

全部题目与逐题讲解

1What did the author find when reading Montaigne's Essays?

  • A.He still couldn't understand it.
  • B.He had made notes in it.
  • C.It was a brand new copy.
  • D.It was his favorite work.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

1. B　细节理解题。根据题干关键词Montaigne's Essays定位到第二段第一句。根据第二段中的“To my surprise I discovered in the margins what clearly was my own faded handwriting.”可知,作者惊讶地发现书的空白处依稀可见自己之前的笔记。故选B。

2According to the author, what could be a purpose of voluntary rereading?

  • A.To improve the quality of teaching.
  • B.To learn from admirable characters.
  • C.To collect details for writing novels.
  • D.To help making important decisions.
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

2. A　细节理解题。根据题干关键词voluntary rereading定位到第三段第四句。根据第三段最后一句“A devoted teacher might also wish to refresh his closeness to a work, and thus avoid teaching through the same old written notes with soporific effects.(一位忠实的老师也可能会希望更新他对一部作品的亲密感,从而避免通过具有催眠效果的一成不变的书面笔记来教学)”可知,教师通过主动再读更新内容,从而提高教学质量。故选A。

3According to Paragraph 4, involuntary rereading can bring about _____ .

  • A.unexpected reading experience
  • B.total concentration on new books
  • C.changed preferences for books
  • D.enhanced reading skills
查看答案与讲解

答案：A

3. A　细节理解题。根据第四段中的“the new chance encounter with the text produced surprise and astonishment(与文本的新一次的不期而遇带来了惊讶)”以及“My reaction to the renewed reading no longer corresponded to the original experience(我对重新阅读的反应不再与最初的经历一致)”可知,非主动再读能为读者带来意料之外的阅读体验。故选A。

4What can be learnt about subconscious rereading?

  • A.It prevents memory from fading.
  • B.It helps make who we are.
  • C.It involves actual reading.
  • D.It works best with poetry and music.
查看答案与讲解

答案：B

4. B　细节理解题。根据题干关键词subconscious rereading定位到第五段首句。由第五段中的“This form of remembered contact with a book...continue to strengthen and shape us.”以及“we may over the years recite to ourselves poems...which have become part of our self-recognition”可知,潜意识再读有助于塑造自我。故选B。

5What could be the best title for the passage?

  • A.Accessing Masterpieces through Rereading
  • B.Rereading: Voluntary or Involuntary?
  • C.Rereading: Pursuit of Truth
  • D.Transformative Power of Rereading
查看答案与讲解

答案：D

5. D　主旨要义题。文章从作者面对未读书籍的羞愧感出发,引出了重读的概念。接着,文章详细描述了不同类型的重读(主动再读、非主动再读和潜意识再读)以及它们带来的影响。最后,文章强调了重读的价值,指出它能帮助我们更好地认识自己和世界。D项(重读的变革力量)强调了重读对个人成长和认知世界的深远影响,是最佳标题。

为什么我们看不到自己的双标？

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Most of us sometimes fail to practice what we preach. Yet while we're quick to spot this moral inconsistency in others, we have a hard time recognizing it in ourselves. "We're motivated to see other people as hypocritical because it helps us win arguments," says Ike Silver, an assistant professor at the University of Southern California. But we tend to justify or explain away our own contradictions.

    我们大多数人都有过“言行不一”的时候。可虽然我们能轻易发现别人道德上的矛盾之处，却很难察觉到自己身上同样的问题。南加州大学的助理教授艾克·西尔弗（Ike Silver）表示：“我们倾向于认为别人是伪君子，因为这样可以帮助我们在争论中占上风。”但面对自身的矛盾时，我们往往会为之辩解或找借口。

    hypocritical
    adj. 虚伪的
    contradiction
    n. 矛盾
    inconsistency
    n. 不一致，矛盾
  2. 02

    Nobody likes being a hypocrite. Yet, in reality, it's impossible to always act consistently, because different situations may require different responses. You may reasonably lecture your teenager on the dangers of drinking too much, yet you did exactly that on occasion when you were in college.

    没人想当虚伪的人。但在现实生活中，我们就是不可能始终如一地行事，因为不同情境往往需要不同的应对方式。你可能会郑重告诫自己的青少年孩子不要酗酒，但你自己在大学时也曾喝得烂醉。

    hypocrite
    n. 虚伪的人
    reasonably
    adv. 合情合理地

    句子结构

    You may reasonably lecture your teenager on the dangers of drinking too much, / yet you did exactly that on occasion / when you were in college.

    yet 并列两个完整分句，形成「现在告诫孩子」与「自己当年也做过」的反差。did exactly that 代指 drinking too much，而非 lecture your teenager；when 从句给的是自己过去做这件事的时间。

  3. 03

    How can we do better? We can start by accepting that it is normal to be inconsistent sometimes. Daniel Effron, a professor at London Business School, cautions against labeling other people as hypocrites. "It can prevent you from engaging in the substance of their argument," he says.

    我们怎么才能做得更好呢？可以先从接受这件事开始：有时，言行不一其实很正常。伦敦商学院教授丹尼尔·埃夫隆（Daniel Effron）提醒我们，不要轻易给别人贴上“伪君子”的标签，“那样会让你忽视对方观点的实质内容。”

  4. 04

    It takes some self-reflection to identify our own hypocrisy. If you do decide that you're being hypocritical, admit it. Research shows that when people are honest about their own hypocrisy, others tend to forgive them for not being consistent. And here's some good news: People who notice their own hypocritical behavior often are motivated to change. This is because the cognitive dissonance of advocating for something but not actually doing it makes people very uncomfortable.

    识别自己的虚伪行为需要自我反思。如果你确实觉得自己虚伪，那就坦然承认。研究表明，当人们坦承自己的虚伪时，别人往往更容易原谅他们的言行不一。而且好消息是：那些能够察觉到自己虚伪行为的人，往往更有动力去改变。因为“言行不符”的心理冲突会让人极度不适。

    hypocrisy
    n. 虚伪
    cognitive dissonance
    认知失调
    advocate for
    支持，倡导，呼吁
  5. 05

    Rather than feeling ashamed about our hypocrisy, we should embrace the opportunity to learn to do better next time.

    与其为自己的虚伪感到羞耻，不如把它当作一次学习如何变得更好的机会，好好把握住。
