Passage 2 主题：自然保护

Passage 2 主题：自然保护 · 动物行为与动物认知

My love for animals began through watching wildlife documentaries when I was a kid. I went on to study Environmental Science at university, and became interested in marine (海洋的) biology when I did my master's degree. That's when I realised that there was a possibility to do more for Hong Kong.

In Hong Kong, many people would associate marine life only with seafood, and few people would consider it as a part of nature to be appreciated. Although our marine life is highly diverse, our waters have been overfished for more than 40 years. Records show that, decades ago, Hong Kong had such high production of seafood that it supported about 90% of the local demand. Today, at least 90% of the seafood we eat has to be imported to meet local demand, as our waters are so overfished that they can no longer support the local appetite.

Despite this shift, we are still a city in love with seafood—according to the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, Hong Kong has the second highest annual seafood consumption per capita(人均) in Asia—but consumers are barely aware of where their seafood comes from and which species are threatened.

There is a lot of room for improvement in Hong Kong. Improving local fishery management policies and increasing the coverage of Marine Protected Areas, as well as conducting the relevant research to inform decisions, could move the city towards greater sustainability in this area. And consumers should avoid eating seafood that is on the threatened species list. As a general rule of thumb, if people are not sure what the species is, they should choose something else that they are more familiar with.

With the marine ecosystem already weak and easily destroyed, the greatest threat is continued ignorance and inaction. It is time for us to show that we can also be part of the solution. If each person plays their part—even for just one meal a day—Hong Kong would already be on its way to making considerable progress towards sustainable seafood consumption.

“猫斯坦布尔”：人与猫共享一座城

词汇梳理
  1. 01

    Catstanbul: Inside the city where cats rule the streets

    猫斯坦布尔：走进那座猫咪占领街头的城市

  2. 02

    More than fifteen million people compete for space in Turkey's largest city, which straddles Europe and Asia on either side of the Bosphorus, much like a house cat perched half on the sofa, half on the coffee table. That's the appropriate visual, because there are an estimated quarter of a million stray kitties living here, too.

    在土耳其最大的城市里，有超过1500万人争夺有限的空间——伊斯坦布尔（Istanbul）这座城市横跨欧亚大陆，分布在博斯普鲁斯海峡（Bosphorus）两岸，如同一只家猫，一半身子蹲在沙发上，一半搭在咖啡桌上。这个比喻再贴切不过，因为伊斯坦布尔还生活着大约25万只流浪猫。

    stray
    n. When we're talking about animals, a stray animal is an animal that does not have a home or owner, and usually lives on the streets.
    straddle
    If we say a place straddles something else.
  3. 03

    "Istanbul cats are, on the whole, neither pets nor strays, but a hybrid of those terms," says Marcel Heijnen, photographer and author of City Cats of Istanbul, adding that the cats are not owned by specific people "but taken care of by the community".

    摄影师、《伊斯坦布尔的城市猫咪》（City Cats of Istanbul）的作者马塞尔·海宁（Marcel Heijnen）说：“从整体来看，在伊斯坦布尔，猫咪既不是宠物，也不完全是无家可归，而是介于两者之间的一种存在。”他补充道，这些猫并不属于某个具体的人，“而是由整个社区共同照料。”

    a hybrid of
    A hybrid is something made by combining, mixing or joining two different things.
  4. 04

    "Each municipality has a veterinary department that helps street animals in their district" explains Fatih Dağlı, co-founder of Cat Museum Istanbul. "Private animal clinics also offer discounted services for street cats, and residents often chip in to pay the vet bills."

    “每个市政区都设有兽医部门，为辖区内的街头动物提供帮助，”法提赫·达利（Fatih Dağlı）解释说，他是伊斯坦布尔猫咪博物馆（Cat Museum Istanbul）的联合创始人。“私人动物诊所也会为流浪猫提供折扣服务，居民们常常自发凑钱支付兽医费用。”

    chip in
    To chip in is a phrasal verb, an idiom, it means to give something, usually money or help.
  5. 05

    This devotion to cats is hardly new. "The adoration of stray cats dates back to when Istanbul was under Ottoman rule," says Heijnen. "During this period, local foundations made sure that stray animals were taken care of. That love for strays turned into a full-time profession when a job called mancacı ("cat sitter") was created. "Mancacıs were tasked with the job of making sure the city's cats were fed."

    这种对猫的热爱并非近年才有。“对流浪猫的喜爱可以追溯到伊斯坦布尔仍处于奥斯曼帝国（Ottoman Empire）统治时期，”海宁说。“那时，当地的慈善基金会会确保流浪动物得到照料。这种关爱后来甚至演变成一份全职职业——mancacı，也就是“猫咪看护人”，“他们的职责就是确保城市里的猫咪得到喂养。”

    adoration of
    Adoration means very strong love or admiration for something you admire, respect, love something.
    hardly new
    If something is hardly new, it means it is not really new.
  6. 06

    Today, the human residents of Istanbul still happily share their spaces with their city of cats. So much so that the nickname,"Catstanbul" is widely used by feline fanciers around the world – and many tourists make the trek here because of the cats.

    时至今日，伊斯坦布尔的人类居民依然乐意与城市里的猫咪共享空间。正因如此，“猫斯坦布尔”（Catstanbul）这一昵称被全球爱猫人士广泛使用——许多游客也正是为了这些猫，专程来到这里。

    feline fanciers
    There are two important words here.
    make the trek
    To make the trek means to travel a long distance, often with lots of effort, especially when the journey is not easy.
