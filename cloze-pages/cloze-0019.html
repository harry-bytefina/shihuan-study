积极的人生态度

完整文章

Marathons are often framed as a test of physical endurance. But for some, they begin as a playful claim—a silly family argument about who could run the marathon the fastest. It was debate over who could outpace the 42 kilometers at first, but within minutes, we were online, signing up for a full marathon—no real training plans, no second thoughts, just four stubborn people determined to prove each wrong.

We had only three and a half weeks to train, and my longest run was 20 kilometers. Though, that distance was nowhere near the full marathon length, I still felt like myself, and I knew it was going to be a really tough task.

One day, after one of my sister's long days in the clinic, she sank onto the couch and said, “I just went through a marathon of my own today.” That with me ever since. We often use the word “marathon” to describe the non-stop, exhausting days healthcare workers to bear. I realized the coming race could mean something bigger.

In the months leading up to the race, my mother was with a brain tumor(脑瘤), then underwent surgery and began her recovery. I witnessed first-hand the extraordinary care she received. I realized who the real marathoners were—the healthcare workers working impossible shifts and everything together for patients like my mom. Just then I found my “why”, wanting to run to represent the people who for her.

I started running in my scrubs(外科手术服)—as a dental student I wear them regularly. I filmed everything—the good runs, the bad runs and the moments when I wanted to . But what I didn't was how many people would actually find the journey meaningful. By race day, strangers from social media were shouting words of encouragement. Some even told me they felt inspired to run too.

Crossing that finish line wasn't just about my sisters. It was about becoming part of a large of healthcare workers, runners and everyday people who show up, push through and keep going no matter what happens.

Running the race was my way to those healthcare workers running silent marathons daily. Their is what I always aimed to be to. I'll carry this lesson as I my career in dentistry, remembering to keep going like those healthcare heroes.
